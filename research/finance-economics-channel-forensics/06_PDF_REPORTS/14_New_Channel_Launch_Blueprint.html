New Channel Launch Blueprint (2026-11-14)

Labels: VERIFIED / INFERRED / HYPOTHESIS / UNKNOWN. Everything here that predicts performance is a HYPOTHESIS with a test and a kill rule. Nothing was validated on a live channel. Source: nine-channel study (PDFs 01 to 11), opening and thumbnail tests, topic pilot (PDF 12). Studio CTR, retention and traffic-source data are UNKNOWN.

1. Niche choice

  • Niche: finance and economics explained through real, dated events tied to the viewer's money (debt, housing, retirement, jobs, prices).
  • Why (INFERRED, MEDIUM-LOW): in the nine channels, strong videos were mostly a real named event with a viewer-money link; weak ones were abstract questions, institutions or full company histories. Selection bias is possible (videos were picked by views).
  • Warning (VERIFIED in the pilot): topics with measurable demand are already covered; see PDF 13. The niche is crowded; the difference must come from execution and angle, which is untested.

2. Audience and promise (HYPOTHESIS)

  • Audience: English-speaking adults who want to understand a money event without jargon.
  • Promise: "A real, dated event that touches your money, in one story, in 11 to 14 minutes."

3. Content pillars (HYPOTHESIS)

  1. Money events (a default, a ruling, a release) and what it changes for the viewer.
  2. How a named company or deal actually went wrong or right (filings as the source).
  3. Everyday-money mechanics through one concrete case (not an abstract concept).

Not in the first 10: institutions ("who owns the Fed"), abstract concepts ("where does money come from"), full company histories. They were weak in 5 of 9 channels (exceptions exist).

4. Formats (HYPOTHESIS)

  • One visual character system of our own (no copying of another channel's character), 11 to 14 minutes, at least one real source chart or document on screen per video (pattern seen in Casual Finance strong videos).
  • Length: 6 channels' strong videos fall in 10 to 20 minutes but length is confounded; 8 minutes is the mid-roll rule, not a quality rule (Playbook Part D).
  • No Shorts and no sponsors in the first 10 videos (neither explained results in the nine channels).

5. Opening, title, thumbnail rules (HYPOTHESIS, association only)

  • Opening, first 15 seconds: real name or date, one large number, the viewer's stake. No joke, skit, invented character, "Meet X", or question-first opening. Support: 181-video test, stake-first openings beat the channel median in 4 of 7 channels (p up to 0.06), no effect in Economics Explained; labels are rule-based, not blind; topic confound remains.
  • Thumbnail: a real name or event plus one idea. A big number alone is not a rule (one concept got 1.15M and 13.6k with different framing). Thumbnail blind test was leaked, so it is NOT evidence.
  • Playbook rubric (see scorecard in PDF 15): the nine channels most often fail P13 (stakes in 5 to 15 s), P14 (open loop before 30 s) and P17 (direct address); these are the cheapest rules to follow better than the channels studied.

6. Topic screen (the workflow that decides every video)

For each candidate, in this order (Trends first, as learned in the pilot):

  1. Google Trends, YouTube Search, United States: 7 days, 30 days, 90 days, 12 months, 5 years, all time. "Not enough data" or a one-day spike = no durable demand.
  2. YouTube autocomplete and 5 YouTube searches (Chrome or yt-dlp); read the 3 closest videos (title, views, age, vidIQ VPH and tags).
  3. Read the transcript of the closest video. If the same question and payoff is already public, REJECT.
  4. Check the primary source (filing, release, court docket) and its reuse terms.
  5. Decide PASS, REJECT or NEEDS MORE RESEARCH and write the decision with sources.

Cost seen in the pilot: 16 candidates went through the screen, 0 passed; budget a day of screening per two videos.

7. Production workflow (HYPOTHESIS; creator/team workflow of the studied channels is UNKNOWN)

Research note with sources, then script with the opening rule, then character/visual pass with source charts, then voice, then edit, then thumbnail and title with a logged change time, then publish; keep a one-line log per video (topic, screen result, title, thumbnail, publish time, any retitle with timestamp).

8. Scoring matrix (per candidate, 0 to 2 each; HYPOTHESIS)

Durable demand (Trends) / not already answered (strict rule) / viewer-money link / primary source available and reusable / fits character system / can be told in 11 to 14 minutes. A candidate needs 2 on the second criterion (strict rule) to proceed; the others only rank.

9. 30-day plan (HYPOTHESIS)

  • Days 1 to 5: set up channel, character system, source-chart template; screen 6 candidates with section 6.
  • Days 6 to 30: publish 4 to 6 videos only from candidates that passed; after each, log views at 24 h, 7 d, 14 d (and Studio CTR, 30 s retention once the owner supplies them).
  • Retitle or re-thumbnail only with a logged timestamp, otherwise the effect cannot be separated.

10. Test and kill rule

  • After 10 videos: if none reaches 3x the median of the first 3 videos (with Studio CTR and 30 s retention), the model is weak. If one reaches 10x, re-test that topic framing once before treating it as repeatable (hit concentration was 74% to 91% in two studied channels).
  • Opening lever: if the median views of stake-first and other openings differ by about 1.0x in the first 10 videos, stop treating the opening as a lever.

11. UNKNOWN and not done

Studio CTR, retention, traffic; whether the model transfers to a new channel with no subscriber base; creator workflow of the studied channels; blind relabel of openings and thumbnails; PENNY and Paper Billions 14 and 28 day re-pull (2026-11-20 and 2026-12-04); any passed first-10 topic (none).
