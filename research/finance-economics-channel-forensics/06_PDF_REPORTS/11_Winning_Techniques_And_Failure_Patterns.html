1. Repeated winning patterns (earlier pass)

03_CROSS_CHANNEL_FINDINGS/REPEATED_WINNING_PATTERNS.md

Repeated Winning Patterns (supported by ≥3 channels)

Evidence base: 69 channel reports (01_CHANNEL_REPORTS/*/CHANNEL_REPORT.md), cross-channel concept ledger (02_SHARED_EVIDENCE/CROSS_CHANNEL_CONCEPT_LEDGER.csv, rule-based title matching), PI = views ÷ median of 15 nearest uploads on the same channel. All patterns are correlational; CTR/retention/traffic sources were not available.

W1. Topic/subject is the main lever — packaging is secondary (channels: 16, 3, 61, 62, 47, 20, 15, 35, 22, 26, 13, 6, 10 and most others)

  • Mr. Finance (016): identical title, thumbnail layout and hook on all 65 videos; views 401 (ski resort) → 266k (gym) by subject alone.
  • Matty Invests (003): same narrator/script style: young-adult budgeting PI 0.03-0.10 → retiree lifestyle PI 20-498 after a pivot.
  • Built On You (061), Mr. Ranks (020), The Expensive Part (047): one template, 100-1,000x spreads by subject.
  • PDF P21 verdict: FOLLOWED on 46 channels, CONTRADICTED on 3 (brand-dominated: Patrick Boyle; packaging-driven pair: Finance ANALYST).
  • Exception that matters: when topic is held constant, packaging can still be decisive — Finance ANALYST's same reunion story: 367 → 368k views after a specific title; Rick Finance's compounding pair: 229 → 205k.

W2. Winning topics share four properties (≥20 channels)

  1. The viewer can place themselves in it (age, balance, birth year, store they visit): "Ready at 45?" (Ivan), "Secretly Ahead at 55" (Tom Lazy), "Born Before 1966?" (Lana), "Costco Owes You" (Robin).
  2. Concrete stake with a number: "$500,000… $1,667 per month" (Matty), "$117 more every month" (Mark Calloway), "$46.14" (Tom Lazy Dividend).
  3. Gain/permission/protection framing beats loss/outrage for the same audience (see W5).
  4. An everyday object or place rather than an abstraction: buffets, mattress stores, Costco, shipping containers, airline seats, gyms (Business Decoded, Brimm, Neu, Mr. Finance).

W4. Parameter series beat remakes (5 channels)

Same promise, new parameter → repeated wins: Carl Invests ($100 → $500 → $1,000/month: PI 71, 25, 55), Robin MBA (Costco → Aldi: PI 446, 159), POV Wealth (lottery at work → lottery in a tiny apartment next day: PI 41, 24), Finance Simplified (growth vs dividends re-run with new numbers: PI 551 → 30), Ivan ("8 MORE Signs… PART 2": PI 10).

W5. Framing: gain, permission, protection, reassurance (≥12 channels)

  • Senior network: "money you can keep / bills you no longer owe / the IRS can't touch" wins; death/IRS-levy/scam fear and "you may be owed" windfalls lose (Nia, Lana, Mark Calloway, Madeline, Walter, Robin).
  • Retirement: "you've earned the right to stop working" (Uncle John), "secretly ahead" (Tom Lazy, Ivan), "it does the rest" (Rick) beat traps/dark sides.
  • Alicia: aspiration ("costs nothing") beats warnings.

W6. Hooks that recur in winners (≥15 channels; transcripts)

  • Matched pair: "Two guys, same age, same city, same $X…" (Mark Invests, Logical Money, Mark Retired at 55, Marcus Retires, Lucas Grant/POVs).
  • Exact time + object + number story drop: "It's 11:14 on a Sunday night…", "At 6:52… the ticket in your apron pocket is worth $84 million".
  • Number-first with a named source: "3.2%… from the Federal Reserve" (Mark Retired at 55), "$364,000… Survey of Consumer Finances" (Tom Lazy), Redfin 58% (Casual Finance).
  • Entitlement + injustice: "the law itself says you do not have to pay… Nobody tells you" (Nia).
  • Paradox: "One stock can make you a millionaire and never pay you a penny…" (Finance Simplified); "Here's something that should be mathematically impossible" (Nick).

Note: hook phrasing alone did not separate winners and losers within channels (identical openers on Walter Canada, Rick Finance, Mr. Finance) — hooks matter as delivery of a good topic, not as a substitute.

W7. Thumbnails: one idea, one number or one contrast (≥10 channels inspected)

Winning thumbnails show a binary choice (BUY vs RENT, ROTH vs 401k), a goal state with an amount ("WALK-AWAY MONEY", "$0 BILL"), a calm protagonist against a show-off (POV channels, often wordless), or a physical risk metaphor (umbrella in a rain of zeros). Losers: crowded infographics, three-to-five options, sad/worried faces, abstract props. Thumbnail style is constant within channels — style is identity, not the success driver.

W8. Format arbitrage and niche bending create channels (≥8 channels)

POV/second-person (Lucas Grant, Finance POV, POV Wealth, Ronnie, Nate), "every rank/level" ladders (Dark Ledger, Mr. Ranks), ELI5 allegories (Primate Economics bananas → MonkeyExplains; Primitive Education caveman; Crayon Capital), ownership-economics template into history (The Expensive Part), US senior concepts into Canada/UK/Australia (Walter, Arthur Penrose, Frank Law, Wealth Logic Australia).

W9. First movers win; late copies of browse-driven concepts fail — perennial search topics are the exception

  • Late copies failed: Finance POV "$5 a day" (539 views, after Sonny's 255k), Uncle Frank "wealthy retirees" (148, after Matty's 1.08M), Marcus "7 Things IRS Can't Take" (242, after 4 earlier versions), POV Wealth stepdad (3.3k, after Ronnie's 282k).
  • Copies that won: Logical Money's "Renting vs Buying – The Real Math" (1.73M, after Mark Invests' 495k), Capital Case Rockefeller (380k after Crayon's 1.3M) — perennial/canonical search subjects.

W10. Shorts work as discovery for allegory/concept formats

Primate Economics (inflation Short 15.7M), Casual Finance (macro Shorts 200-390k). Shorts on niche topics (robo-advisors, compounding stages) or milestone announcements get hundreds of views.

2. Failure patterns (earlier pass)

03_CROSS_CHANNEL_FINDINGS/FAILURE_PATTERNS.md

Repeated Failure Patterns (supported by ≥3 channels)

F1. Windfall promises ("you may be owed / free checks / hidden payouts") — 6 channels

Nia (zwKGhzEC5Jg PI 0.10), Lana (3 videos PI 0.07-0.08), Walter Canada (uxuK6112Cko 0.04, gMos6YxA2K0 0.05), Madeline (q_gt30cqIsk 0.05), Housing, Robin ("15 MORE places"). Viewers appear to discount too-good-to-be-true money (HYPOTHESIS).

F2. Outrage, conspiracy and shock framing — 6 channels

"Rigged… fake math" (Mark Calloway 0.14), "NEW TAX RATES WILL SHOCK AMERICA" (Robin 0.16 — script admits routine indexing), "BlackRock: The Conspiracies You're Living In" (Capital Case 0.06), Ronnie/Nostalgic Wheels "getting what they deserve" (Target 129 views). Contradicts PDF p.329 and p.345 (no conspiracy framing, no false urgency).

F3. Medicare deadlines and mechanics — 12 channels, median PI 0.47, 14 flops vs 6 wins

Medicare open-enrolment/plan-cancellation videos fail across the senior network (Mark Calloway, Lana, Marcus, Housing, Sarah Rickshaw, Uncle John, Tom Lazy). Exception: Nia's pre-deadline Annual Notice of Change video (PI 85) and Kevin's Medicare Savings Program "checks" (PI 63) — framed as money saved, not as a deadline.

F4. Narrow technical mechanics for a broad audience — ≥6 channels

IRMAA surcharges (Mark Retired at 55, Mark Calloway), RMD/bracket cutoffs (Tom Lazy ×4, Uncle John, Uncle Frank), ACA subsidy cliff (Tom Lazy Dividend), credit utilisation (Tom Talks Money, Madeline).

F5. Self-remakes within weeks and variant flooding — 7 channels

Kevin Explains (3 remakes PI 0.04-0.08), Tom Lazy Dividend ($420k remake 0.12), Dark Ledger (IRS ladder 3 days after banks: 0.13), Business Decoded (furniture after mattress: 0.33), Arthur Penrose & Frank Law (bill/£10k variants within days), Nick ("Milestones in your 40s" after 30s: 0.41). Contrast with W4 parameter series: what changes must be a new promise, not new words.

F6. Late copies of browse-driven concepts — ≥8 channels

See W9: $5-a-day, wealthy retirees, IRS list, stepdad revenge, "how X treats you at every level", Japanese habits, "wealthy drive old cars", insurance-company explainers (Nick, Ronnie 28 views, Nostalgic Wheels 171).

F7. Generic, oversupplied lifestyle clichés — ≥8 channels

"Wealthy people drive old cars" (Alicia 0.38, Max), "Japanese money habits" (Mark Invests 0.25, Ivan 0.26), "Psychology of Money summary" (Mark Invests 0.11), book summaries (The Wise Future 0.07), "hidden fees / subscriptions / free shipping" (Tom Talks Money, Alicia, Bob Invests).

F8. Geopolitics, war and politics on money channels — 8 channels

Brimm (Germany 0.16, America-Europe 0.09), Neu (China icebreakers 0.16), Economics Explained (Iran/Venezuela/Cuba), Money & Macro (Hungary, populists), How Money Works (Iran), Carl Invests (yen crisis 0.08), Casual Finance (oil crisis "secretly good" 0.28). Domestic wallet impact beats foreign conflict.

F9. Off-promise topics (topic-channel fit) — ≥10 channels

Ronnie Finance explainers (28-319 views), Finance POV thrift topics, Mr. Ranks crime/addiction ladders, Lucas Grant introspection, Max Invests silver-trading call, Business Decoded ransomware, The Expensive Part grim jobs. A concept that wins elsewhere fails when it breaks the channel's promise (Carl's $100/month concept won; Finance POV's $5/day flopped).

F10. Identity and quality failures — ≥6 channels

Name/handle/description mismatch (Nostalgic Wheels, Housing, Ronnie), keyword-stuffed suffixes ("/ Wealth Mindset" — Max Invests), typos and mislocalised titles (Marcus "Only Know 1st", "UK Pensioners" with US script), self-introductions before the promise (Nick, Ivan, Max). Max Invests is the archetype: template look of winners, no audience, abandoned.

F11. Policy-risk practices (documented, not proven causal)

Verbatim cross-channel scripts (Housing 13/24, Sarah Rickshaw 8/12, Kevin 14 near-duplicates), possibly synthetic presenters without disclosure, a prime-minister look-alike and a fabricated quote post (Walter Canada), misleading legislative/IRS claims (Kevin H.R. 9064, Madeline $600 rule — VERIFIED contradictions), political figures in thumbnails. One channel in the set (Wealth Logic) was removed for Community Guidelines violations.

What did not explain failure (null results)

Upload cadence and gaps (Spearman ≈ 0 on almost every channel), title length, presence of numbers alone, hook phrasing alone, thumbnail style within a channel, video length (except a few channels with modest correlations in both directions). Consistent with the 2026-11-12 audit: within-channel text/thumbnail style features are near-null predictors.

Evidence: concept ledger; Logical Money vs Finance POV $5/day. PDF: P01 Outlier, Method 1/4, P21. Do: keep an outlier database across 15+ channels (PDF p.7) and record first-publication dates. Avoid: copying a concept 2+ months after it peaked.
  2. AUDIENCE PROBLEM — Name the viewer's self-placement: age, balance, birth year, store, decision. Evidence: Matty pivot, Ivan "Ready at 45?", Lana "Born Before 1966?". PDF: P02. Do: one sentence "this is for people who…" (PDF p.65 step 1).
  3. CONTENT ANGLE — Gain/permission/protection beats loss/outrage; a hidden number beats general advice. Evidence: F1, F2, W5. PDF: P08, P22. Avoid: windfall promises, conspiracy, "SHOCK".
  4. TITLE + THUMBNAIL — Title: specific subject + situation + stake (PDF five-part engine); thumbnail: one binary choice, one amount or one calm-vs-showy contrast, ≤4 words, consistent character. Evidence: Finance ANALYST 367→368k pair; montages. PDF: P08-P11. Avoid: crowded infographics, CAPS, politicians' faces, look-alikes.
  5. HOOK (0-5 s) — Matched pair, exact-time story drop with an object and a price, or number-first with a named source. PDF: P12. Note: hooks are near-universal; they enable, not differentiate.
  6. INTRODUCTION (5-30 s) — Confirm the title's promise with the key number or the ironic situation; no self-introduction before it (Nick/Ivan/Max losers). PDF: P13, P14, P22.
  7. STORY STRUCTURE — Ladders/levels (escalation), 20-year matched race, list with mini-scenes, or dream → numbers → verdict. PDF: Engagement §5 escalation; Masterclass explainer structure (p.329).
  8. VALUE DELIVERY — Real numbers, named datasets (SCF, SSA, Redfin), exact forms/steps for protection topics. PDF: P16. Accuracy is non-negotiable in YMYL (two verified misleading winners exist; one channel terminated).
  9. PAYOFF — A clear verdict or the one action ("pick three this week", "run your three numbers", "send this to your parent"). PDF: P18, P22.
  10. NEXT VIDEO / SERIES — Parameter series (new amount, new store, new age), explicit next-video links (Alicia, Carl, Uncle John). Avoid: remaking the same promise within weeks (P01 contradictions).

C. What a new creator should launch with (practical blueprint)

  • Niche choice: Model 1 (business-ownership/everyday economics) if you can produce clean visuals; Model 2+3 (retirement adequacy + decision duels) if you prefer low-cost animation and data. Treat senior benefits as a later, accuracy-staffed option.
  • First 10 videos: 5 subjects from a cross-validated list (≥2 channels with PI ≥5 in the last 6 months), 3 parameter variants of whichever performs best, 2 tests of a second format (format rotation, PDF P03).
  • Packaging test: for every flop with a good subject, retitle with a specific scene/stake (Finance ANALYST precedent) rather than abandoning it.
  • Compliance: original scripts (no cross-channel paragraphs), disclose synthetic presenters/characters (Uncle John model), cite sources in descriptions (Nia/Wise Future model), no look-alikes or invented quotes.
  • Measure: Studio CTR, 30-second retention and traffic sources — none of which this public dataset can show (PDF Part D §6, §10).
