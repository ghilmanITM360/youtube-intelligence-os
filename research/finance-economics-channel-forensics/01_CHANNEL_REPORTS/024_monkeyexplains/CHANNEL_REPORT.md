## 17. Strengths and weaknesses
Unique, ownable comedic format; consistent opener; real-event timing; high floor of views. Weaknesses: caricature/legal risk, evergreen pieces lag, slow cadence.

## 18. Replicable working model
**"Satirical Scandal Explainer"**: pick this week's economic scandal or bubble → stand-in character with "a huge problem" → absurd metaphor for the numbers → what's really going on behind the scenes → satire disclaimer. Packaging: hand-drawn caricature + one punchline.

## 19. What to copy
A signature character opener; absurd-unit metaphors (bananas) for big numbers; fast publishing on genuine events; "Worse Than You Think" framing.

## 20. What not to copy
Pure definition videos without a peg; real-person caricatures without clear satire framing.

## 21. Missing evidence and limitations
No subscriber/description data; pastebin source lists not opened; event dates inferred from scripts; Shorts mixed with long-form timelines.

- Not available from this dataset for any channel: CTR, impressions, retention curves, traffic sources, subscriber growth history, comments text. Hook timings are word-position estimates (untimed transcripts).

## 22. Final channel diagnosis
1) Succeeds through a distinctive satirical format applied to current scandals. 2) Advantage: brand voice. 3) Model: satirical scandal explainers. 4) Topic: bubbles, debt, corruption, tech prices — timed to news. 5) Packaging: caricature + punchline. 6) Script: joke-dense allegory. 7) Interest: understanding + laughter. 8) Failures: timeless definitions (relatively). 9) Learn: format gives the floor, timing gives the ceiling. 10) Don't copy: real-person likeness without satire safeguards. 11) Repeatable: format concept yes; humour writing is a skill barrier. 12) Reputation: format loyalty matters. 13) Lesson: **a strong format plus a news peg beats either alone.**
