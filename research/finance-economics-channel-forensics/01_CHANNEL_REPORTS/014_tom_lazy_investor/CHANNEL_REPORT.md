## 17. Strengths and weaknesses
Named-dataset authority, reassurance benchmarks, experiment framing, scale. Weaknesses: extreme volume, template duplication across channels, narrow bracket topics, IP-adjacent mascot.

## 18. Replicable working model
**"Benchmark Reassurance"**: age-group median from a named survey → "if your numbers beat these, you're ahead" → what to do with the surplus → one experiment per month ("I tested…").

## 19. What to copy
Survey-median hooks; age-benchmark titles; "I tested X" experiments; same-name-different-rules comparisons.

## 20. What not to copy
Narrow high-balance bracket thresholds as a series; verbatim sister-channel titles/thumbnails; IP-adjacent characters; 40+ long uploads per month.

## 21. Missing evidence and limitations
Network/sister-channel relationship inferred from identical titles, thumbnails and research hooks — ownership UNVERIFIED; Aug–Nov 2026 only.

1) Wins with broad benchmark reassurance and Roth comparisons; loses with narrow bracket cutoffs and copied titles. 2) Advantage: data authority at scale. 3) Model: benchmark reassurance. 4) Topic: retirement-account planning. 5) Packaging: mascot + chart/gauge. 6) Script: dense modelling. 7) Interest: "am I ahead?". 8) Failures: narrow audiences, repetition. 9) Learn: same data, broader question. 10) Don't copy: verbatim templates, IP-adjacent mascot. 11) Repeatable: benchmark format yes. 12) Reputation: 100k base supports floor (~14k). 13) Lesson: **the widest version of the question wins; the bracket-line version is for a few thousand people.**
