## key
Clean 3-D-render 8-minute economics explainers (Aug 2024–Sep 2026, ~2/month) whose breakouts (116k-536k) explain **something the viewer has personally noticed — airline cabin layouts, Aldi stores spreading, Airbnb in Athens, Berlin's clubs, Dutch e-commerce** — while country-decline and geopolitics ("How Germany Destroyed Their Economy", "What Happens If America Abandons Europe") and corporate rise-and-fall histories collapse to ~800-1,700 views.

## identity
Faceless, design-led channel "Brimm." with minimalist white 3-D dioramas (bright red/blue focal object). No description/subscriber data in DB. Primary sub-niche: **"why the world looks like this" business/urban economics** (Europe + US). Audience: curious 20-40 viewers of Wendover/Polymatter-style explainers. Problem/job: explain an everyday observation with economics.

## topics
Housing/urban (13 videos, median PI 2.31, 7 ≥2x — Airbnb, tourism, cities) strongest; macro/country decline (0.46, 6 flops) weakest; geopolitics 1.31 (mixed); company cases 0.98. Early period stronger (1.61 → 1.04); sporadic cadence with a 216-day gap.

## comparison
Winners use "why/how this…" curiosity framing (truth/secret/why words 41% vs 9%) and slightly more numbers in scripts. The topic distinction (observable everyday phenomenon vs macro/geopolitics/corporate history) mirrors Neu exactly — two independent explainer channels, same split (cross-channel replication, INFERRED). Thumbnails: winners = white diorama + one coloured object; losers = dramatic photoreal war/fire or logo collisions.

Company data (Aldi store targets), cabin configurations, tourism/housing statistics; links almost absent (one trench.to). Idea sources (INFERRED): personal observation/travel (airline cabins, Athens, Berlin), news on store expansion, Europe-focused business media.

## trend
Mostly evergreen; Aldi expansion and Airbnb regulation tie into ongoing trends; war/geopolitics timing did not help.

## thumbnails
Distinctive minimalist white 3-D scenes with one saturated object (red Airbnb block, blue Aldi store, sliced plane), 1-4 words or a sign label (BERGHAIN, LOCAL). Consistent identity in winners; losers break the style.

## titles
"Why [Group] Use This Exact [Thing]", "How This Unassuming [Thing] Is Quietly Changing America", "What [Company] Did to [City] in 10 Years". Losers: "How [Country] Destroyed…", "What Happens If…", "The Rise and Fall of…".

## hooks
Concrete object + numbers ("This is a Boeing 777… 216 seats"), paradox ("On paper, the Netherlands shouldn't…"), viewer scene ("Imagine you're living in…"). Losers open with broad historical summaries ("For nearly 80 years, Europe has lived behind a shield…").

## script
~8 min (fixed), documentary narration, low "you", numbers moderate; endings summarise a takeaway ("next time you buy a plane ticket, remember this").

## engagement
Click: curiosity about something seen; stay: tight explanation; return: aesthetic consistency.

## publishing
~1.8 uploads/month over 25 months with a 216-day gap — sporadic; quality over quantity.

## model
**"Why Does This Look Like That?"**: pick a phenomenon viewers have seen (seat layouts, store waves, tourist neighbourhoods) → concrete object + numbers in the first sentence → economic mechanism → personal takeaway. Packaging: white diorama with one coloured hero object.

## copy
Observable-phenomenon topics; numbers-first object hooks; minimalist hero-object thumbnails; fixed 8-minute length.

## avoid
Country-decline geopolitics; rise-and-fall corporate histories; switching to dramatic war imagery.

## limitations
No subscriber/description data; sporadic sample across 2 years; cabin figures plausible but not verified.

## diagnosis
1) Wins with everyday-observation economics; loses with geopolitics and histories. 2) Advantage: design + topic curiosity. 3) Model: everyday-phenomenon explainer. 4) Topic: travel, retail, cities. 5) Packaging: white diorama. 6) Script: 8-min tight narrative. 7) Interest: "I've noticed that — why?". 8) Failures: macro/geopolitics, histories. 9) Learn: start from what the viewer has seen. 10) Don't copy: off-brand war thumbnails. 11) Repeatable: needs 3-D design capacity. 12) Reputation: low. 13) Lesson: **economics of the thing in front of you beats economics of nations.**
"If I wanted to build a channel like this, I would study m5WPi3LX9z0 and wlvup8NLKaY and replicate: one everyday phenomenon per video, a numbers-first object hook, an 8-minute mechanism explanation and a single-object minimalist thumbnail — leaving geopolitics to bigger channels."
