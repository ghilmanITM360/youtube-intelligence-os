## model
**"Enough-to-Walk-Away Data Check"**: one adequacy question → national statistic or two-option comparison in sentence one → ladder of levels/percentiles → "run your three numbers" action. Packaging: goal-state metaphor + one amount.

## copy
Statistic-first hooks naming the source; adequacy/percentile framing; "walk-away" ladders; 62-vs-70 style numeric comparisons.

## avoid

## limitations

## diagnosis
1) Wins by answering "do I have enough to quit?" with credible national data. 2) Advantage: statistical authority + early-retiree persona. 3) Model: adequacy data check. 4) Topic strategy: thresholds, percentiles, claiming comparisons. 5) Packaging: cartoon host + goal metaphor + amount. 6) Script: dense, analytical, long. 7) Interest: permission to retire. 8) Failures: narrow rules, generic traps. 9) Learn: data persuades when it answers the viewer's own question. 10) Don't copy: volume and length for their own sake. 11) Repeatable: yes with public data. 12) Reputation: persona adds credibility but not required. 13) Lesson: **sell permission ("you're ahead / you can walk away") backed by one real statistic.**
