## key
Second-person "POV: your life when…" money-story channel (~30 videos/month, 22-24 min) whose winners are **secret-wealth fantasies and everyday-decision POVs with a social-comparison scene** (704k "Living Rich in Secret"; 273k "Paying Cash for Cars"); the POV format itself is average (median PI 0.82) — it wins only when the scenario is a fantasy or a visible status contrast, and flops on abstract milestones.

## identity
Faceless illustrated character (round blank face, navy "RHG" cap) living scenes in comic-art thumbnails; channel name "Lucas Grant", no description in DB. Primary sub-niche: **money POV storytelling** — second-person narrative ("You're standing in your cousin's driveway…") that dramatises financial choices over years. Audience (INFERRED): 25-45 US viewers who like story over lecture, attracted to quiet/secret wealth. Problem/job: vicarious experience of a wealthier, calmer life + permission to choose frugality.

## topics
74 of 95 titles are "POV"; POV-tagged topic median PI 0.75 (54 videos), whereas the non-POV "wealth psychology" titles run 2.05. News-framed titles (n=2, median 33) are tiny-sample outliers. Content cools over time (median PI 1.29 → 0.92) — first winner (Jun 9) never repeated.

## comparison
Text proxies are flat (both halves ~7 "you" per 100 words, same numbers). Winners use fewer fear words in titles (10% vs 24%) and fewer thumbnail words (median 1.5 vs 2 — winners often wordless). Reusing an opening scene (paper plate at a cookout) across videos did not transfer success.

## trend
Format-trend driven (POV), topics evergreen; early winner then decay suggests novelty wearing off within 3 months (HYPOTHESIS).

## thumbnails
Comic-style rich-colour scenes with the same capped character; 0-2 words; social scenes (café, dealership, sales desk, neighbours). Winners show the character calm while others posture; losers rely on text overlays ("$90,000/Y @40", "YOUR 40s") or solitary scenes.

## titles
"POV: …" + "What Happens When / Your Life As / — It Changes Everything / Not What You Expected". The repeated "POV:" prefix on 78% of titles risks pattern fatigue (PDF p.68 mistake #6).

## hooks
Immersive second-person scene in present tense within the first sentence (100%); best ones contain a **specific social detail and a number** ("$612 a month, he tells you, unprompted"). No self-intro. No explicit promise — the scene is the promise.

## script
Novelistic second-person narration over decades, recurring motifs ("the 15th still comes every month"), minimal data; endings reflective, no CTA/next-video tease, links to other videos in descriptions instead.

## engagement
Click: fantasy + secrecy + status. Stay: immersive narrative (watch to see where "you" end up). Return: related-video links. Risk: sameness of narration across 95 videos.

## publishing
~30 uploads/month (≈1/day), 21-25 min, for 3 months (Jun–Sep 2026); 10 videos missing views/transcripts.

## strengths
Distinct immersive format; wordless contrast thumbnails; strong first-scene writing. Weaknesses: single-format monotony, reused opening scenes, low sourcing, decaying novelty.

## model
**"Secret-Wealth POV"**: second-person scene with a visible status contrast (you vs cousin/neighbour/diner regular) → follow the choice over years with a few concrete numbers → reveal the quiet payoff. Packaging: wordless comic scene where the character is calm and the other person shows off.

## copy
Status-contrast scenes; secret-wealth fantasy premise; wordless story thumbnails.

## avoid
Re-using the same opening scene; abstract milestone introspection; "POV:" on every title; clichés (old cars).

## limitations
No channel description/subscriber data; 10 videos lack transcripts/views; Jun–Sep 2026 only.

## diagnosis
1) Wins when the POV is a fantasy or status contrast; average otherwise. 2) Advantage: immersive format. 3) Model: second-person life simulation. 4) Topic: stealth-wealth and money-decision scenarios. 5) Packaging: wordless comic contrast scenes. 6) Script: literary second person. 7) Interest: living a richer, quieter life vicariously. 8) Failures: introspection, clichés, repetition. 9) Learn: format needs a sharp scenario. 10) Don't copy: one-format monotony. 11) Repeatable: yes. 12) Reputation: none. 13) Lesson: **a POV only works when the viewer can see the contrast in one frame.**
