## key
Small "real math" comparison channel (3.7k subs, median 536 views) that struck one rich vein — **Growth vs Dividend stocks** (three versions, two of them 129k and 205k; PI up to 551) — plus other investment head-to-heads (gold vs T-bills, index vs real estate); consumer-cost comparisons (EV vs hybrid vs gas, $50K cars, salary to own a home) and a Vanguard-fund projection flop at ~400-560 views. Unlike Kevin Explains, *re-running its own winning comparison* worked here.

## identity
Cartoon presenter (suited man, green tie) between two labelled options on white; description (VERIFIED): "animated storytelling channel… We don't give stock tips… through real-life scenarios… eye-opening comparisons". Primary sub-niche: **investment strategy comparisons with long-horizon maths** (dividends, growth, index funds, gold, T-bills, real estate). Audience: beginner-to-intermediate US investors 25-55 deciding *how* to invest. 40% of videos lack transcripts.

## topics
Investing (42 videos, median PI 1.15, 14 ≥2x) and taxes (2.29) lead; retirement (0.22), crypto (0.18), wealth habits (0.61) and consumer topics (cars/EVs under "other", 0.96 median but the bottom five) weak. Comparison-format titles overall median 0.67 — comparisons only win when both sides are **investment strategies the audience actually debates**.

## winners
1. **Stop Guessing! Growth vs Dividend Stocks Explained in 20 Minutes** (hkRXzD3CH3c, 129k, PI 551 — the highest PI in the dataset; baseline ~230 views): paradox hook "One stock can make you a millionaire and never pay you a single penny… Another can send you a check every 3 months and still leave you poorer" + explicit time promise ("In the next 20 minutes, you will").
2. **Dividend Stocks vs. Growth Stocks: The Math Most Investors Ignore** (4bg3bjvjCjE, 205k, PI 30): matched pair "Two investors, same $32,000, same 20 years. One collected $54,000 in dividends, the other 7,000. Guess which one ended up richer."
3. A third Growth-vs-Dividends thumbnail ("$100,000 → 15 YEARS") also sits in the winners' montage (W6).
4. **How Much Do You Need to Invest to Earn $5,900 a Month in Dividends?** (HrgoEuydmc8, PI 13.8), **$50,000, 10 Years, Two Investors — Real Estate vs Index Fund** (AXAsWtqbMwU, PI 12.6), **Gold vs. Treasury Bills** (Z1e2rtZ8Gpg, PI 10.1: "At the end of 1979… gold cost about $513. 26 years later… $514" — a striking flat-decades fact, consistent with historical prices).
Pattern: **one long-running investor debate, matched investors, exact money paths, a surprising winner.**

## comparison
Winners: denser numbers (4.71 vs 3.78/100 words) and fewer "you" (1.69 vs 2.62/100 words; titles 27% vs 42%) — analytical, not personal. Losers ask more questions in titles (40% vs 29%). The key is **topic-audience fit** (investor debates) and **two-option clarity**.

Company/IRS/Fed data (29/29/17 videos); historical price data (gold 1979-2025). No links. Idea sources (INFERRED): perennial investor-forum debates (dividends vs growth on Reddit/Bogleheads), Mark Invests-style "Two investors" device, own winners re-run (three growth-vs-dividend versions).

## trend
Evergreen; no news hooks; slight improvement over time (0.94 → 1.11).

## thumbnails
Presenter between two labelled sides (green "GROWTH" vs "DIVIDENDS"), one key number ($54K vs $7K), chart icons; winners keep it binary; losers show three options, five tickers or lifestyle scenes.

## titles
"[A] vs [B]: The Math Most Investors Ignore", "Stop Guessing! … Explained in 20 Minutes", "Where Would $50,000 Have Done Better?".

## hooks
Paradox (millionaire who never got paid vs dividend payer who got poorer), matched investors with exact sums, historical flat-price fact; explicit promise and duration.

## script
18-21 min of worked comparisons (total return, reinvestment, taxes); endings ask "which would you pick and why" (comment prompts).

## engagement
Click: unresolved investor debate; stay: the reveal of who won; return: next head-to-head.

## publishing
~13.5 uploads/month; consistent.

## strengths
Clear binary comparisons with real maths; paradox hooks; successful angle-varied remakes. Weaknesses: off-promise consumer topics, multi-option comparisons, missing transcripts.

## model
**"Investor Debate Duel"**: a debate investors argue about → matched investors, same money, same years → full-return maths (taxes, reinvestment) → surprising winner → comment prompt. Re-run winners with new numbers/horizons.

## copy
Two-option investor debates; paradox + time-promise hooks; angle-varied remakes of a winning debate.

## avoid
Three-way or five-ticker comparisons; consumer car/EV/salary topics on an investing channel; comparisons without a real debate.

## limitations
40% of transcripts missing; tiny subscriber base makes PI ratios extreme (PI 551 on ~230-view baseline).

## diagnosis
1) Small channel that found a single high-demand debate and re-ran it successfully. 2) Advantage: clean comparison maths. 3) Model: investor debate duels. 4) Topic: dividends/growth/index/gold. 5) Packaging: binary presenter thumbnail. 6) Script: worked returns. 7) Interest: "which strategy wins?". 8) Failures: consumer topics, complexity. 9) Learn: a real debate + binary framing. 10) Don't copy: multi-option clutter. 11) Repeatable: yes. 12) Reputation: none. 13) Lesson: **find the debate your audience already argues about and settle it with maths — then settle it again with new numbers.**
"If I wanted to build a channel like this, I would study hkRXzD3CH3c and 4bg3bjvjCjE and replicate: a genuine two-sided investor debate, a paradox opening with matched investors and exact sums, total-return maths, a binary thumbnail with the two end values, and periodic re-runs with new horizons."
