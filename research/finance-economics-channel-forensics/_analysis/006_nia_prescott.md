## titles
Long (≈80-100 chars) two-part titles: claim + "— Most Never Do / Most Seniors Only Know One / Not Knowing"; ⚠️ emoji on deadline titles; explicit audience ("Seniors", "After 65", "Born before 1961" in script). Problems: repetitive "Most … Never…" suffix on ~all titles; some all-emoji/❌ clutter.

## hooks
Formal statement hooks that combine entitlement + injustice ("the law itself says they do not have to pay… Nobody tells you"), or deadline + asymmetry ("not one office… is required to write to you"). Audience qualifier early ("Born before 1961?"). No greetings, no self-introduction.

## script
~1,400-1,600 words over 9-12 min (~130 wpm, slow for older viewers); structure: entitlement → why nobody tells you → item-by-item rules with eligibility → how to apply/where (official office) → anti-scam caveat ("Applying costs nothing… anyone asking for money…") → share with a parent. Numbers spelled in words (TTS-friendly). No open-loop phrases; strong share CTA.

## engagement
Click: "money that is yours" + deadline. Stay: list of eligibility rules; each item a possible personal win. Share: explicit "hand this to your parent" — the channel optimises for sharing to older relatives (INFERRED). Trust: official links, anti-scam caveats, calm voice.

## publishing
~18 uploads/month, median gap 1 day, 9-12 min. Publishing pace not related to PI; timing relative to deadlines is.

## strengths
Clear audience; entitlement + deadline formula; official-source links; shareability to adult children; recurring calendar of hooks. Weaknesses: possibly synthetic presenter (disclosure/trust risk), repetitive title suffixes, fear topics that flop, same-day news videos that lose to authorities.

## model
**"Senior Entitlement Calendar"**: list what seniors can legally stop paying/keep/claim, tied to a real upcoming date → hook = entitlement + "nobody is required to tell you" → each item: rule, who qualifies, where to apply (official office) → scam caveat → "send this to your parent". Publish 1-3 weeks *before* each benefits deadline.

## copy
Gain-framed entitlement topics; real calendar deadlines; audience qualifier in the first line; official links in descriptions; share-to-parent ending; anti-scam caveat.

## avoid
Death/estate freeze fear, IRS-levy scare and "free checks/unclaimed money" promises; publishing on the day of an official announcement; identical "Most … Never" suffixes on every title; an unexplained synthetic human presenter (disclosure rules).

## limitations
Claims in scripts not verified item-by-item (state exemptions vary); presenter synthetic status unverified; Jun–Nov 2026 only; the October-1 "numbers change" claim is vague in the hook (could be misleading — UNVERIFIED).

## diagnosis
1) Wins when it tells seniors about money they can keep before a date; fails on death, scam and IRS-fear topics. 2) Advantage: entitlement-deadline formula + official sources. 3) Model: senior benefits calendar lists. 4) Topic strategy: benefits calendar + evergreen "after 65" entitlements, remade when they hit. 5) Packaging: serious presenter, flag, date, $0. 6) Script: slow, formal, eligibility-driven, scam-safe. 7) Interest: fear of losing money already earned + hope of keeping more. 8) Failures: loss/death framing, spammy "free checks", late news. 9) Learn: frame protection as a gain with a deadline. 10) Don't copy: synthetic presenter without disclosure; repetitive suffixes. 11) Repeatable: calendar-led entitlement lists. 12) Reputation: low dependence; but YMYL trust requires accuracy. 13) Lesson: **seniors click on "money you're entitled to keep, before date X" — not on "you could lose everything".**
