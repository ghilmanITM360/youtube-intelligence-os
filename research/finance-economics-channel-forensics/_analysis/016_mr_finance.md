## key
A near-perfect natural experiment: all 65 videos use one title ("The Economics of Owning a [X]"), one thumbnail layout ("SO YOU WANT TO OWN A [X]" + illustrated storefront + cost callouts) and one hook ("Okay, so you want to own a [X]…") — yet views range 401 → 266k purely by **which business X is**: everyday dream businesses (gym, café, food truck, cinema) win; exotic or professional ones (ski resort, private island, pharma, martial-arts club) lose.

## identity
Faceless business-economics explainer channel (no description/subscriber data in DB). Primary sub-niche: **small-business ownership economics** — unit economics, margins and hidden costs of a specific business. Audience (INFERRED): 20-45 people who daydream about opening a business, plus curious viewers ("is a gym actually profitable?"). Problem solved: reality check on the dream before investing. Format is the brand.

## topics
Rule-based topic tags mislabel every video "macro_economy" (because of "economics") — analyst re-tag: all are business-ownership case studies. Performance by business type (from titles, INFERRED grouping): consumer dream businesses (gym 266k, café 108k, food truck 30k, cinema 57k, five-star hotel) top; capital-heavy/niche (ski resort 401 views, private island 9k, pharmacy 8k, pharma company 3k, martial-arts club 4k, gold mine) bottom. Data centre (AI-era topical, PI 4.1) shows timeliness can lift a non-consumer business.

## winners
1. **The Economics of Owning a Gym** (4nYkN0wTi2Q, 266k, PI 8.7): "Okay, so you want to own a gym… Walk into any gym on a weekday evening, and it looks like the easiest business in the world" — the viewer has *personally seen* the business (familiarity) + the illusion to be broken.
2. **The Economics of Owning a Cafe** (yZrktmL624M, 108k, PI 6.75 — undated, channel-median baseline): "If cafes were as simple as they look, every street corner would have one…"
3. **…Food Truck** (aMRg9-0zJF0, PI 4.2): "That's the version people see on social media. Long lines…".
4. **…Data Center** (wDbackJX2hI, PI 4.1): timely AI-infrastructure angle.
5. **…Cinema** (yFF-415SyAA, PI 3.5): full-house paradox hook "every seat… taken… And the owner might still los[e money]".
Pattern: **businesses the viewer visits and romanticises**, opened by naming the romantic picture and promising the hidden numbers.

## failures
- **…Ski Resort** (KmQ7koHwuqY, 401 views, PI 0.14), **…Private Island** (KnZ_LUXBOPo, PI 0.20): fantasy-scale ownership few viewers can relate to as a plan.
- **…Pharmaceutical Company** (LPk-WVb7bcw, PI 0.18): hook breaks the template (Turing/Daraprim story) and becomes an industry explainer, not "owning".
- **…Pharmacy** (-A1Yc9rk5_c, PI 0.22) and **…Martial Arts Club** (zcgFKPC44ds, PI 0.13): professional/specialist businesses (requires a pharmacist licence or a black belt) — narrower dreamer pool.
Since packaging and script are constant, the evidence for "topic decides" is as strong as this dataset allows (VERIFIED constancy of title/thumbnail/hook; causal attribution still INFERRED).

## comparison
Text and thumbnail proxies are identical across halves (0% numbers, same 6.5 OCR words, same data density 1.6). Only length correlates slightly with PI (ρ=0.23). Winners = familiar consumer businesses; losers = exotic or credentialed businesses. This is the clearest within-channel support for PDF P21.

## titles
Single template "The Economics of Owning a [X]" (100%). No numbers, no "you", no fear — and still a 660x spread.

## hooks
"Okay, so you want to own a [X]. [Romantic picture]… Here's the thing / Simple, except it is not simple." — consistent dream → reality reversal (contradiction hook).

## script
~24 min: startup costs → revenue model → margins/hidden costs → failure modes → who succeeds; numbers ~3/100 words; endings distil "the one thing that actually matters".

## strengths
Infinitely scalable template; strong dream-vs-reality hook; clear identity. Weaknesses: subject choice drifted to exotic businesses; total template monotony; short lifespan in DB.

## model
**"So You Want to Own a ___"**: pick businesses viewers personally visit and daydream about → romantic picture → numbers that break it → who actually wins → one rule. Packaging: inviting storefront illustration + "SO YOU WANT TO OWN A".

## copy
The template itself; subject selection by familiarity and daydream appeal; dream-vs-reality opening.

## avoid
Exotic/ultra-rich ownership (islands, ski resorts) and credentialed professions; breaking the template's promise with industry history.

## limitations
Topic tags mis-assigned by rule (corrected by analyst); 2 undated videos; no subscriber data; only 2 months of output.

## diagnosis
1) Success is decided by the business chosen, with everything else constant. 2) Advantage: universal dream-business template. 3) Model: ownership economics series. 4) Topic: familiar consumer businesses. 5) Packaging: storefront + one headline. 6) Script: dream → costs → reality. 7) Interest: "could I run this?" 8) Failures: unrelatable or credentialed businesses. 9) Learn: choose subjects the viewer has stood inside. 10) Don't copy: exotic ownership topics. 11) Repeatable: completely. 12) Reputation: none. 13) Lesson: **same video, different subject — 660x difference.**
"If I wanted to build a channel like this, I would study 4nYkN0wTi2Q and yZrktmL624M and replicate the 'So you want to own a ___' template on businesses ordinary viewers visit weekly — gyms, cafés, car washes, laundromats — with a dream-vs-numbers hook and an inviting storefront thumbnail, rotating in a second format to avoid template monotony."
