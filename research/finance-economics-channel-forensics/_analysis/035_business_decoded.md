## key
Everyday-business-paradox explainer (5.8k subs, median 2.6k views) that produced one of the dataset's largest relative hits — **"Why Buffets Don't Care How Much You Eat" (1.46M, PI 297)** — plus mattress stores, free bread, outlet "designer" clothes and Costco's cheap deals (100-180k); crime/industry topics (ransomware, fake medicines, self-storage history) and luxury-pricing fall to 160-1,800 views. It confirms the Neu/Brimm/Mr. Finance pattern: **a paradox the viewer has personally seen** beats abstract industries.

## identity
Description (VERIFIED): "Ever wondered what it really costs to start, buy, or run a business? … from pizza shops and coffee shops to airlines, hospitals, banks…". Faceless illustrated explainers. Primary sub-niche: **consumer-facing business models ("how does this place make money?")**. Audience: curious adults 20-50. One Short is a milestone announcement (says "1,000 subscribers" while titled "5K" — sloppy).

## topics
Company/business cases (14, median PI 1.43) after a weak macro start (13 "macro" videos, median views 639 in Jul-Aug); the pivot to everyday business paradoxes in late Sep coincides with the jump (0.57 → 1.02 median PI).

## winners
1. **Why Buffets Don't Care How Much You Eat** (2T8JrUE_bz4, 1.46M, PI 297): scene "It's Saturday night at an all you can eat buffet… $20… a man is on his fourth plate… steak… shrimp" — the viewer's own suspicion turned into a paradox.
2. **How Empty Mattress Stores Make So Much Money** (LMGT7NErBL8, 101k, PI 97): "You've driven past it a hundred times… the parking lot is almost always empty" — a widely shared internet curiosity (mattress-store money-laundering memes) answered with economics.
3. **Why Restaurants Give You Free Bread — And Still Make More Money** (nt2Zn2GftOQ, 179k, PI 28).
4. **Why Outlet Stores Sell "Designer" Clothes So Cheap** (YDTDdDZVtfk, 138k, PI 21.7): the made-for-outlet lines reveal.
5. **Why Costco's Cheapest Deals Make It So Much Money** (rW0Cm4dGPH0, PI 16): membership-fee model.
Pattern: **"Why does [place you've been] do [apparently irrational thing]?" + a second-person scene in that place.**

## failures
- **How Empty Metal Boxes Became a $40 Billion Business** (Wzwz5HuIZo4, 932 views, PI 0.10): self-storage — riddle-like title hides the subject.
- **The Hidden Business Behind Fake Medicines** (GPoQlVLqChM, 161) and **The Hidden Economics of Ransomware Gangs** (yiuFx4WOkbY, 184): crime industries — no personal experience, sensitive.
- **Why Luxury Brands Charge 10x More for the Same Thing** (CWdr9NRvOEE, 1.8k): heavily supplied topic.
- **Why Do Empty Furniture Stores Stay Open for Decades?** (V10PCD0Wgeo, PI 0.33): mattress-store concept re-run a month later — a self-remake that underperformed.

## strengths

## model
**"Why Does This Place Do That?"**: a place viewers visit + an apparently irrational practice → scene → unit economics → the real profit engine.

## copy
Everyday-place paradox titles; second-person scene openings with a price.

## avoid
Crime-industry economics; riddle titles; quick re-runs of a hit's concept.

## limitations
Montage not inspected; 3.7 months; small subscriber base.

## diagnosis
1) Hit huge with everyday business paradoxes; flops on crime/industry/luxury. 2) Advantage: topic instinct. 3) Model: everyday-place economics. 4) Topic: buffets, outlets, restaurants, Costco. 5) Packaging: OCR suggests short labels. 6) Script: scene + unit economics. 7) Interest: "I always wondered that". 8) Failures: crime, riddles. 9) Learn: start from a place the viewer has stood in. 10) Don't copy: remakes. 11) Repeatable: very. 12) Reputation: none. 13) Lesson: **the best economics topic is the one the viewer already wondered about at the table.**
