## 2. First 30 days: checkpoints
| When | Do | Do not |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 (publish) | note title, thumbnail, publish time, topic, trend dependency | judge anything |
| Day 2 to 3 | check impressions and CTR for sanity (not broken, not wildly outside 2 to 10%) | change packaging on tiny samples |
| Day 7 | retention curve of long videos; traffic source mix | compare with competitors' views |
| Day 14 to 21 | start an A/B test (up to 3 variants, winner by watch time, long-form only, desktop Studio, about 2 weeks; Shorts excluded) only if impressions are enough | test several things at once |
| Day 28 | review: per-video table; apply the kill/keep rule below; revise the calendar | declare the channel a success or failure from 4 videos |
Per-video record (one row each): date, topic, title, thumbnail id, length, impressions, CTR, views, AVD, % viewed, search / suggested / browse share, new vs returning, subs gained, notes.

## 3. Decision rules (HYPOTHESES, replace with our data)
1. Packaging problem: impressions fine but CTR very low and falling → test a new thumbnail first, then title.
2. Content problem: CTR within the usual band but retention drops sharply in the first 30 s → rework hook and intro for the next video.
3. Topic problem: impressions very low across two different packaging attempts → drop that topic type (Phase E kill rule: if both conditional topics show weak impressions after 28 days, return to Phase C and test the other candidate sub-niches).
4. Compare only our own videos with each other, same format and length.
5. Never copy a competitor's CTR number: none is known.

## 4. Your assumption: target Search and Suggested together. My evaluation
**Partly suitable; I recommend a different ordering.**
- Search is the only source we can design for: a specific question ("why is rent so high") that people already type (first autocomplete suggestion, VERIFIED) with an honest title. It does not need history.
- Suggested is an outcome, not a lever: it depends on each viewer's history and how viewers respond to the video. Audience overlap with the reference channels can be argued from topic adjacency (INFERRED) but we could not test it (the sidebar test was personalised, UNKNOWN). Designing "for Suggested" mostly means good packaging and satisfaction, which are needed anyway.
- Browse (home feed) is how the small channels in our data (Neu, Brimm.) seem to reach people on topics with no measurable search demand (HYPOTHESIS; not provable without their Studio data).
- Evidence limits: public views do not show traffic sources. Recommendation: **Search-anchored evergreen videos first (rent), a news-window video for spike traffic (airfares), then one Browse-style industry test.** Treat Suggested as something to measure from day 7, not to plan around. Shorts are tracked separately.
- Where I agree with you: clear titles, specific questions, consistent expectations and satisfying structure help both.
