# Phase D round 1: topic discovery and validation (2026-11-18)

Scope: 25 seed topics drawn from the Phase C shortlist (S1 industry mechanisms 12, S2 company/history stories 8, S3 everyday mechanisms 5). Evidence: YouTube search supply (`../12_YOUTUBE_SEARCH_AND_SUGGESTED/YOUTUBE_SEARCH_SUPPLY_2026-11-18.csv`), Trends and news (`../11_GOOGLE_TRENDS_AND_NEWS/TRENDS_AND_NEWS_CAPTURE_2026-11-18.md`), Phase B/C competitor data. Labels VERIFIED / INFERRED / HYPOTHESIS / UNKNOWN.

## Limits (apply to every row)
- Search results were fetched from a signed-in browser (US region set), so ranking may be personalised. Only the first 12 video results per query; "recent" = published within about 12 months, from rounded text ("3mo ago").
- Views are public totals, not CTR, retention or search volume. Trends were read by eye, 12 months, US only. Suggested/Browse placement cannot be checked.
- Queries are my guesses at viewer phrasing; a different phrasing could change supply. This is a screen, not proof of an opening. **No topic here is approved.**

## Rule used (same spirit as the 2026-11-14 screen)
Advance only if: (1) demand is measurable in Trends or by competitor outliers, (2) recent strong supply for the exact question is not saturated, (3) a distinct angle with primary sources is feasible, (4) it is not a news read-out.

## What this suggests (INFERRED, MEDIUM-LOW)
1. Search demand and "open supply" rarely coincide: the topics with measurable demand have strong incumbents, and the open ones have no measurable demand. This matches the 2026-11-14 result (0 of 10).
2. S2 (company history) is weak on search; its reference wins appear browse-driven, so it depends on packaging, not topic choice.
3. S1's practical path is Neu-style Browse/Suggested reach plus one or two search-backed anchors (airlines); this cannot be proven without our own Studio data.
