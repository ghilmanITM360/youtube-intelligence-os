Google Trends and news capture (2026-11-18)

Captured 2026-11-18 in Chrome (Trends Explore, United States, Web Search, all categories). Trends values are relative (0 to 100 within one chart) and were read by eye from screenshots; no CSV was exported. Exact-phrase terms with low volume show as about 0 next to a bigger term, which means "not measurable here", not "nobody searches". Five terms per chart.

Chart (window) Term Reading (INFERRED from the chart)
S1, 12 months how airlines make money steady, average about 40; one spike to 100 around Apr-May 2026; about 30 in Nov 2026
S1, 12 months how airports / cruise ships / supermarkets make money, how container shipping works about 0 with a few short blips (airports reach about 25 twice)
S1 + context, 5 years data center rises from about 5 to 100 (late 2025) then about 30 now; blockbuster small spikes in 2022
S2, 12 months toys r us average about 40; high Nov 2025 (100), low about 27 mid-2026, spike about 90 in Sep-Oct 2026, about 57 now (cause not checked)
S2, 12 months wework about 16, flat
S2, 12 months blockbuster collapse, kodak collapse, nokia decline about 0
S3, 12 months how inflation works 100 in Nov-Dec 2025, falling to about 10 by Aug 2026 (average about 38)
S3, 12 months how banks create money steady about 18 to 25

S3, 12 months how credit scores work about 9; why groceries expensive about 4

Reading: measurable search demand sits in broad, familiar subjects (airlines, banks, rent, inflation, Toys R Us), not in the long tail of "how X makes money". Long-tail industry topics may still reach viewers through Browse and Suggested (Neu, Brimm. evidence), but Trends cannot confirm that (HYPOTHESIS).

News hooks (WebSearch, 2026-11-18; secondary sources, several syndicated or inconsistently dated, not verified against primaries)

  • Container shipping: carriers are returning to the Suez Canal; overcapacity may push rates down; one weekly update (13 Nov 2026) lists high spot rates that conflict with the overcapacity story, so current levels are UNVERIFIED. Source pages: C.H. Robinson Nov 2026 update, Xeneta weekly, Seatrade Maritime.
  • Airlines: IATA's December 2025 outlook was US$41B net profit for 2026 (3.9% margin); results from mid-2026 say IATA cut this to about US$23B because jet fuel rose about 70%. The cut is reported only by secondary outlets here; IATA's own page was not opened at capture time. Later the same day the IATA press release (No. 26, 2026-06-07) was read and confirmed $23.0B for 2026 (2025 estimate $45B; $41B was the earlier forecast): see ../09_TOPIC_DISCOVERY/PRIMARY_DATA_PULL_2026-11-18.md. Date note: the cut was announced in June 2026, five months before this capture, so it is background, not a fresh 7-day event. Both are hooks, not topics: a news read-out alone is not monetizable (policy 1311392), and Neu or large channels may already cover them.
