# Thumbnail model from the six reference channels (2026-11-19)
Owner request: "winning thumbnail models" from Casual Finance, Monkey Economics, MonkeyExplains, Crayon Capital, PaperBillionsTV, Capital Case.
Method: contact sheets of the top-viewed thumbnails per channel from our library (Casual Finance 12, Monkey Economics 11, MonkeyExplains 12, Crayon Capital 9, Capital Case 8, Paper Billions 6; 58 images, all long-form plus some Shorts) viewed by me on 2026-11-19. This is OBSERVATION of a sample (INFERRED patterns). It does NOT prove that any feature causes views: our own title and hook tests found no feature separating winners (see `13_TITLE_MODELS/`), and no thumbnail test has been done. Shorts thumbnails appear as vertical frames with blurred side bars; ignore them for this model.

## Two families
- **Chart-forward minimalist (Casual Finance, Monkey Economics/Explains):** white or plain ground, one real-looking chart, a serif or hand-lettered 2 to 4 word headline, a small worried mascot. This is the closest fit to our "the number behind the price" idea, because our topics have real series (rent index, airfares, jobs, CPI).
- **Cinematic character scene (Crayon Capital, Capital Case, Paper Billions):** big illustrated person or company, dark dramatic background, one name or number as text. Fits history and company stories.

## Cautions
- Several thumbnails caricature real people (Jensen Huang, Epstein, Rockefeller, Trump). Real-person depiction raises policy and legal risk; do NOT copy that. Use invented characters.
- Paper Billions thumbnails use sexualised or sensational scenes ("BLACKMAIL?", dinner-date framing). Not a model for a serious channel; it also risks misleading packaging.
- Copying a layout one-for-one is a similarity risk (Phase Eight). Copy the STRUCTURE (one idea, short text, one chart, one mascot), not the art or the exact words.

## Proposed model for our channel (hypothesis, to be tested)
1. Family A, chart-forward: one real chart from our primary source (BLS, IATA, EIA), red line only where the data actually rises or falls, a "2026" or period label, source tag in small type.
2. 2 to 4 word headline in large serif or bold sans; no claim the chart does not support (for example, never "rents are falling").
3. Our own mascot (an ant or bee if the owner picks an insect name), always the same small character reacting to the chart.
4. White or very light ground for charts; one dark cinematic variant reserved for company or history videos if we ever make them.
5. Text under about 4 words; no real people's faces.
Test plan: for each video make 2 or 3 variants and use YouTube's Test and compare (long-form only, up to 3 variants, winner by watch-time share), per the official help page read in Phase A.
