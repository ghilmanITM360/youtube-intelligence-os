## Reusable story grammar

INTERPRETATION: recognizable anomaly; immediate stake; prerequisite explanation; visual mechanism; source/counterexample; consequence; qualification/payoff. Curiosity comes from delaying a resolvable mechanism, not merely withholding all useful information. SpaceX's initial personal reveal comes early while its rule explanation is delayed; these are two different payoffs.

Humor appears as invented dialogue, character reactions, metaphor and imported memes. Evidence introduction often alternates simplified diagrams with source excerpts. Contradictions recur, but typical investor comedy and the weak MicroStrategy opener show humor/contradiction are not sufficient predictors. Approximate ASR words/min measures text span, not audible articulation. Midpoint resets are topic-specific: SpaceX B015/B019 revisits the buyer question before/after sponsorship. A reset need not be at precisely 50% runtime. CTA is not always last: SpaceX B028 precedes final qualification B029.

## Original script templates

### Self-contained Short, approximately 45–75 seconds

Our design template, grounded in available 43–75s examples: 0–3s one observed anomaly and matching visual; 3–10s precise question/stake; 10–30s one mechanism with two or three progressive steps; 30–45s one dated evidence panel and material exception; final 10–20s answer plus conditional consequence. Fit to narration, never force a fixed word quota. The 60s Japan example supports a complete causal-chain structure; the 43s SpaceX teaser supports an alternate explicit teaser, not proof that withholding an answer is optimal. Use one question per Short and label an unfinished teaser as such.

### 5–8-minute explainer

Actual 6.23m investor-types and 6.78m MicroStrategy examples support this runtime range; the 4.63m novice-investor story supports an adjacent compact story. Our original template: 0–20s anomaly and promised answer; 20–60s context/one fictional analogy; 1–3m two mechanism steps; 3–5m dated evidence, alternatives and worked example; 5–7m consequence, what would falsify the explanation and payoff; optional CTA after answer. Six archetypes are useful for comedy but should not be presented as an exhaustive factual taxonomy. Separate invented dialogue from evidence.

### 10–15-minute mechanism documentary

Supported examples: Brace 12.55m, economy/AI-financing 13.8m and SpaceX 14.87m; housing/bond/IPO examples are longer (15.35–17.38m), so they cannot establish a mandatory 10–15m target. Our template: first 30s puzzle/stake; 0.5–2m roadmap and definitions; 2–6m actor/mechanism chapters; 6–8m counterevidence and reset; 8–12m consequence/scenarios; 12–15m answer, limits and CTA. A sponsor, if used, gets a clear commercial label and a short recap afterward. Do not place the core answer behind a sponsor by default.

## Retention hypotheses and tests

- HYPOTHESIS: a clear early question reduces confusion. Test 30s retention plus a comprehension prompt against an equally accurate alternative opening.
- HYPOTHESIS: progressive dependencies reduce cognitive overload. Test recall of a mechanism and retention around chart builds, not number of cuts alone.
- HYPOTHESIS: delayed mechanisms and sponsorship risk unresolved-promise drop-off. Inspect Studio retention at B015–B019 equivalents; no retention curve exists here.
- HYPOTHESIS: repeated alarm rhetoric can fatigue returning viewers. Compare new/returning retention and title truthfulness across an original series.

No public-view ranking validates any of these mechanisms. Use authorized narration, original scripts/illustrations, dated financial facts and explicit scenario conditions.
