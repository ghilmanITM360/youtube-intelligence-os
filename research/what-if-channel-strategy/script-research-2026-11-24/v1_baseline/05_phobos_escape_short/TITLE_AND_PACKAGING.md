# Slot 05 · Short · Title and packaging
*Self-review draft. No A/B tests, CTR data or audience data exist for any title below; none is predicted.*

**Original calendar title:** Could You Jump Off a Moon and Never Come Back?
**Recommended working title:** Could You Jump Off Phobos and Never Come Back?

**Three alternative titles**
1. Can You Escape Phobos With a Jump?
2. How Fast Would You Need to Jump to Leave Phobos?
3. Phobos Has Almost No Gravity: Could You Jump Away for Good?

**Viewer's expected payoff (one sentence):** The viewer learns the isolated-body escape speed (~11 m/s), why a jump falls back in the simple model, and that escaping Phobos would still leave you orbiting Mars.

**Thumbnail concept (text description only; no asset created):** A lumpy grey moon in the foreground with a large Mars limb behind; a tiny figure with a dotted arc that curves back to the surface. Text (max 3 words): "JUMP OFF PHOBOS?". Original art, no NASA imagery reused.

**Why title and thumbnail work together:** Title and thumbnail both ask the jump question for a specific moon; the calendar's generic "a moon" is narrowed to Phobos in both.

**Science, ambiguity, overlap and overpromise risks**
- Calendar says "a moon"; narrowed to Phobos (the calendar core question names Phobos).
- 3 m/s jump is an assumption; a simple model is not a trajectory.
- Could be read as an unconditional real-world result: script says "a proper model is needed".

**Competitor / prior-art findings (with coverage limits)**
- Munroe "Little Planet" (escape-speed rule of thumb) - related mechanism; Phobos appears only in a book photo caption (OCR). No registry title on Phobos. V020 (low-gravity jump) read: technique only. Coverage limit: other creators not surveyed.

*Packaging checks applied (creator-heuristic prompts, not platform facts): the title promises only what the script delivers; the first spoken sentence repeats the promise; the thumbnail text is ≤ 4 words and prints no unverified number.*
