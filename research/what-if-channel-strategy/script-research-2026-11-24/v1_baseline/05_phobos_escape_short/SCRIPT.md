## Voice-over

Phobos is a lumpy little moon of Mars, about twenty-two kilometres across. Could you jump off it and never come back?

If Phobos were a perfect ball, alone in space, you would need to leave the surface at about eleven metres per second. That is roughly forty kilometres an hour.

On Earth, a very strong jump leaves the floor at roughly two to three metres per second. Say Phobos gives you the same. Not enough. In our simple model, you rise, then fall back, in a trip lasting tens of minutes.

Real Phobos is not a ball. Its shape, its spin and the pull of Mars all change the number, so only a proper model could say for sure.

And even if you escaped Phobos, you would still be orbiting Mars. Leaving Mars takes hundreds of metres per second more. So: not with a jump, and not for good.
