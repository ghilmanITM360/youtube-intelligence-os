## Voice-over

Radio waves travel at the speed of light, and it still takes a day for one to reach Voyager 1.

Light covers about three hundred thousand kilometres every second. That gets it to the Moon in just over a second. It gets it to the Sun in a little over eight minutes.

Voyager 1 is much farther. In November 2026, NASA announced that the spacecraft had reached one light-day from Earth, about twenty-six billion kilometres. A light-day is exactly what it sounds like: the distance light travels in twenty-four hours.

So a command sent from Earth takes about a day to arrive. A reply takes another day to come back. That is two days for a single exchange.

And the delay keeps growing, by about five seconds every day, because Voyager keeps moving away.
