## Voice-over

Without the Moon, would Earth still have tides? Yes, because the Sun makes tides too.

A tide is not just a pull. It is a difference in pull. The Sun pulls on Earth about a hundred and eighty times harder than the Moon does. But it is almost four hundred times farther away, so its pull barely changes from one side of Earth to the other. That makes the stretching it causes only about forty-six percent of the Moon's.

You can already see the Sun's tide. When the Sun and Moon line up, at new and full Moon, their tides add up, and we get bigger ones, called spring tides. At right angles, tides are smaller.

So with no Moon, many coasts would still have two high tides and two low tides a day, driven by the Sun. Smaller, by an amount that depends on the coast.
