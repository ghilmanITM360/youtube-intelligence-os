# Slot 04 · Long · Title and packaging
*Self-review draft. No A/B tests, CTR data or audience data exist for any title below; none is predicted.*

**Original calendar title:** Could One Sideways Jump Put You in Orbit?
**Recommended working title:** Could One Sideways Jump Put You in Orbit Around a Tiny World?

**Three alternative titles**
1. How Small Must a World Be for a Jump to Reach Orbit?
2. Could You Jump Into Orbit Around a Tiny Planet?
3. Orbit Is a Speed, Not a Height: Could a Jump Reach It?

**Viewer's expected payoff (one sentence):** The viewer learns that orbit is sideways speed, how three speeds (grazing, orbital, escape) differ, why the launch direction matters as much as the speed, and why a grazing lap depends only on density.

**Thumbnail concept (text description only; no asset created):** A tiny grey planet filling the lower half of the frame, one footprint-sized figure on its surface, and a dotted loop skimming the surface and returning to the figure. Text (max 3 words): "JUMP INTO ORBIT?". Original line art; no real asteroid imagery.

**Why title and thumbnail work together:** The title names the premise (sideways jump, tiny world); the thumbnail shows the figure, the tiny planet and the closed path. The script defines the toy world in paragraph 3 so "tiny world" is not a surprise.

**Science, ambiguity, overlap and overpromise risks**
- Viewer may think it works on any real asteroid: the script labels the world a model (para 3) and repeats the limits (para 14).
- "Sideways" is ambiguous: the script says exactly horizontal is required (para 9-10).
- 3 m/s jump speed is an assumption, not a sourced value (V04-C07).
- Direct topical overlap with Munroe's "Little Planet" - disclosed; structure and examples are our own.
