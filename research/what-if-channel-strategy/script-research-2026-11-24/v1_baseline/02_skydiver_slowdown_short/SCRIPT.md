## Voice-over

Jump from high enough, and you can slow down without opening a parachute.

Start ten kilometres up. The air is thin, so it barely pushes back, and you speed up. In our simple model you reach about three hundred kilometres an hour, around twenty seconds in.

But the air keeps getting thicker. Soon its push is bigger than your weight, and the extra push slows you down, even though you are still falling.

By the ground, the model has you down to about one hundred and ninety kilometres an hour, the speed of someone who jumped from only one kilometre up.

The slowing takes a bit over two minutes. Real suits and positions change the numbers, and this is a model, not a safety guide.

A higher jump just gives the air more speed to take back.
