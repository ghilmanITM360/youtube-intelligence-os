## Voice-over

Scientists fire a laser at the Moon, and some of its light comes back about two and a half seconds later. That is lunar laser ranging, and it works because astronauts left mirrors there.

Apollo crews placed arrays of small corner mirrors on the surface, each sending light straight back toward its source. The first echo was caught in August 1969, less than two weeks after the first landing.

Light needs about one point three seconds each way, but the Moon's distance changes, so the round trip runs from about two point four to two point seven seconds.

A pulse carries about three hundred quadrillion photons, yet most pulses bring back nothing detectable. In runs at the Apache Point telescope, the average was a fraction of a photon per pulse. So stations fire thousands of pulses and add up the rare returns, pinning the distance down, in the best cases, to about a millimetre.
