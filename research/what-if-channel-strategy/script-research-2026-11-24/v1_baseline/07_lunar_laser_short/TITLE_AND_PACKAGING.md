# Slot 07 · Short · Title and packaging
*Self-review draft. No A/B tests, CTR data or audience data exist for any title below; none is predicted.*

**Original calendar title:** How Long Does a Laser Take to Reach the Moon and Return?
**Recommended working title:** How Long Does a Laser Take to Reach the Moon and Back?

**Three alternative titles**
1. How Long Does Light Take to Go to the Moon and Back?
2. Why Scientists Fire Lasers at the Moon (and What Comes Back)
3. The 2.5-Second Round Trip to the Moon

**Viewer's expected payoff (one sentence):** The viewer learns the round-trip time (about 2.4-2.7 s), why it changes, and the surprising fact that most pulses return nothing so stations add up thousands of shots.

**Thumbnail concept (text description only; no asset created):** Earth at the bottom-left, Moon top-right with a tiny mirror-panel icon on its surface, a thin beam going and returning, and a stopwatch showing "2.5 s". Text (max 3 words): "2.5 SECONDS". Original line art.

**Why title and thumbnail work together:** Title and thumbnail both centre the round-trip time; the first sentence of the script gives it.

**Science, ambiguity, overlap and overpromise risks**
- Must refer specifically to lunar laser ranging with retroreflectors (done, paras 1-2).
- "Most pulses bring back nothing" is from one instrument's runs (APOLLO 0.135-0.25 per shot); the script says "in runs at the Apache Point telescope".
- The "about a millimetre" claim is best-case.
- Closest xkcd video is about aiming many lasers at the Moon (V018) - different angle.

**Competitor / prior-art findings (with coverage limits)**
- xkcd V018 "What if everyone pointed a laser at the moon?" (long, 2024-11-05) and V019/V047/V048 read in full by reader agents; book chapter "Laser Pointer" (pp.27-34, partly read via OCR). They cover pointing many lasers at the Moon and its brightness, not round-trip ranging statistics.
- No registry title on lunar laser ranging. Coverage limit: other creators' LLR videos not surveyed.

*Packaging checks applied (creator-heuristic prompts, not platform facts): the title promises only what the script delivers; the first spoken sentence repeats the promise; the thumbnail text is ≤ 4 words and prints no unverified number.*
