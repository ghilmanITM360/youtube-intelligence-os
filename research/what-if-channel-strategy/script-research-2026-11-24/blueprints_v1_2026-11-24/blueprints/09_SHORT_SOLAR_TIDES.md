## A. IDENTITY
- **Calendar:** Day 25, Short (45–60 s).
- **Current working title:** "Would Earth Still Have Tides Without the Moon?"
- **Issue:** none; good clarity. The independent point must be the Sun's tide, not a repeat of the Long's conclusion.
- **Proposed alternatives:**
  1. "Would the Sun Alone Still Make Tides?" (names the mechanism)
  2. "The Sun Pulls on Earth 179× Harder Than the Moon — So Why Is the Moon Boss of the Tides?" (puzzle title; long, test for clarity)
- **Central question:** Without the Moon, would the oceans still have tides?
- **One-sentence answer (qualified):** Yes — the Sun makes tides too, because tides come from the *difference* in pull across Earth, which falls with distance cubed; the Sun's tide is about 0.46 of the Moon's in forcing and, in measured data at eight US stations, the Sun-only semidiurnal part is 13–35 % of today's biggest — smaller, and on a 12-hour solar clock.
- **Viewer expects:** "no Moon, no tides".
- **Viewer actually learns:** why tides depend on the difference of pull, not the strength; that real Sun-only tides are measured; that the number varies by coast.

## B. PRECISE SCENARIO

- **Normal:** Sun, Earth's rotation and oceans as today; the Sun's measured tide today (S2) is real data.
- **Known vs hypothetical:** S2 amplitudes are measured; the "no Moon" tide is an inference (V09-C04).
