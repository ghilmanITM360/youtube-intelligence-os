## A. IDENTITY
- **Calendar:** Day 22, Long (4–5 min).
- **Current working title:** "What Would Happen to the Ocean If the Moon Vanished?"
- **Issue:** very broad and highly competed title (Claude review: 29 near-premise uploads in 30 days, mostly small); Moon-vanish stories usually drift into climate and tilt claims we cannot support.
- **Proposed alternatives:**
  1. "If the Moon Vanished Tonight, What Would the Ocean Do First?" (Claude review's angle: sequence)
  2. "The Ocean Without the Moon: Would Tides Stop?" (puts the tide question up front; must remain distinct from Short 09)
- **Central question:** If the Moon suddenly disappeared, what would the ocean do — and what would stay?
- **One-sentence answer (qualified):** The ocean would not drain or stop: the Moon's tide would end, the Sun's tide (about 0.46 of the Moon's in forcing; at the eight US stations we checked, the Sun-only semidiurnal part is roughly an eighth to a third of today's biggest, varying by coast) would remain, and high tides would settle onto a 12.00-hour solar clock with no spring–neap cycle.
- **Viewer expects:** a catastrophe (waves, flooding, chaos).
- **Viewer actually learns:** how tides arise; what the Sun alone leaves; why the tide clock and spring/neap cycle change; why no single percentage fits every coast.

## B. PRECISE SCENARIO
- **Changes (ASSUMPTION, magic):** at time t0 the Moon and its gravity are removed, instantly; nothing else changes (Earth's spin, oceans' state, the Sun).
- **Normal:** Newtonian tide-generating forces; the Sun remains.
- **Timeline:** light and gravity changes reach Earth 1.28 s later (the gravity-at-c part is recalled, V08-C01).
- **Not modelled / not claimed:** debris, momentum conservation of the removed mass, real basin resonances, long-term spin or tilt outcomes.

## E. NUMBERS INTO IMAGINABLE SCALE
- 0.46: "the Sun's tide is a bit under half the Moon's" (forcing ratio; not a height).
- S2 vs M2: show real feet values, e.g. Seattle M2 3.49 ft vs S2 0.88 ft (ratio 0.25) — measured fitted amplitudes.
- 12.42 h vs 12.00 h: "high tide slides ~50 minutes later each day; afterwards it would not slide".
- 4,671 km vs 6,371 km: "the barycentre sits ~73 % of the way to Earth's surface" (4,671/6,371 = 0.733).
Qualification: forcing ratios ≠ local heights; show station names.
