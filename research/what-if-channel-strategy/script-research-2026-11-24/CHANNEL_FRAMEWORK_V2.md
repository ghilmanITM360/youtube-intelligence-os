# Channel framework V2 — Unreasonably Exact (evidence-graded, 2026-11-24)

Direction (owner): clear unusual science questions; defined assumptions; serious reasoning; cause-and-effect consequences; understandable scale; original visual storytelling; natural humour and wonder. Cinematic presentation only where it helps comprehension; no single story pattern is forced on every video.

Every item below is placed in exactly one of four categories. A rule in category 2–4 is **not** a proven rule.

## 1. Observed in the reviewed xkcd transcripts (lead's own full reading, 83 files; counts approximate where marked)
Source: `transcript_review/LEAD_TRANSCRIPT_REVIEW.md`, `XKCD_TRANSCRIPT_PATTERNS_AND_EXCEPTIONS.md`.
- 41/43 longs open on a named asker plus the question; 5/40 Shorts keep an asker.
- Hypothetical rules/allowances are stated aloud, the rest is held to real physics (T05, T09, T17, T43).
- Four reasoning shapes: limit ladder, clock ladder, invariant-first, feasibility audit (multiply unit-bearing quantities).
- Numbers are given picturable anchors; catalogues with hidden arithmetic exist but are the weaker pattern.
- Uncertainty is stated in plain words, sometimes with an expert quote (T30, T07, T29, T23, T35, T38).
- Early verdict is common; late answer is also used when the structure is a ladder of candidates (T11, T24).
- Humour is dry and attached to a finished explanation; about two-thirds end on a joke or callback; the rest end on a principle, a limit or a wonder image.
- No subscribe-type CTA in any of the 83; one spoken promo (T19).
- 38/40 Shorts are cuts of a long; seams show where a cut fails to stand alone.

## 2. Advice from project creator resources (creator guidance, not platform fact)
Adopted because it matches category 1 or our rules: title promise = script promise; five-part title check; claim sheet with source/date/confidence; write for the ear, avoid stock openings and AI-sounding phrases; vary sentence length; clarity for a listener who cannot re-read; no visible act labels; explanatory progression "through knowledge rather than invented conflict"; open loops must close; Shorts as standalone pieces; policy-safe framing source → information → complexity.
Source: `playbook_review/`, `RESOURCE_APPLICATION_AND_CONFLICT_MATRIX.csv` (Brain v1 md truth hierarchy and labels adopted as the structure of this document).

## 3. Our channel's own creative decisions
- Calendar order and format fixed (4 Long + 6 Short); titles keep the core question.
- Long band 3–5 minutes (owner); Shorts about one minute as an **editorial choice** (YouTube allows up to 3 minutes for square/vertical, support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877, read 2026-11-24). Real runtime needs recording.
- Every script: premise and assumptions spoken; every number spoken (a Short must work as audio alone); measured, calculated and assumed values labelled; no forced joke, CTA or cliffhanger; no copying of Munroe's wording, jokes, art or sequence.
- Visuals: original diagrams that carry the explanation; labels MEASURED / CALCULATED / MODEL / ASSUMED proposed (owner to confirm); no media stored in the repository.
- Status vocabulary: DRAFT_READY_FOR_OWNER_REVIEW / DRAFT_WITH_OPEN_SCIENCE / CONDITIONAL_DRAFT; claim statuses SOURCE_CHECKED, CALCULATED_SELF_REVIEW, ASSUMPTION, HYPOTHESIS, UNRESOLVED, EXPERT_REVIEW_NEEDED, TIME_SENSITIVE_RECHECK, REMOVED.
- Seven lenses are Claude self-review, never seven independent reviewers.

## How a script is built (applied to all ten)
1. State the question and the premise; name every allowance. 2. Choose the reasoning shape that fits (ladder, clock, invariant-first, audit). 3. Speak each number once with an anchor. 4. Separate measured, calculated, assumed. 5. State the answer when the reasoning reaches it; restate it once. 6. State what is not known. 7. Check: title promise, banned phrases, overlap, fact ledger.
