## Voice-over

A radio signal travels at the speed of light, and it still takes about a day to reach Voyager 1.

Light covers about three hundred thousand kilometres every second. That is a little over a second to the Moon, and about eight minutes to the Sun.

Voyager 1 is much farther. On the eighteenth of November, 2026, NASA said the spacecraft had reached one light-day from Earth, about twenty-six billion kilometres. A light-day is the distance light travels in twenty-four hours.

So a command sent from Earth takes about a day to arrive, and a reply takes about a day to come back. One exchange costs roughly two days.

The exact delay changes a little from day to day, because Voyager keeps moving away while Earth swings around the Sun. But at this distance, it stays close to a day each way.
