## Voice-over

Without the Moon, would Earth still have tides? Yes, because the Sun raises tides too. How big they would be at your coast is something we cannot tell you.

A tide is not made by a pull alone. It is made by a difference in pull between the near and far sides of Earth. The Sun's pull on Earth is enormous, yet the Sun is so distant that the near side and the far side feel almost the same pull, and the leftover difference is small. NASA's figure is a tide-raising force a little under half the Moon's.

You can already see the Sun's part. At new and full Moon, the Sun and Moon act together and we get larger tides, called spring tides. At right angles, the Sun partly cancels the Moon, and tides are smaller.

So even with no Moon, the Sun's push would remain. But that is a statement about a force, not about the water. How much water it moves depends on each ocean basin's shape and depth, so the tide at a given coast could differ a lot from today's.
