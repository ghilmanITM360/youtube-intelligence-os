## Voice-over

Jump from ten kilometres up, without a parachute, and in a simple model you slow down before you land. Here is why.

At ten kilometres the air is thin, so it pushes back only a little. You speed up, and after about twenty seconds you reach roughly three hundred kilometres an hour.

But you are falling into thicker air, and the air's push grows with the square of your speed. Soon it is bigger than your weight. Now it slows you, even though you are still falling.

By the ground, the model has you near one hundred and ninety kilometres an hour. That is about what the same model gives for a fall from only one kilometre. The slowing takes a little over two minutes.

Real suits, body positions and air change these numbers. This is a model of speed, not advice about surviving a fall.

A higher start just gives the air more speed to take back.
