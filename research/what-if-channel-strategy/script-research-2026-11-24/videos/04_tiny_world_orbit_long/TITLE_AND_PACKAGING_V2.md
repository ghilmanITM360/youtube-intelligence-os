# Title and packaging V2 · slot 04

**Recommended title:** Could One Sideways Jump Put You in Orbit Around a Tiny World?
**Alternatives (same question, different wording):**
- Could a Jump Put You in Orbit Around a Tiny World?
- How Small Must a World Be for a Jump to Reach Orbit?

## Five-part title check (Playbook title engine, used as a checklist)
| Part | This video |
|---|---|
| Subject | A person on a very small world |
| Situation | A smooth airless ball, 3 km radius, density 2,000 kg/m³ |
| Trigger | Orbit is a speed, and the speed shrinks with the world |
| Tension | Is a human jump fast enough, and does direction matter? |
| Promise | A speed ladder, the launch-angle catch, the lap time and the limits |

## Promise check
Title asks whether a sideways jump could reach orbit. Paragraphs 5–9 give speed, ladder and catch; paragraph 13 answers: on the idealised world yes, barely; ordinary jump no. PASS (self-check).

## Title rules applied
- One dominant question; no unqualified absolutes ("every", "only", "first", "best", "last", "real reason").
- No manufactured mystery, no fear framing, no invented statistic.
- Title and thumbnail text must not claim more than the script answers. Scorecard numbers are not used: they are not a CTR predictor.

## On-screen honesty labels (proposal, owner to confirm)
- MODEL: toy world, not a real asteroid
- ASSUMED: take-off speed 2–3 m/s

## Description, first two lines (draft)
- Orbit is a path, not a place. How small must a world be for one jump to reach it?
- A toy-world calculation with its assumptions stated; real asteroids differ.

## Thumbnail idea (text description only; no image was made)
A tiny round world with a curved dotted path that skims its surface, and a small figure at the launch point with an arrow labelled 'LEVEL?'. Original line art.

**Prior art disclosure:** Randall Munroe's What If? has an article on a very small planet ('Little Planet'). This script uses its own toy world, numbers and the launch-angle catch; the owner should judge any resemblance.
