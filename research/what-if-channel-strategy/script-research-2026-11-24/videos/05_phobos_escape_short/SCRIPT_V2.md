## Voice-over

Phobos is a lumpy little moon of Mars, about twenty-two kilometres across on average. Could you jump off it and never come back?

If Phobos were a perfect ball alone in space, you would need to leave the surface at about eleven metres per second, roughly forty kilometres an hour.

A very strong jump on Earth leaves the floor at about two to three metres per second. If Phobos gave you the same, the ball model says you would rise, then fall back about a quarter of an hour later.

But Phobos is not a ball. Its lumpy shape, its spin and the pull of Mars all change how fast you would need to go, and they change it from place to place. Reported values differ, and we did not find a source that settles the lowest one. Only a proper model of the real shape could say whether any spot makes a jump enough.

And even if you did leave Phobos, you would still be in orbit around Mars. Leaving Mars would take hundreds of metres per second more.
