# Visual DNA — weird_and_worrying_questions

Global, evidence-supported production characteristics. Scene-specific detail lives in `TIMELINE.csv`; this file states the repeated rule once and notes exceptions.

## CHARACTER DNA
Repeated rule: a single plain bald stick figure (C01) — round head, minimal limbs, no facial features beyond an implied gaze — seated at a desk for the entire video; this is the sole recurring "on-camera" character.
Exceptions: C02 (hat + trenchcoat + magnifying glass) appears once, single-use, for the "mirrors" aside (F01.8) and never recurs. A second, distinct illustrated stick figure (G06, the "imagined self" inside the thought bubble) and a crying figure (G11) are graphic elements, not the on-camera character C01.

## ENVIRONMENT DNA
Repeated rule: flat plain white background (E01) for the entire runtime; no set dressing beyond the desk.
Exceptions: none observed.

## OBJECT DNA
Repeated rule: the desk + monitor + keyboard (O01) is a fixed, static prop present and unmoving in every state.
Exceptions: a physical coffee mug (O02) is held by C01 only in the opening joke (F01.0–F01.5) and is thrown off-panel during F01.6; it never reappears.

## COLOR PALETTE
Repeated rule: pure black line-art on white, matching a hand-drawn/xkcd-style aesthetic; the only non-monochrome element is the red mercury column inside the thermometer (G03).
Exceptions: none.

## LIGHTING
Repeated rule: flat, shadowless line-art; no lighting model present.

## CAMERA / FRAMING
Repeated rule: **one fixed, locked camera position for the entire video** — no cuts, pans, zooms, or reframing were observed anywhere. This is the video's defining structural trait and is why the whole runtime is modeled as a single shot (S01). All apparent "cuts" from scene-cut detection are large content swaps within this one static frame (thought-bubble graphics appearing/clearing, a second character entering/exiting), not camera changes.
Exceptions: none.

## COMPOSITION
Repeated rule: C01 + desk occupy the lower-right area of frame; a gray question card (T01), when present, sits top-left; the thought bubble (G01), when present, occupies the upper-center/upper-left area above C01.
Exceptions: the two "plaque" states (F01.21–F01.22) replace the Q-card + bubble layout with a single full-frame graphic (G10); the closing caption states (F01.25–F01.26) show no bubble or Q-card at all, only a short caption line above C01.

## TYPOGRAPHY
Repeated rule: two distinct text systems are used consistently — T01 (a gray rounded-rectangle "Q" card with sentence-case question body text and a "— Name" attribution line, used for all five named questions) and T02 (bold, all-caps, hand-drawn-style caption text with occasional underline emphasis, used for asides/punchlines/reactions that are not attributed questions).
Exceptions: none.

## GRAPHICS
Repeated rule: every gag illustration (teeth, thermometer, mirror/star array, volcano, reactor, plaque, crying figure) is a bespoke single-use graphic drawn inside or replacing the thought bubble; none are reused across different jokes.
Exceptions: the thought-bubble container outline itself (G01) is the one recurring graphic, reused as a frame for five of the six gags.

## ILLUSTRATION / 3D STYLE
Repeated rule: loose hand-drawn black-ink vector/sketch style, consistent with the same illustration language used elsewhere in this project (matches `what_if_the_moon_were_made_of_protons` and `how_long_could_you_drive_towards_the_sunset`).

## DEPTH
Repeated rule: flat 2D compositing throughout; no foreground/midground/background layering beyond the character+desk vs. bubble-content plane.

## MOTION LANGUAGE
Repeated rule: nearly every state transition is a **content swap** (a graphic or caption appears, fully formed, or clears to blank) rather than a continuous physical animation; genuine continuous motion is limited to two cases — the thermometer's mercury drop (A03) and the crying figure's growing puddle (A23). No camera movement, parallax, particles, or decorative motion of any kind was observed anywhere.
Exceptions: the mug-throw (A06) is the one instance of an object undergoing a translating motion (off-panel).

## TRANSITION LANGUAGE
Repeated rule: all transitions between visual states are hard content cuts/fades within the single locked frame (bubble/Q-card content appears or clears); there are no wipes, dissolves, or camera-driven transitions.

## ASSET REUSE PATTERN
Repeated rule: this video has the lowest reuse profile of any video in this project — of 19 total assets, only C01, E01, O01, G01, T01, T02 and AU01 (7 of 19) recur across multiple states; the remaining 12 assets (C02, O02, G02–G11 minus G01) are single-use, one per gag, reflecting the video's fast joke-per-question structure (six independent question/answer gags in ~47 seconds).
