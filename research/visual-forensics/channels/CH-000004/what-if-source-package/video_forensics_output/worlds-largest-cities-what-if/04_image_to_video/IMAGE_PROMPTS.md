## IMG02
- USED BY SHOTS/STATES: S02, all states F02.0–F02.8 (every city pin and every camera position in the second half of the video is a crop/overlay pass on this one image)
- SOURCE STATE: N/A (first appearance of the map)
- REUSE RELATIONSHIP: REUSE IMAGE IMG02 for the entire back half of the video — do not regenerate the map per shot. Because I2V tools and most image generators can't produce one giant pannable map in a single pass at usable resolution, generate IMG02 as a tall/wide master plate at the highest resolution your tool supports, then crop windows from it for each state (same principle as the CapCut build).
- TEXT BAKED: NO for city names/dots (added in final assembly per state, since exact spelling/position must be reliable) — YES for continent names (NORTH AMERICA, ASIA, EUROPE, AFRICA, AUSTRALIA, SOUTH AMERICA) if your generator can render legible hand-lettering reliably; otherwise generate a clean plate and add continent labels in final assembly too.
- STANDALONE IMAGE PROMPT:
"Minimalist hand-drawn doodle map illustration of a fictional world, ultra-wide/tall master plate (generate at the largest aspect ratio and resolution your tool allows, e.g. 1:3 or taller, so it can later be cropped into a vertical 9:16 panning sequence). Flat 2D style, black ink outlines, flat color fills, no gradients, no shading, no photographic texture. Ocean rendered as a solid teal band along the top/bottom edges. Land masses rendered with three internal color zones by latitude band: a pale yellow arid/desert zone, a green vegetated/temperate zone, and patches of light cyan for inland water, flooded lowland, or ice — the bands run roughly horizontally across the whole image, cutting across continent shapes rather than following their natural real-world climate. Continents are recognizable but reflowed/rotated relative to normal world-map orientation: North America positioned low/center, Asia/Europe/Africa forming one large connected landmass across the upper-middle, Australia and Indonesian-style islands lower-left, South America lower-right. Thin gray horizontal reference lines cross the whole image at regular intervals (some solid, some dashed), suggesting latitude/parallel lines. Black hand-drawn triangular hachure marks indicate mountain ranges along several coastlines. No characters, no vehicles, no other imagery — map only."
