## 7. Attention hypotheses (INFERRED, no retention or CTR data)
1. A new visual state appears every 1.14 s on average and a hard cut every 2.5 s, so there is rarely a long unchanged picture (longest single shot 14.96 s, and that shot keeps adding pop-ins); narration of about 188 words per minute (1,633 ASR words) is matched by on-screen pop-ins, which may make a numeric argument (20%, 60%, 75%, 2.5%) easier to follow.
2. In 134 of 141 selected phrase pairs the picture changes before the spoken word (median lead 0.39 s); a visual that is already on screen when the phrase lands may reinforce the claim, but the pair selection is mine and the lead may partly reflect ASR timing.
3. Board scenes work as section openers: 12 of the 15 boards (11 distinct texts, POSITIONING twice) carry a question or one-word frame (What's going on?, WHO?, POSITIONING, Who is in danger then?, Who is affected?, PROBLEMS, OPPORTUNITY, Long-term?, So.... what now?, LEVERAGE, Geopolitics?) that previews the next part; the other three show a statement (Every day... then 20% of all oil!, Some benefit!) or the ABACUS.AI logo.
4. The sponsor is entered through a topic bridge (bottlenecks exist in technology too) and the yellow bar starts 14 s before the sponsor line (Which brings us to the sponsor at 305.5 s); the bar may signal the end of the sponsor segment to the viewer, and its last frame (f8530) lands 0.1 s before the narration returns to oil.
5. Repeated templates (six-barrel fade, dollar-and-barrel zigzag, counted-flag build) let one drawn asset carry several price and supply points; the second and later uses are cheaper to produce and recognisable to a returning viewer.
