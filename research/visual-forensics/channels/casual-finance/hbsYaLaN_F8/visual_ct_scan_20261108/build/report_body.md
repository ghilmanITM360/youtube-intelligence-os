## 7. Attention hypotheses (INFERRED, no retention or CTR data)
1. A new visual state appears every 2.5 s on average and a hard cut every 3.5 s; 216 of 257 change episodes are single frames, so the viewer follows the next card or sentence, not motion. ASR counts 1,524 words over 554 s (about 165 words per minute).
2. In 162 of 202 selected phrase pairs the picture changes before the spoken word (median lead 0.19 s); a visual already on screen when the phrase lands may reinforce it, but the pair selection is mine and the lead may partly reflect ASR timing.
3. Chapter cards sit in ASR gaps: no word is found for f855-f1013 (5.3 s); f2458-f2622 (5.5 s); f5323-f5480 (5.2 s); f7833-f7942 (3.7 s); f14088-f14262 (5.8 s), which covers all three chapter-card intervals and the TV-clip stretches; whether those gaps are silence, music or clip audio is UNAVAILABLE because audio was not heard, but the pattern may mark chapter breaks as pauses.
4. Text cards (59 states) work as punchlines, as pace breaks and as list builders; cards repeat the same lines (I Hate Crypto., So yeah...) as callbacks.
5. Recurring set pieces (casino, closed bank, ship and iceberg, report card, Luna and Terra, FTX logo) may help the viewer link claims across chapters.
6. The video opens on a single word card and ends on a desert mirage metaphor, a card list and an end card, which may end on a summarising image and a subscribe call.
