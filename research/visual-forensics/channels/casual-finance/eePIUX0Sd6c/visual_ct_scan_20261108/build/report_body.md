## 4. Motion and animation (MEASURED + OBSERVED)
- Activity episodes: 276 (149 single-frame, 127 multi-frame, 47 of at least 20 frames, longest 354 frames). The longer episodes are continuous animation or camera drift, unlike the previous long-forms in this campaign where nearly all changes were single-frame steps.
- 24 fps cadence (MEASURED): in five continuous-motion runs (f1559-f1688 tunnel walk, f7595-f7707 dragon vault, f8241-f8398 push-in on five figures, f10931-f11078 blindfolded figures, f11492-f11575 collapsing city) unchanged frames recur every fifth frame (gap of 5 frames: 24 of 25, 20 of 21, 27 of 28, 30 of 31 and 13 of 14 intervals; the exceptions are one 4-frame or 10-frame gap at a run edge). INFERRED: the animation is authored at 24 fps and conformed to the 30 fps timeline by repeating every fifth frame; the authoring tool is UNAVAILABLE.
- Slow push-in (MEASURED): ORB feature matching with a similarity transform (RANSAC) between frame start+4 and end-3 of each state of at least 25 frames with in-state activity gives a net scale >= 1.004 in 56 of 94 measured states, 33 flat (1.000 +- 0.004, mostly screen recordings and monitor sketches) and 5 inconclusive (< 150 inliers). Median 0.79 % per second, interquartile range 0.64-0.97, extremes 0.25 and 4.39 (the 19-frame f610 text swap), plus the tunnel walk (6.1 % per second, f1557, which also scrolls the background). Examples: cave scene f1200 to f1330 scale 1.0366 (tx -34.8, ty -19.9); mountain scene f1360 to f1475 1.0374. Held cards and text cards zoom too (f394 1.015 over 28 frames). The curve shape inside each shot is UNAVAILABLE (only net scale was measured).

## 7. Attention hypotheses (INFERRED, no retention or CTR data)
- Constant visual novelty: a cut every 3.2 s on median plus a new pop-in, swap or pose roughly every 3 s inside longer shots keeps something changing; the slow push-in keeps held illustrations from looking frozen.
- Jokes and callbacks: gold bar, iceberg and fork-in-road motifs recur so that viewers can read the topic from the picture before the narration lands (the pairing statistics below show pictures usually arrive slightly before the key word).
- The sponsor block reuses the main cast and gags (weightlifter, paper pile, meme) so it reads as part of the video; the return to the topic is marked by a boardroom shot (f4222).
- The video opens and closes on the same chalkboard host and gold chart, and the end card holds 5.0 s.

## 8. Uncertainties and limits
- Audio: ASR only (Whisper small.en); nobody listened; AV sign-off is BLOCKED. Words named in ASR (for example people or brands) are spoken-text hints, not identifications of the pictured people.
- Phrase pairs: 142 pairs were chosen by hand from states with a nameable element and with a word within about 1.5 s; states without a nameable element were not paired and no unpaired count was logged, so the lag statistics are selection-biased and truncated near +-1.5 s. Compare with the random-time control in the generated section.
- Video ID attribution is the roster/queue attribution (filename carries the ID); live YouTube was not re-checked.
- Small screenshot, table, forum and legend text is not transcribed; photographs of real people are not identified; the Labubu listing price digits are not legible at the decoded size.
- Zoom: only net scale between two frames per state was measured with ORB features (five states inconclusive); easing inside each shot is UNAVAILABLE. The 24 fps statement is a measured repeat cadence, not proof of the authoring tool.
- Section boundaries and the sponsor-block extent are INFERRED from visuals and ASR; quiet frames inside holds were measured unchanged (thr 0.06 % / 300 px) but not individually viewed.
