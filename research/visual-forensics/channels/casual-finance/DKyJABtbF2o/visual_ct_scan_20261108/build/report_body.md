## 7. Attention hypotheses (INFERRED, no retention or CTR data)
1. A new visual state appears every 2.9 s on average and a hard cut every 5.0 s; 201 of 218 change episodes are single frames, so the viewer is never asked to follow motion, only to read the next object or sentence. Narration is about 153 words per minute (1,329 ASR words over 522 s; Xkk9GnnJHBg, measured earlier in this campaign, ran about 188), so the pace may suit a number-heavy argument.
2. In 107 of 122 selected phrase pairs the picture changes before the spoken word (median lead 0.30 s); a visual already on screen when the phrase lands may reinforce it, but the pair selection is mine and the lead may partly reflect ASR timing.
3. Four titled chapters (f2892-f5809 (97.3 s), f5810-f8140 (77.7 s), f8141-f10667 (84.2 s), f10668-f15682 (167.2 s)) give the viewer orientation; the longest chapter (Chapter 4) is also the most graphic, with 17 of the video's 22 logo states and 13 of its 31 chart states.
4. The video opens and closes on the same Templeton quote scene (f539 and f15526) and uses five distinct quotes in six quote scenes, so repetition may frame the thesis and make the closing line feel earned.
5. Parallel assets (two bubbles, two Earnings Multiples charts, pie and concentration charts, Nifty Fifty logos beside Mag 7 logos) let the viewer compare eras by recognition rather than explanation.
6. One-line text cards (24 states) and comedic scene gags (cliff jump, grenade, Etch A Sketch, baby in a crib) act as punchlines between data beats.
