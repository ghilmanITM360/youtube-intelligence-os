# 04 — Art direction, fine art, editorial, illustration, graphics, color and symbolism

## Systematic per-state design audit

**Visual medium & asset origin**: live footage, flat vector, ink drawing, photo collage, 3D-inspired rendering, rendered 3D, AI-like imagery, screenshot/UI capture, simulation/infographic. State visible features and source uncertainties. Exact artist, agency, AI platform, asset supplier, font or production software cannot be certified by pixels alone.

**Composition**: primary/secondary/tertiary hierarchy; subject coordinates and approximate frame coverage; center/thirds/symmetry/diagonals/grids; negative-space fraction; foreground/midground/background depth; layering/occlusion; balance/density; focal isolation; paths from face/text to object; continuity across adjacent states. Don't force golden ratio, S-curves, triangles, or compositional theory if unsupported.

**Color science/design**: actual sampled palettes if pixel samples available, not invented hex; dominant, secondary, accents, text backgrounds; luminance hierarchy, contrast, saturation, temperature; warm/cool separation, harmony schemes only if supported; color coded concepts/characters/risk; color motif across shots; possible emotion association explicitly interpretive, with alternatives and cultural/context caveats.

**Illustration/character**: line weights, stroke uniformity, line endings, silhouette legibility, proportion/exaggeration, facial-feature geometry, icon design, scale logic, simplification, character wardrobe/surface/texture consistency, visual humor, metaphor, symbol use, character/prop asset reuse. Compare reference states to detect accidental morphing and unexplained redesign.

**Space and environment**: recurring backgrounds, negative space, prop staging, environmental storytelling, realistic vs abstraction, texture, shadows, ambient occlusion-like cues, highlights/specularity, realistic vs graphic depth, lighting sources, focal separation and continuity. Record gradients/shadows/glow/particle layers only as observed pixels.

**Typography/editorial**: literal text, hierarchy, approximate size, weight, case, alignment, line breaks, tracking impression, placement, contrast, accessibility at 320px; text fill/stroke/shadow, reveal/replacement/hold timing, chart labels/units, punctuation and disclaimer readability. Distinguish actual typography from generated-looking text and report errors literally. Estimate category (geometric sans/condensed display), never invent exact typeface.

**Information design**: clarity of diagrams/infographics, axes/scales/truncation, ratio accuracy, number continuity, labels/units, legibility and what visuals add beyond spoken words. A chart must not make unsupported numerical comparisons seem measured.

**Fine-art/editorial influences**: assess repetition/rhythm, symbolism/metaphor, scale, abstraction and visual narrative as possible parallels. Names used by the user's prompts (Yayoi Kusama, Richter, Koons, Ai Weiwei, Banksy, Hockney, Murakami, Ngai, Niemann, Jullien, Shaun Tan, Mary Blair, etc.) are **inspiration lenses** for comparison, not proven influences. Explain concrete shared formal attributes and alternatives. Do not imply original artist involvement or copy unique creator trade dress.

## Per-scene questions

1. What exactly is visible? 2. Which elements are stable vs changing? 3. Why might this arrangement communicate the idea efficiently? 4. Is the visual an evidence demonstration, literal metaphor, comedic exaggeration, orienting diagram or decoration? 5. What is the effect of removing it? 6. Which design principle can be reused in ORIGINAL form? 7. Which limitation prevents stronger attribution?

Deliver design-token table: palettes with method, approximate line widths/text-size scale, recurring shapes/background layers/asset IDs, spacing conventions, data-viz grammar, continuity exceptions. Do not invent tokens that haven't been empirically observed.
