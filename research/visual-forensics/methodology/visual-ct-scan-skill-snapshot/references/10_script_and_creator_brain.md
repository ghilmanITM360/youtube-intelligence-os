# 10 — Script architecture, editorial psychology and creator decision model

## Separate three jobs

1. **Verbatim source**: what the creator actually said (ASR fidelity/time limitations disclosed).
2. **Narrative architecture**: what purpose the segment seems to serve (with source phrases and time ranges).
3. **Original editorial guidance**: what could improve usefulness/clarity, not a disguised copy of source wording.

## Script forensic panel

- Literal hook / audience problem / promise and promise specificity.
- Sequence of open questions and subsequent answers; setup → BUT → THEREFORE, not just AND THEN.
- Quantitative claim and sourced evidence; original dollars/date/year/units.
- Emotional stakes vs actual support; contrast, escalation, reversal, payoff, practical steps.
- Sentence/phrase-to-visual matching, graphic evidence strength, timing.
- Repeated characters/props/motifs as anchors, callbacks, setup-payoff memory.
- Good objections/counterexamples, hidden assumption, title contradiction, moralization/overstatement risk.
- CTA/bridge and whether viewer gets promised answer.

## Comparative consensus

If multiple script evaluators exist, record their evidence separately and adjudicate contradictions against source text. A simulated "panel of experts" is brainstorming, not independent empirical validation. Avoid fabricated consensus or invented reviewer names and scores.

## Finance/health care guardrails

Facts and math must be independently reproduced; compare nominal vs real, median vs mean, denominators, non-overlapping cash flows, qualified medical claims, clinical approvals. Graphical dollar figures must match audited calculation ledger. Narration-to-graphic mismatches are factual as well as editorial errors.
