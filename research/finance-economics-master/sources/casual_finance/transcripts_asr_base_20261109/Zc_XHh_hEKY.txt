Two economists saw this danger coming in 1981. Thomas Sargent and Neil Wallace predicted it in one of the most influential macroeconomic papers of the last 50 years. They warned that if the government never runs a surplus, the central bank can't make the debt smaller. All it gets to decide is what form the debt takes. Bonds or cash, those are the two options. So when the Fed fights inflation, it's squeezing the cash side, which means the bond side has to grow to make up the difference. And bonds pay interest. So the longer the Fed holds the line, the faster that pile of debt compounds. So the tightening that brings inflation down today is the same thing that forces somebody to print later, meaning the Fed isn't actually addressing the root of the problem. They're not actually fighting the inflation, they're just postponing it. And if you think this is just some doomer theory from 1981 with no bearing on today, well, guess what? A few weeks ago, the new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh launched five new task forces to tackle different economic problems. One of them is an inflation frameworks task force to reassess how the Fed measures, understands, and responds to structural drivers of inflation. Three people were picked to lead this team. One of them is Thomas Sargent. The man who co-wrote the paper from 45 years ago is now being asked by the Fed to lead a team tackling the inflation issues.
