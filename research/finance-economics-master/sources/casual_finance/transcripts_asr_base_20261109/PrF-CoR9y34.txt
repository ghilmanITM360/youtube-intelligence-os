They introduced something called yield curve control, which sounds like a Marvel villain, but actually just means we're gonna cap long-term bond yields, whether the market likes it or not. Basically, the Japanese government superglue interest rates to the floor, so for decades Japanese government bonds yielded pretty much nothing, and that created massive problems for Japanese investors. Because imagine you're a Japanese life insurer, pension fund, or even a bank. You have a billion dollar investment portfolio, and your domestic government bonds are paying you 0%. This pretty much leaves you with two choices. One, accept no return on your cash, or two, go shopping overseas. And they chose option two. So Japan entered a multi-decade long global shopping spree that led them to become the largest foreign holder of US treasuries, one of the largest buyers of US mortgage-backed securities, and the world's largest net creditor nation, which is just the formal way of saying Japan became the world's ATM.
