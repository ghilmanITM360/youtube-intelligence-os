How are we actually paying for this sh**? The answer to that starts with 1 number, 1.65 trillion dollars. That's what one study found just five tech companies owe and obligations that never appear on their balance sheets. Moving that much money out of sight isn't a trick you can invent overnight. It takes a system, one with a name, and it's a name Wall Street has spent the last two decades trying very hard to forget. It's institutions that borrow like banks and lend like banks, but aren't banks. So no deposit insurance, no fed safety net, and none of the regulation written for banks. Meanwhile the same five companies report about 1.35 trillion dollars of debt on the books. So the hidden pile of debt is not only bigger than the visible one. It's much bigger. Take meta for example. Their off balance sheet debt is nearly triple the reported debt. In 2007 the shadow banking system was built on piles of mortgages. Today it's data centers. The AI build out runs on financing machinery from the same family that broke the world in 2008. It's gotten so out of control that the International Monetary Fund is now flagging it. Tobias Adrian, a senior official at the IMF and former senior vice president at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, laid it out simply. What is quite worrisome from a financial stability perspective is that the major tech firms are starting to leverage up themselves.
