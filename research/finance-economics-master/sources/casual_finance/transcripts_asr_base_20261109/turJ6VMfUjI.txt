Well, neither somebody chose it. So why the 2% if inflation's the thing quietly making everything more expensive, why aim for any of it at all? Because while on paper a 0% inflation rate sounds great. In reality, it's not. When a recession hits, the feds may move as to cut interest rates. In a world with 0 inflation, rates already sit near the floor. So the second a real economic downturn arrives, the fed gets one cut, maybe two, and then slams into zero, structurally trapped and out of road. That's how an ordinary recession can spiral into a decade-long depression. But you can't aim too high either. Push it to 4% and prices double every 18 years. Businesses can't forecast their cost, and nobody can price a long-term contract without guessing. So 2% is that Goldilocks number, high enough to keep a cushion between the economy and catastrophe. Low enough that you barely notice it on an ordinary Tuesday.
