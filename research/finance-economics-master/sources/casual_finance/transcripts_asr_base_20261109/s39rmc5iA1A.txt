It's like opening a credit card in your cousin's name. You're the one swiping it. You're the one enjoying everything it buys. But when the bank asks about your debts, your answer is technically zero. Let's say we have a company called the Acme Corporation. They want to borrow a mountain of money, but they don't want the debt showing up on their own books. The solution is Acme creates a second company called a Special Purpose Vehicle or Special Purpose Entity. On paper, Acme and the SPV are completely separate. They have their own names, own bank accounts, and most importantly, their own debts. The SPV takes on a bunch of debt and uses that borrowed money to go out and buy the assets Acme needs. Then the SPV leases the assets back to Acme for them to use. The SPV borrows the money and owns the asset. Then the original company gets to use it without having the debt on their books. The main difference between 2008 and today is that the shadow banking system today is hiding in plain sight. Everyone's missing it because of where it lives. It's buried in the footnotes of public filings and the only people who read footnotes are finance geeks.
