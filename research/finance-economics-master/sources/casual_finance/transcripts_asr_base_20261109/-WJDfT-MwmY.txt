For every dollar in, about a dollar 40 went out. Over the first 10 months of this fiscal year, the government collected $4.5 trillion and spent 6.3. That puts the deficit at nearly 6% of GDP. The 50-year average is 3.8, and it's only getting worse. The Congressional Budget Office has it widening to 6.7% by 2036. And America's debt and interest payments are chained together. That's where the real fiscal problem for America lies. When borrowing costs go up, the interest bill goes up, which widens the deficit, which means we have to borrow more debt, which grows the pile, which is one more reason to charge you more next time. It's a circular feedback loop that feeds itself. That's the actual problem, not the pile itself, but the self-reinforcing loop. To hold the line, the government would have to run a primary surplus and collect more than it spends on everything except interest. So the question was never whether America can outgrow its debt. It's whether America can outgrow its deficit. Something America has managed 12 times in the last 50 years.
