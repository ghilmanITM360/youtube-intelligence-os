# COMMENT_SAMPLE_FINDINGS (2026-11-23)
**Method:** scripts/sample_comments.py via yt-dlp (android_vr client; the default clients fail with "page needs to be reloaded"), public unauthenticated, YouTube "top" sort, up to 150 top-level comments per video, no replies. 13 videos, 1,814 comments (raw/comments_sample.jsonl, local only). **No author names, ids or URLs stored.** Text is paraphrased below, not quoted at length. Coding counts (COMMENT_SAMPLE_CODING.csv) are keyword heuristics; the themes were found by reading the top-liked and request-like comments, not all 1,814.

**Videos:** 4 xkcd What If longs (Aony69nO5kI, alkJ7xScBiw, 9VG0yqq_PHM, 4ZVtHknKuvg), 3 xkcd Shorts (TIDHee_Q5Gk, 35KZm1yiyIE, alBQCm0QpJg), 2 Voyager one-light-day videos (GLpny4lTmvg, J1JKp4OqZJ4), and one video each for tiny-body jumping (n_HGTIAyxg0), lunar reflector (dsRsap2_RAc), Earth's shadow (qA4umk8HGMY, only 14 comments), neutron stars (udFxKZRyQt4).

## Observed themes [OBS, this sample only]
1. **The most-liked comments on xkcd What If are jokes about the series' own pattern** ("everyone dies", "what if we tried MORE", mass-extinction expectation, wolves, body horror). The audience is attached to a known brand voice, which a new channel cannot inherit; copying the "everyone dies" trope would imitate Munroe rather than add an original voice.
2. **Viewers propose their own follow-up hypotheticals** under xkcd videos (e.g. toaster in a bathtub, all dirt vanishing, nitrogen replaced by helium, the inverse "all ice melts" scenario, tropical-forest water cycle, DNA swapped instead of removed, sneezing on a small moon). These are raw question seeds, each from one commenter, with 0–7 likes: ideas, not demand measurements.
3. **Science scrutiny is visible:** viewers add technical corrections or extensions (fluid behaviour of the pool question, heat-dump physics of a fridge, whether sound exists in space, a claim that a beam "would burn through the Earth"). In the jump-comparison video a viewer says the motion looks inaccurate; in a Voyager video a viewer asks why sources are not cited. Supports the plan's rule to show assumptions and sources on screen.
4. **Tiny-body jumping (WIB-17 premise):** the top comments on the planet-jump video (521 and 223 likes) are about Phobos where one good jump reaches escape velocity. That is observed interest in the exact "small world, jump into orbit" idea behind WIB-17's tiny-planet premise, in one video. Same sample also shows the premise already circulates, so originality must come from the calculation, not the idea.
5. **Voyager one-light-day:** comments are emotional/commemorative (launch-era memories, "safe travels") and jokes; one viewer notes that 1 light-day is a tiny fraction of the distance to the nearest star. Few questions; no request for a follow-up explainer in the sample.
6. **Lunar reflector video:** top comments are jokes unrelated to the physics (laser/cat, a cross-channel loop), so little topic signal. **Neutron stars:** top comments are jokes about "nuclear pasta" and a sponsor line; no substantive questions in the top 4. **Earth's shadow:** only 14 comments; two viewers ask to reuse the clip for a flat-earth debunk — no demand signal.

## What this can and cannot support
- CAN: language and humour viewers use about the genre; question seeds; evidence that corrections appear; the xkcd audience's brand attachment.
- CANNOT: how many viewers want any candidate topic, CTR, retention, or what our channel's audience would say. Top-sort favours jokes and early comments; 150 per video is a small slice; only large channels were sampled; comments by non-viewers or bots not filtered.
- **Decision impact:** no decision or score changed. Mild qualitative support for keeping WIB-17 early; supports "no trope copying" under originality (L-rule) and the on-screen source requirement. Confidence levels unchanged.
- Privacy: comment authors are private individuals; keep raw/comments_sample.jsonl local, do not publish it or quote comments with identifying context.
