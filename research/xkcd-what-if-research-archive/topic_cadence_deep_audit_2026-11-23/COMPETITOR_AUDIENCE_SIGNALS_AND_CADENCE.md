## 2. What this can and cannot tell us
- NEW (resume crawl): SciShow 20 longs / 30 d; Veritasium 2 / 8 in 30 d and 6 / 18 in 90 d; Kurzgesagt 2 / 4 and 7 / 13 — big brands run ~1 long per 1–2 weeks, while the tiny What-If channels post a long every 1–2 days (WhatIfAnimations, AumSum's What-If). Rhythm differs by channel size and format, which again argues that cadence is not evidence of appetite.
- CAN: public rhythm of comparable channels; that several tiny channels post daily; that the best-known *brand* paces itself at ~1 long/3 weeks + weekly Short.
- CANNOT: how many videos viewers want; that cadence causes views (Lightbound posts daily with 17K median vs xkcd 1/3 weeks with 1.3M median — channel size, brand, packaging, topic, library and team all differ); anything about CTR/retention/returning viewers (private).
- Alternative explanations present in the data: huge subscriber differences, existing brand audience, promotion (xkcd site/book), production scale (unknown), topic mix.
- Comments: sampled 2026-11-23 (13 videos, 1,814 top-sorted top-level comments) — see COMMENT_SAMPLE_FINDINGS.md. Small, like-biased, large channels only; not demand evidence.
- **New saturation observation (titles only, YouTube search "this month/week" filters 2026-11-23):** many micro-channels publish near-identical titles for the same What-If premises (e.g. ≥10 variants of "What If Light Only Traveled 100 km/h?", ≥10 of "What If the Moon Vanished Tonight?", ≥8 of "all rain fell as one giant drop"). This is observed supply of low-subscriber channels; video quality not inspected.

## 3. Cadence scenarios (workload = ASSUMPTION, not measurement)
Assumptions: one primary creator + AI assistance; ~15–30 h per researched long (science check, original art, edit), ~3–6 h per Short, ~1–3 h/week overhead. No actual capacity data exists → these are placeholders to be replaced by the owner's real hours.
| | Longs | Shorts | Est. hours/week | Pros | Costs/risks | Learning value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A Conservative | 1 per 14 d (≈4 per 60 d) | ≤1 per long | ~8–17 | quality, science review time | slow data, ~4 long observations in 60 d | low n, but each carefully checked |
| **B Balanced (provisional pick)** | **1 per ~10 d (6 per 60 d)** | **~1/week (8 per 60 d: 4 standalone + 4 excerpts)** | **~13–26** | matches xkcd's own rhythm (long/3 wk + weekly Short) scaled up modestly; gives 72h and 7-day reads between launches | still demanding for a solo creator | enough variety to compare 3–4 families loosely |
| C Aggressive (original plan) | 10 per 30 d | 13 per 30 d | ~50–100+ | max data | ≈5× xkcd's long pace; science errors, burnout; Day 1 already missed | many tiny-n cells, little power |
**Provisional start: B, conditional on real capacity.** Fallback if capacity is under ~15 h/week: A (1 long/14 d + up to 1 Short per long) and drop excerpts first. Evidence for B: xkcd rhythm [OBS]; official guidance that no fixed schedule is required [REPORTED, 09-24 audit]. Unknown: actual hours, whether the owner can sustain science review lead time.
