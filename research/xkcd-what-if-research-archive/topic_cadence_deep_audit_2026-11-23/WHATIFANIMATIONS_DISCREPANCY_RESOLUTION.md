# WhatIfAnimations "433K" vs 3.82K: resolution (2026-11-24)

**Result: the channel has about 3.8K subscribers. "433K" is not its subscriber count.** The origin of the 433K figure is not proven; one candidate is below, labelled as inference.

## Evidence (OBSERVED unless marked)
- Channel id UCESitBa9MCU0T8h6moQe6SQ, name "WhatIfAnimations". Subscribers: **3.82K** in the search result and the yt-dlp listing (2026-11-23) and **3.83K** on the live channel page fetched 2026-11-24. Three reads, same order of magnitude.
- Channel page total views (2026-11-24): **820,892**. Sum of lifetime views of the 32 uploads in the audit inventory (2026-11-23): 825,300. These agree closely, so 433K is also not the channel's total views.
- No channel in the audit's channel set has a subscriber count near 433K (checked all of CHANNEL_SELECTION_AND_COMPARABILITY.csv). So it is not a name mix-up with another channel in the set.
- The original plan (WHATIF_TOP_VIDEOS_PLAN_2026-11-23.pdf, NEW-08 slot) says only "Crowded: WhatIfAnimations 433K" with no source, no date and no unit.
- The channel's top video, "What If Light Kept Getting Slower and Couldn't Stop?" (TAsqnXcCqRI, published 2026-10-18), had 463,676 views in the 2026-11-23 inventory and 464,308 on 2026-11-24. A "433K" reading is **plausibly** that video's view count at an earlier time, mislabelled as subscribers. **INFERENCE, not verifiable**: I have no earlier snapshot of its views, and a count growing from 433K to 464K would need roughly 31K views, not shown by any data here.

## Earlier explanations withdrawn
The first write-up offered "a different channel or a personalised-browser artefact". The different-channel explanation is ruled out above; no evidence supports the artefact idea. Withdrawn.

## What this changes
- Corrected facts: WhatIfAnimations is a ~3.8K-subscriber channel with one very large outlier (464K views, about 56% of the channel's 821K total views).
- **NEW-08 note** ("What If Light Were as Slow as a Car?", calendar slot 11): the "crowded" warning stays valid but for a different reason than a big channel. A small channel got 464K views on a close premise (light getting slower), and its own second light video has about 64.8K views. This is OBSERVED prior art with real demand evidence for the premise family. It was not in the topic search evidence (0 rows), so it is added here as a finding, not re-scored.
- No score, order, cadence or decision was changed. Because the premise now has observed prior art with high views, the existing instruction for NEW-08 stands and is stronger: it needs a clearly distinct question (the car-speed road scene) and the Gamow/MIT prior-art mention.
