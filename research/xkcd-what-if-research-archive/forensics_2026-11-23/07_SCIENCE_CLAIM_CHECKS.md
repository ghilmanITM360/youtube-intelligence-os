## Sources fetched this session (web search summaries and page reads; same-model; not an expert review)
- Lightning flash energy: [National Geographic, quoting NOAA's MacGorman: 100 to 30,000 MJ per discharge, typical 1,000 to 5,000 MJ; the article gives 280 to 1,390 kWh and about 9 days to 1.5 months of household use](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/120308-energy-in-forces-of-nature) (page loaded, read by a subagent; it does not define a ground-delivered share). The arXiv paper 2101.12361 loaded but its text could not be extracted, so it is **not used** as a source. A search summary of Cooray (1997) gives about 9.5e8 J dissipated in a four-stroke ground flash, and a range of 2e8 to 7e9 J per flash (not read in full).
- Original article: [What If #16 "Lightning"](https://what-if.xkcd.com/16/) loaded and contains **no energy figure** (only currents and timing), so it is not the video's source for SC03.
- Household electricity: [EIA FAQ 97, loaded: 2022 average residential customer 10,791 kWh/yr = 899 kWh/month](https://www.eia.gov/tools/faqs/faq.php?id=97&t=3). The v1 value "865 kWh per month in 2024" was **not reproduced** on that page and is dropped. US total: EIA 2023 sales 3,861 TWh (MER/STEO; 3,874 in EIA-861), search summary.
- Hiroshima yield: about 15 kt TNT = 63 TJ (Wikipedia Little Boy; OSTI report gives 15 kt best estimate, range 6-23 kt quoted).
- Earthquake energy: [USGS page, rough Mw relation log E = 5.24 + 1.44 Mw](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/earthquake-magnitude-energy-release-and-shaking-intensity); the Gutenberg-Richter joule form 10^(1.5M+4.8) is an older empirical relation (also in USGS OFR 01-0005 in dyne-cm).
- Sun binding energy: uniform sphere 2.28e41 J (PhysicsForums worked example); n=3 polytrope 1.5 GM^2/R, about 5.7e41 J (own multiplication of a UMD stellar-structure coefficient). A forum figure of 6.9e41 J was disputed there; no standard-solar-model value was found.
- Constants: [NASA NSSDC Earth Fact Sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html) equatorial radius 6378.137 km; sidereal day 23.9345 h (Britannica / archived NASA sheet); [NASA NSSDC Moon Fact Sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/moonfact.html) mass 0.07346e24 kg, with 384,400 km mean distance from NASA/JPL material (the current sheet lists 378,000 km at opposition); geostationary radius 42,164 km / altitude 35,786 km (Wikipedia-level source); water heat of fusion 333.55 J/g (CRC values in secondary tables).
- [NIF 500 TW and 1.85 MJ on target, 2012, LLNL](https://www.llnl.gov/article/38161/national-ignition-facility-makes-history-record-500-terawatt-shot) (found earlier; not used in the 10 claims).
- **Still not fetched** (standard constants from the author's knowledge): G, c, sigma, solar mass and radius, mean Earth radius 6371 km (IAU nominal), g0 (CODATA, listed on the NASA sheet), water specific heat, Wentworth sand classes, body energy density. A reviewer should check these.

## Schematic vs quantitative, and likely misreadings (OBSERVED + INFERRED, one coder)
- The frames in these claims are **quantity labels on schematic drawings**, not simulations: the globe with a thin shell (SC01), a "1G | 6G" comparison (SC02), a household-and-lightning comparison (SC03), grey bands with "470 m/s" on a flat map (SC09). Proportions in them were not measured against the stated numbers.
- Misreading risks: SC02 reads as exact when the true value (5.95 g) is a constant-density extrapolation; SC03: whether a viewer is misled depends on what "energy delivered" means, which is unresolved (v1 claimed a factor of 5 to 25; withdrawn); SC05 can be read as geophysics when it is an extrapolation of a scale whose result depends on which energy-magnitude relation is used; SC09's band shows speed as a flat stripe although it varies with cos(latitude).
- No hidden animation software or simulation method is inferred from appearance.
