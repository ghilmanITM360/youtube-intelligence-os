## Independent science checks that change the decision

**Day 1.** Integrated vertical drop from rest with fourth-order Runge–Kutta and ground-event interpolation using NASA Glenn’s drag form, `dv/dt = g(h) − ρ(h)(CdA/m)v²/2`. Reused the prior tabulated Standard Atmosphere representation and fixed `CdA/m = 0.00505 m²/kg`, so this is an independent integrator and sensitivity check on shared inputs. At steps 0.10 and 0.05 s, values agreed to the precision shown. Body and sea-level inputs, windless vertical trajectory, intact fixed posture and no parachute are model choices. It is one fitted drag parameter, with a 36 s vs 30 s discrepancy in supersonic duration in the historical check. Constant Cd is a major extrapolation to 100 km. The model compares speeds just above ground. Impact force depends on stopping conditions. The original script continues to 400 km beyond its title's headline boundary. Keep a distinct, clearly labeled orbit comparison or scope the main story to 100 km.

**Day 3.** `0.7 cm³ = 7×10⁻⁷ m³`. At the illustrative lower density `2.7×10¹⁷ kg/m³`, multiplication gives `1.89×10¹¹ kg = 189 million tonnes`. At `10¹⁸ kg/m³`, it gives 700 million tonnes. A 2,700 kg/m³ reference rock cube would have sides roughly 412–638 m. The prior calculation instead used 8 g divided by lead density, giving ~0.705 cm³ and a slightly different upper result. “At least 190 million” is overly strong as printed for the displayed rounded input. Use “about 190 million tonnes at the assumed density” or a range, and scope to an inert magically confined test sample. Teaspoon and pellet masses must stay separate. No verified material confinement or downstream behavior is established by mass multiplication. [Lattimer and Prakash](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.07820)

**Moon.** The solar/lunar tide-generating force ratio is approximately `(M_sun/M_moon)(r_moon/r_sun)^3 ≈ 0.46` at representative distances. It is not 46% of every local forecast height. Basin resonance, coastline, phase and transient response matter. Deep-time obliquity varies with spin and model setup, so omit a deterministic million-year timeline. Use one coastal scenario and distinguish the hypothetical removal from measurable present-day tide components. [NOAA tide forcing](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/education/tutorial_tides/tides02_cause.html) · [Lissauer moonless-Earth model](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20140011208/downloads/20140011208.pdf)

**Light / aliens / Voyager.** For WIB-22, a distance of 100 ly gives roughly 100 years of one-way lookback in the stipulated static geometry, but image resolution and detectable signals require named instrument, source, noise and exposure assumptions. Do not infer anyone can see people on Earth. Slow light in a medium and an altered invariant vacuum speed are different models. MIT's rendered experience establishes prior art and provides a route to a bounded model, while a changed-c alternate universe remains on hold. Voyager gives a real delay by construction, about two days for a prompt return signal at fixed one-light-day range. Communications duty cycles and processing add latency. [SETI Earth Detecting Earth](https://www.seti.org/news/earth-detecting-earth/) · [MIT Game Lab](https://gamelab.mit.edu/a-slower-speed-of-light/) · [NASA Voyager light-day](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/voyager-1-what-is-a-light-day/)

**Tiny planet.** An ideal constant-density sphere has `v_circ/R = sqrt(4πGρ/3)` for a circular path close to the surface. With assumed tangential speed 3–3.6 m/s, `ρ = 5,514` gives 2.42–2.90 km and `ρ = 2,000 kg/m³` gives 4.01–4.81 km. These are illustrative inputs, so they do not contradict prior values derived using a different speed range. A vertical jump under these assumptions returns near its starting point. A near-surface tangential path requires clearance over terrain. [OpenStax orbital mechanics](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/13-4-satellite-orbits-and-energy)
