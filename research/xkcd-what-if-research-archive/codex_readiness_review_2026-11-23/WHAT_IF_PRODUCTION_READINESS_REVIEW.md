## Proposed minimum viable test — do not execute now

A **45–60 second** original internally reviewed science explainer, 45 seconds minimum to exercise multiple connected scenes; this is a practical test design, not a validated sufficiency threshold. Choose a fresh, low-risk question with a simple calculable mechanism and available authoritative primary references, avoiding a recognizable What If question or joke. Four functional beats: (1) original question and orientation; (2) simple mechanism with labeled schematic; (3) a quantified comparison or scale change; (4) consequence and original resolution. Include an original character or visual anchor, an editable diagram, a deliberate transition or camera-like reframing, and an audio-led hold. Mark non-scale drawings and limitations.

**Science:** create a claim ledger with original source links, retrieval date, values, equations, units, assumptions, uncertainty and independent arithmetic; separate schematic from quantitative geometry. A qualified subject reviewer should check any technically consequential or uncertain claims before publication. Never reuse the four audit cases as settled facts by default.

**Original assets:** a distinct character/visual anchor, environments and props, diagram components, typography, unit labels, colors, effects and licensed/original narration and sound. Supply editable layered source files with named layers, anchors, coordinates and linked claim/scene IDs; independently create assets without copying source drawings, designs, scripts, jokes, audio or exact animation. Confirm licensing and keep private media in the configured durable root.

**Division:** Codex prepares source and calculation records, script/scene specifications and original editable asset contracts; Claude assembles and animates the provided assets, aligns licensed/original narration and sound, renders and returns editable project plus issues. Either role must report actual capability/tool failures; no assumption that either tool has access. Exchange manifest with hashes, scene IDs, asset IDs, source and claim links, frame rate, durations and versioned review notes; log each handoff and revision. The owner remains sole decision-maker about starting and publication.

**Acceptance criteria, chosen before production:**
1. A viewer unfamiliar with the topic can state the question, mechanism and conclusion after one viewing; review with a human and record confusion or failure, not a claimed population result.
2. Every spoken/on-screen numeric claim has a traceable source, reproducible calculation, clear units and an appropriate qualifier; any unresolved material claim blocks publication. Schematic visuals cannot imply unsupported precision.
3. Independent human viewing at normal speed and muted checks visual continuity, readable labels and whether the intended progression can be followed; compare the work to its own original brief rather than scoring resemblance. Inspect motion at native frames where a timing claim matters.
4. Human listening checks intelligibility, emphasis, speech/visual sync, music/effect interference, clipping and silence; correct defects and repeat the affected passage and full final viewing. Record reviewer and limitations.
5. Deliver playable full export and editable project, source assets and manifest; a fresh reopen and re-render verifies missing files, fonts, timing and dependencies. Record resolution, frame rate, codec, duration and rendered hash. No silent reliance on the original reference media.
6. For an actual content plan, retrieve and cite the repository reference framework and run `scripts/run_youtube_intelligence.py --framework-plan <plan.json>`; follow the framework-guided gate when claiming guidance. Save any production script under `productions/<project>/**/*SCRIPT*.md|txt` with required frontmatter and run `scripts/check_slop_risk.py`; deliver findings and C1–C5 checklist as repository policy requires.
7. Maintain a failure log linking each problem to scene, source, evidence, reviewer, severity, change and retest. Propose knowledge-base updates with explicit observation vs hypothesis and counterexamples; do not call a one-video result a validated channel-wide rule. Only update canonical records when separately authorized.
