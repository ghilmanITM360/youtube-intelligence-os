## Counterexamples and limits
- **-y7_DWddSK8 (WEAK, 0.33x, 247 views) has the features the STRONG sibling has**: the same channel, a numbered "quietly rich" list title, a frame story, a thumbnail that matches the script item for item, and a well-placed end subscribe plus next-video pointer. Its content defects (four miscalculated headline figures) are also present in TYPICAL videos. This is the clearest case that none of the observed features separates the tiers.
- The numbered-list title appears in a 24x, a 1.6x and a 0.33x video; the open loop and an early ask also span tiers.
- The two STRONG videos are the only ones with no major arithmetic inconsistency, but two TYPICAL videos (-ZcdgylpeJg, -fuCWtCi4Mc) also have none and five TYPICAL videos with errors still sit at 0.7-2.0x, so an error does not prevent a typical result and its absence does not make a strong one (n = 2).
- Tier is peer-set dependent for three videos (-DIT32sBkAk and -nTyzYhIoBc rise to STRONG with five peers; -y7_DWddSK8 rises to TYPICAL).
- The WEAK Lucas Grant video (-IpucSOwKgQ, 0.26x) and the TYPICAL one (-nTyzYhIoBc, 1.62x) are both POV stories without a CTA from one channel, so format and CTA do not explain their difference. Upload age spans 0.5 to 319 days; K01's lifetime-rate ratio only partly controls for it.
- Single snapshots only: **when any video broke out, and how fast, is UNAVAILABLE.** No CTR, impressions, retention, audience or audio data exist for any of them.

## Alternative explanations not testable here
Channel size and prior audience, topic demand (a payday routine and "signs of wealth" are broader topics than Japanese bonds, UK vehicle exports or a 19-clip reaction), release timing and promotion, thumbnail and title click behaviour, recommendation placement and retention are all unmeasured. Nothing above supports a causal statement about views.
