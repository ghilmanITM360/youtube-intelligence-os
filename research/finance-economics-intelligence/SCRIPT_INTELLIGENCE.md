## Quality opportunities supported by actual wording

**Unit consistency:** QvaHDxdxQFg explicitly introduces a 2% monthly rate, calculates 100 → 102 → 104.04 → 106.12 across months, then describes 2% on the new balance “every day.” Record this as an internal transcript inconsistency pending audio verification. Do not silently repair ASR. [CFPB's daily-rate explanation](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-daily-periodic-rate-on-a-credit-card-en-46/) distinguishes daily rates derived from APR; a monthly example needs its own consistently labeled unit. Checked 2026-10-16.

**Comparator consistency:** DAwQLnBO94U compares investment earnings of $9,400 with a $2,100 raise, then says the money made more than the job. Earlier salary in the same text is $54,000. The presented comparison supports earnings exceeding the raise, not the salary. This is a specific script QA issue, not evidence of deliberate deception or a confirmed spoken error.

**Projection transparency:** the three POV scripts describe large balances, raises and life decisions, but these reads do not establish complete cash-flow schedules, return assumptions, taxes or real persons. Treat them as narrative scenarios, not verified financial case histories. Our scripts should label invented scenarios and show enough arithmetic to reproduce them.

## Original writing brief

Open with one observable contradiction and a bounded question. Explain two or three necessary links before adding another concept. Give every important number a source, date, unit and denominator. Use one worked example with assumptions, one meaningful exception and a direct answer. A callback can close the emotional story, but must not replace the mechanism answer. CTA follows the payoff.

Test hypotheses with original-channel retention and comprehension feedback: question-first versus scene-first opening; one mechanism versus a catalog of concepts; assumption-labeled chart versus unexplained big balance. No competitor CTR, retention or private analytics exists here.
