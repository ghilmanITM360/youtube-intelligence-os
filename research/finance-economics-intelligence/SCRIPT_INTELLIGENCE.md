## Quality opportunities supported by actual wording

**Unit consistency:** QvaHDxdxQFg explicitly introduces a 2% monthly rate, calculates 100 → 102 → 104.04 → 106.12 across months, then describes 2% on the new balance “every day.” Record this as an internal transcript inconsistency pending audio verification. Do not silently repair ASR. [CFPB's daily-rate explanation](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-daily-periodic-rate-on-a-credit-card-en-46/) distinguishes daily rates derived from APR; a monthly example needs its own consistently labeled unit. Checked 2026-10-16.

**Comparator consistency:** DAwQLnBO94U compares investment earnings of $9,400 with a $2,100 raise, then says the money made more than the job. Earlier salary in the same text is $54,000. The presented comparison supports earnings exceeding the raise, not the salary. This is a specific script QA issue, not evidence of deliberate deception or a confirmed spoken error.

**Projection transparency:** the three POV scripts describe large balances, raises and life decisions, but these reads do not establish complete cash-flow schedules, return assumptions, taxes or real persons. Treat them as narrative scenarios, not verified financial case histories. Our scripts should label invented scenarios and show enough arithmetic to reproduce them.

## Original writing brief

Open with one observable contradiction and a bounded question. Explain two or three necessary links before adding another concept. Give every important number a source, date, unit and denominator. Use one worked example with assumptions, one meaningful exception and a direct answer. A callback can close the emotional story, but must not replace the mechanism answer. CTA follows the payoff.

Test hypotheses with original-channel retention and comprehension feedback: question-first versus scene-first opening; one mechanism versus a catalog of concepts; assumption-labeled chart versus unexplained big balance. No competitor CTR, retention or private analytics exists here.

Existing timestamped Casually Finance/ Casual Economics research remains separately reusable through [immutable sources](REUSED_SOURCE_REGISTER.csv). Source-reported 0–34.52s housing contradiction and SpaceX 0–42.78s buyer question are inherited evidence, not newly timed reviews. Full Economics AV sign-off remains 0/11.

## Continuation: two newly rankable finance scripts

Full reads now total **17**, spanning seven channels (14 long-form, three Shorts). Carl `TDEme_uxx4c` is strong at 55.63×; Bob `JIz_O73sq4A` is typical at 0.55×. Both use familiar-person problems, numeric promises and delayed explanations, but their performance differs. These two are not a controlled strong/weak pair.

**Carl:** the script promises a milestone map, states an 8% return assumption and explicitly says it is not guaranteed. It then adds employer match to explain the opening million-dollar figure. The thumbnail's million-plus outcome is therefore conditional on more money than the title's $500 monthly contribution. Under a constant nominal 8%/12 monthly rate and end-of-month deposits, `500*((1+.08/12)**360-1)/(.08/12)` is about $745,180. An extra $180/month produces about $268,265, totaling about $1,013,444. This reproduces a hypothetical model, not future returns. Later the same named character's 30-year balance changes from about $1.162m to $888k without a fully specified return path. Preserve this as an unresolved scenario-consistency question. The arithmetic is more transparent than a bare big-balance promise, but the assumptions need to remain visible throughout.

**Bob:** the script distinguishes distributions from return, introduces NAV, then uses a worked example, behavioral framing and many named research references before a guide/product CTA. The references were observed, not verified. It states an 11% starting yield and 0.89% expense ratio, then says the result is 11.11%; simple subtraction gives **10.11%**. This is an internal wording/arithmetic discrepancy pending audio review; it does not establish that subtracting an expense ratio from the quoted distribution yield is the correct fund calculation. That requires a specific fund, return definition and fee treatment. Broad tax, return-of-capital and market assertions likewise need primary-source checks.

**Pilot implication:** reveal material assumptions before the payoff, maintain one scenario ledger, and audit citations rather than counting them. Delayed conditional reveals may create curiosity but can also weaken the promise; no retention or CTR effect was measured.
