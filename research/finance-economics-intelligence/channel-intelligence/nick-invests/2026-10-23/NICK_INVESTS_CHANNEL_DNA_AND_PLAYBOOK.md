# Nick Invests channel DNA and original Finance playbook

## A. Channel DNA

Observed registered identity: accessible US personal finance delivered through money milestones, costly household decisions, numerical stakes, status reversal and relief from financial anxiety. All 363 registered videos are long-form; no Shorts result can be inferred. In twelve inspected images the recurring identity is a cartoon host, white ground, large black type and a concrete financial object. In six full transcripts the explanation is linked to everyday consequences, agency and the meaning of security. These are observations of the available corpus and sample, not demographic analytics or proof of retention.

## C. Weak or inconclusive patterns

Dollar specificity alone: 82 titles, 79 eligible, median 1.00034× (five-peer 0.95081×). Warning/trap wording: 79 titles, 77 eligible, median 0.90656×; 16 weak versus 10 strong. A large dollar value or fear word is not a defensible winning formula. Identical visual identity spans strong and weak videos. A reused payday/order title can revert to ordinary performance. ## D. Original title frameworks

Use these as hypotheses with a real payoff: 'Same [income/balance], different [usable outcome]: here is why'; '[Purchase price] versus the full [time horizon] cost'; 'What changes when [specific constraint] disappears?'; 'Before you choose [A/B], compare these [limited number] inputs'; '[Familiar number] means less until you define [denominator]'. Generate original wording and avoid copying the reference creator's headline or promising universal wealth. Evidence for families is observational; every framework needs its own counterexample and first-party test.

## E. Original thumbnail frameworks

Use an original ledger, simple flowchart, two clearly labeled alternatives or one expense/runway meter. Primary text should add the missing decision variable instead of duplicating the title. Limit small labels, use accessible contrast and test actual 320px readability. Security can be shown through covered expenses rather than cash piles; cost comparisons can use equal starting resources rather than an exaggerated future-loss total. These design choices are PLAUSIBLE_HYPOTHESIS; none is proven to increase CTR from competitor data. Do not reproduce the creator's recognizable host, artwork or branding.

## F. Hook structures

Show a recognizable moment, ask a bounded question, promise a calculation with known assumptions, reveal a first useful result, then explain the tradeoff. For a budget case, establish location, household and year before a national claim. For a compounding case, state contribution timing and rate basis before the large future figure. For a comparison, show one fair counterexample early. Avoid an unverified first-30-second rule from Nick's untimed text; measure our actual opening after publication.

## G. Script and story frameworks

Decision story: everyday situation → labeled hypothetical household → matched options → dated ledger → sensitivity → human tradeoff → practical next action. Progress story: problem → expense-relative milestone → before/after options → limits → repeatable action. Mechanism explainer: puzzling result → small reproducible example → general relationship → exception → decision checklist. Curiosity loops must pay off; anecdotes illustrate and never substitute for population evidence. Use narration beats with real timing only during our own production. The six-source matrix documents support and failure cases.

## H. Financial editorial checklist

1. For each factual assertion record original primary source, release date, reference period, geography, population, unit and uncertainty; keep source extracts separate from interpretation.
2. Define household versus adult, mean versus median, net worth versus investable assets and cash versus market portfolio.
3. Independently recompute annuities and amortization; identify end/beginning contribution dates, nominal annual versus effective rate, fees, taxes and inflation. A second computation should use a genuinely different formulation or cash-flow ledger.
4. For major purchases compare usable alternatives with equal starting resources, residual values, financing and operating costs on the same timeline. Depreciation, cash spent and opportunity cost cannot all be summed blindly.
5. For a rule, show the domain where it works and a counterexample. For affordability, separate lender criteria from personal spending comfort. For retirement, specify horizon, allocation, withdrawal convention and failure risk.
6. Treat historical returns, hypothetical constant-return illustrations and guaranteed deposit terms separately. Never promise wealth or guaranteed market outcomes.
7. Label composites and dramatizations. Do not claim a study proves a threshold or psychological mechanism when it reports association.
8. Review title/image/script consistency and all 'always', 'never', 'everyone', 'guaranteed' and 'research shows' language. A disclaimer cannot replace correct claims.
9. Review calculations and sensitive tax/benefit details before publication; preserve a correction log after release. Do not make paid-product revenue or outcome claims without evidence.

## I. Differentiation strategy

Proposed positioning: 'Money decisions you can inspect.' Keep relatable scenes and numerical clarity, add a complete visible source-and-assumption trail, equal alternatives and honest counterexamples. Let viewers choose according to their circumstances; preserve enjoyment and safety rather than moralizing all consumption. Publishing sources does not itself prove demand, but it directly addresses documented credibility gaps.

## J. Repeatable production workflow

Choose audience need → find a strong reference and weak counterexample → write the distinct editorial question → build a dated claim ledger → compute and stress-test → draft an original story → reconcile title, image and promised payoff → check source and numeric accuracy → produce original visuals/audio → record versions and publish only after human editorial review → measure equal-age results → retain failures and corrections. This is a recommended manual process; no repository automation or new system phase was built.

## K. Measurement and experiments

Use our own Studio metrics only. Track traffic-specific impressions and CTR alongside watch time, actual opening retention, payoff retention and returning viewers. Prefer native randomized within-video packaging tests if available; sequential swaps are time-confounded. Hold topic and format steady when testing packaging, and repeat on multiple videos. Report medians and leave-best-out sensitivity. Launch results with six videos are exploratory; do not declare success from one breakout or a high CTR with poor promise fulfillment.

## L. Future audit priorities

First fill evidence gaps that matter to a decision: use the separately recovered source publication days for a clearly labeled future day-precision comparison if needed; acquire a user-approved mature follow-up snapshot separately; obtain matched historical image evidence if a packaging-effect question becomes necessary; research a broader weak/typical script sample; compare Lucas Grant and Carl Invests on matched topics; use first-party exports when an actual owned channel exists. Original audiovisual evidence is needed for editing, delivery and sound questions. Do not start the next individual NICK audit or acquisitions automatically.
