# 30-day content test plan

This is a proposed launch experiment, not an automated publishing commitment. Produce six carefully researched long-form videos, with two held as reserves; the 30 ideas are a research backlog, not a requirement to publish daily. A 30-day launch can establish feasibility and preliminary signals, not prove a winning channel formula. Videos published late in the month need their full 7/28-day observation windows afterward.

## Days 1–4: establish the measurement contract

Choose US audience and household assumptions explicitly; if the actual intended market differs, re-source first. Research I01 (expense-based savings), I02 (all-in housing) and I03 (matched car alternatives). Build a source sheet recording claim, population, geography, data vintage, release date, units, URL, calculation and limitations. Give each hypothetical household a label. Calculate a base, lower and higher scenario; check equal starting resources and non-overlapping cash flows. Define one main question and a promised payoff per script.

Create an original visual identity with real ledgers/diagrams and licensed assets. Do not use Nick's cartoon, artwork, scripts, voice imitation or visual branding. Make two title variants and two thumbnail directions per video, changing one concept at a time. Test legibility at actual 320px display size. Have a human explain the promise after looking at each thumbnail for a few seconds; that is a readability check, not a CTR estimate.

Log baseline analytics available to the operator: impressions, impression CTR, traffic source, views, watch time, average view duration, average percentage viewed, available audience-retention curve, returning viewers and subscriber changes. Record title/thumbnail version and time. No competitor Studio metric is supplied or inferred.

## Days 5–11: first decision trio

Publish I01 around day 5, I02 around day 8, I03 around day 11, subject to complete verification. Keep long-form runtime within one chosen band (for example 12–18 minutes, an editorial starting choice rather than a Nick-derived optimum), production quality and upload time consistent. The existing channel data support testing useful decision specificity; they do not prove that 20-minute videos are optimal.

For I01 compare months-of-runway versus dollars in the title. For I02 test leftover-cash versus price-only thumbnail framing. For I03 test a matched-cost ledger hook versus repair anxiety. Cross-video results remain confounded by topic; use within-video native randomized packaging experiments where the operator's platform actually offers them. Otherwise treat sequential revisions as observations affected by time and traffic, and retain version timestamps. Never change title, image, length and hook together and attribute an effect to one element.

Collect 24/72-hour snapshots for operational checks. Do not crown a winner at either checkpoint. Compare each video's equal-age 7-day result to the channel's own same-format/similar-topic releases when they exist; a new channel has no stable baseline yet. Keep all videos in the dataset, including low-view releases.

## Days 12–18: replicate one need and one utility structure

Publish I04 (variable-income payday, around day 15) and I05 (same emergency, three financing paths, around day 18). I04 tests whether a more specific audience problem can overcome the generic payday sequel limitation. I05 tests a controlled example with a visible cost reveal. Keep the same production band. Rotate the first topic once rather than expanding into ten unrelated subjects. Prepare I06 and I10 as reserves only after the data definitions and math are ready.

Read the first complete 7-day windows. Inspect the opening retention curve at the point where the video actually delivers its promise; these are our measured timed videos, unlike Nick's untimed ASR. Tag any mismatch between thumbnail promise and script, comments showing misunderstanding and facts needing correction. A source correction is mandatory regardless of CTR.

## Days 19–25: test whether specificity generalizes

Publish I06 around day 22 if the distribution/denominator visuals are clear. Compare its relevant benchmark framing with prior concrete household decisions, keeping topic differences explicit. If research is not ready, use I10 only after reproducing every assumption. Do not rush high-complexity tax, insurance or retirement content to meet a calendar. Review I01/I05 as a related liquidity pair and I02/I03 as major-purchase decisions; these are imperfect matched pairs, not randomized causal trials.

## Days 26–30: review and choose the next experiment

Use only complete 7-day windows in the primary launch review; put newer uploads in a pending column. Recheck I01 at its eventual 28-day window outside the month if necessary. Record the median result, all individual outcomes and a leave-one-best-video-out summary. One breakout must not set the expected performance target.

Promote a packaging hypothesis only after at least three comparable video tests and a consistent direction in quality-adjusted outcomes. This is a decision rule, not a power calculation. With only six releases many hypotheses will remain INSUFFICIENT_DATA. Plan additional replications next month. Prefer a clearer decision and stronger evidence if numeric reach differences are inconclusive.

## What counts as improvement

Primary packaging outcome: watch time generated per impression where the operator can calculate it consistently, with impression CTR and viewing duration inspected together. Approximation CTR × average view duration mixes denominators unless duration is from the same impression-derived view population; label any such proxy and keep traffic sources separate. Native experiment results, if available, take precedence over this cross-population proxy.

Secondary outcomes: opening/first-30-second retention, midpoint and payoff retention, average percentage viewed, returning viewers and evidence of correct understanding in comments or a voluntary survey. Compare traffic-source mix and equal-age windows, and report sample sizes. Higher CTR accompanied by worse promise delivery is not an editorial win.

Pre-register an editorial relevance threshold (for example a 10% relative improvement in the primary metric without a material retention/accuracy decline); this number is a proposed decision threshold, not a statistically detected effect. Do not use an arbitrary impression count as proof of significance. Report uncertainty intervals if the native experiment provides them; otherwise state that results are descriptive and collect more comparable releases. Stop on a material factual error, promise mismatch or missing verification, not on a temporarily weak view count.

## Specific initial hypotheses

1. I01: months-of-expenses framing improves qualified comprehension versus a magic dollar threshold.
2. I02: an all-in cash-flow image earns better qualified viewing than a house-price shock.
3. I03: matched alternatives sustain interest without a misleading million-dollar retirement cost.
4. I04: variable-income relevance improves usefulness over a recycled universal payday routine.
5. I05: identical shock and starting resources make a financing comparison easier to understand.
6. I06: median-versus-mean explanation differentiates a familiar statistics topic.

All are PLAUSIBLE_HYPOTHESIS until tested on our own channel. Keep source evidence, measurements and interpretations in separate columns. Publish no investment or virality guarantees.
