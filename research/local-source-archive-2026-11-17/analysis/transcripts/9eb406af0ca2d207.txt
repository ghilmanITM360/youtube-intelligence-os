If the debt is this big, the interest is this big, and the deficit is double the target,
why isn't this already a full-blown crisis? America's economy still grows faster than it
borrows. The average rate across everything the United States owes is around 3.5%. The economy,
in dollar terms, grew at about 6.5% over the past year. And when the rate you pay is lower
than the rate you grow, the old debt gets smaller against the economy all by itself.
The weight of it comes down without anyone repaying a single dollar of principal.
Growing faster than your debt doesn't mean you can borrow whatever you want. It gives you
breathing room. But if you extend past that breathing room, the debt climbs anyway,
which is exactly what's happening to the United States.
Federal debt held by the public is a little over 100% of GDP. The Congressional Budget Office has it
at 120% by 2036. So the debt load is still climbing, while growth is beating the interest
rate by three full points. The Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget set the date.
2031. That's when the two lines meet. That's the year the rate America pays to borrow,
passes the rate the economy grows. We go from having breathing room to having
someone suck the oxygen out of the room.
