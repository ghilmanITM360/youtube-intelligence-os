The only evidence that America spent its own money holding up Japan's economy
is a photograph of a handwritten to-do list.
It was sitting in front of the Secretary of the Treasury of the United States,
with one line on it legible from across the room:
"Buy Japanese yen. Five to ten billion dollars."
The photo was taken at 11:33 in the morning, on July 31st,
by a photographer with a long lens sitting across the room.
That Friday, for the first time since 1998,
the United States walked into the currency market and bought yen,
and the receipt was a doodle on a notepad.
What makes this interesting is the rate America pays to borrow money for 30 years
also just hit the highest level on record since July of 2007,
and the country we're helping prop up holds more American government debt
than anyone else on earth.
So the question becomes: why are we doing this?
What are we actually buying?
Because the story that gets reported,
and the story that's actually unfolding underneath, are rarely the same one.
Of everything in this story, the only thing that told you exactly what was happening,
with nothing hidden beneath the surface,
was a handwritten to-do list that nobody was ever supposed to see.
