In 1985, the Bureau of Labor Statistics
found that 80% of full-time workers
at medium and large American companies
were in a defined benefit plan, or a pension.
By 2000, that number was 36.
Today, that number sits around 14%,
which may just sound like a small paperwork change,
but it isn't.
Under a pension, the company owes a fixed amount
on a fixed schedule, so it has to go buy something
to match that shape.
But under a 401 , the company owes nothing past payday.
According to the numbers from EBRI
and the Investment Company Institute,
more than 70% of all 401 assets sit in stocks,
while standalone bond funds get about 5%.
The Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development
is an international organization of 38 developed countries.
You can think of it as the Avengers, but for economics.
Across its member countries, pension funds'
domestic bond holdings have been cut in half,
from 8% in 2007 down to 4% last year,
which means the biggest change in structural demand
in the bond market in 50 years
was caused by someone in HR named Susan.
