On August 18th, the 30-year touched the highest level in 19 years.
The very next morning, the Treasury decided it was time to step in and do something about it.
Then on August 19th, the United States Secretary of the Treasury, Scott Besant,
announced the Treasury was doubling the size of its buyback operations at the long end.
$2 billion per operation now becomes at least four.
The plan was that the extra buying pressure would drive long-end bond prices up,
and as those bond prices rise, their interest rates would naturally fall.
Then the next day, after this accelerated buyback was announced,
Scott Besant went on television and said,
My job is to make sure that the market is looking at fundamentals
and that the market does not dictate policy.
That was the sentence that caused the entire finance industry to erupt.
Five days after Besant said this on national television, on the evening of August 24th,
the Wall Street Journal published an opinion piece called Let the Bond Market Speak.
Treasury yields act as the last disciplinarian,
the only critic Washington can't ignore
because it's the only one that has to be paid.
