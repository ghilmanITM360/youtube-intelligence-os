Japan owes more relative to the size of its economy than any major country on Earth.
This year, the cost of servicing that debt jumped nearly 11% to 13 trillion yen out of
a total budget of 122.3 trillion yen.
And right as Japan's debt bill is spiraling, the yen has also collapsed to 40-year lows.
Why the currency is falling isn't complicated.
It comes down to basic math.
For the last few decades, Japan kept its borrowing cost pinned near zero.
Whether this was a good idea or not is up to interpretation, but they've recently
started rolling it all back.
Japan's benchmark rate now sits around 1%, while America's sits between 3.5 and 3.75.
That gap is the entire story, because capital doesn't catch feelings.
It's not your clingy ex-girlfriend.
Capital goes wherever it gets paid the most.
So investors dump the yen, chase the higher yield in dollars, and drive the currency off
a cliff.
And if the US dollar sits on one side of nearly 90% of all currency trades on Earth, buying
yen almost always means selling dollars.
