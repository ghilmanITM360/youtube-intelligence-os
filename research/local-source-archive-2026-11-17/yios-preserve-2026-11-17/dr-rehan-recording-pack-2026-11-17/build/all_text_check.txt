[Interviewer] Is a blood pressure of 90 over 60 too low?
0:00

Is ninety over sixty too low? Not necessarily. A reading below ninety over sixty is commonly

called low blood pressure. But the number alone does not tell us whether it is dangerous. A low reading
with no symptoms, in someone whose pressure is usually low, is often fine. A reading that is not so low
but comes with dizziness, fainting or confusion is a concern. So the answer to worry about the number,
the symptoms, or both is: look at both, and look at what is normal for you. (89 words, Answer first)
[Interviewer] Which symptoms should we pay attention to?
0:43

The symptoms that matter are dizziness or lightheadedness, fainting, blurred vision, weakness,

tiredness, nausea, and confusion. Palpitations and headaches can also happen. One lower reading with
no symptoms is usually not a cause for alarm. Persistent symptoms with low readings are different, and
should be looked at. Keep a note of when the symptoms happen, what you were doing, and your
readings. That record is very useful for your doctor. (70 words, Symptoms that matter)
[Interviewer] My mother gets dizzy when she stands up. Is that low blood pressure?
1:16

Dizziness on standing may be a postural drop, which doctors call orthostatic hypotension.

Standing makes blood pool in the legs, and in some people the body does not adjust fast enough. In the

clinic we check this. The person lies down for five minutes, and we measure blood pressure. Then they

do not try this alone if you are unsteady. You could fall. It should be done in a clinic, with someone beside

2:10

[Interviewer] Is it true that cold weather is bad for the heart?
0:00

Does cold weather strain the heart? It can. The extra strain matters most in older people and in

people who already have heart disease or high blood pressure. But this does not mean going outside in

[Interviewer] What does cold do inside the body?
0:35

When you breathe cold air or your skin gets cold, the blood vessels throughout the body

narrow. That can raise blood pressure, and it can also narrow the coronary arteries, the vessels that feed
the heart. So the heart is working harder while its own blood supply is tighter. For many people this is no
problem. For someone with narrowed arteries or high blood pressure, it can be enough to trigger
symptoms. Studies have also linked colder periods with more heart problems, but those studies show a
link, not proof that cold alone is the cause. (96 words, What cold does)

are most affected. You do not need snow for this. Hurrying uphill on a cold morning, lifting heavy loads,

[Interviewer] Why did my doctor want to compare my ankle and arm blood pressure?
0:00

Why compare ankle and arm blood pressure? Because the ankle pressure can show whether

blood is flowing freely to the legs. The test is called the ankle-brachial index, or ABI. We measure the top
blood pressure number in the arm and at the ankle, and divide the ankle number by the arm number. In a
healthy leg, the ankle pressure is about the same as the arm, or a little higher. If the arteries in the leg
are narrowed, the ankle pressure is lower. The ABI helps us diagnose peripheral artery disease, or PAD,
when leg arteries are narrowed by plaque. (101 words, Answer first)
[Interviewer] When would a doctor order an ABI?
0:48

A doctor orders an ABI when the symptoms or the examination suggest PAD. The classic

symptom is calf, thigh or buttock pain when you walk, that goes away when you rest. Other clues are
weak or absent foot pulses, cool or pale feet, hair loss on the legs, and sores on the feet or toes that heal
slowly. An ABI is recommended in this situation to establish the diagnosis. It is a different question from
screening someone who has no symptoms, which I will come to. (86 words, Why a doctor orders it)
[Interviewer] How is the test done?
