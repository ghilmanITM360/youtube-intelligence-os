# 3 visual directions

All three keep:
- no AI video;
- code-drawn anatomy and graphs;
- the doctor's footage as the anchor;
- text on the empty grey wall to his left (and behind him, using the matte, for the hero titles).

---

## 1. Clinical Calm (kit default) ⭐ recommended
- **Palette:** warm off-white `#F4F1EC`, ink navy `#14213D`, warm grey `#8A8A8A`, one accent **clinical red `#D64545`**. Navy and red already sit in his scrubs and on the monitor.
- **Type:** Inter/Figtree for labels, **Instrument Serif** for the one emotional word ("*too* low").
- **Motion grammar:** Swiss grid with lots of air. Mask reveals on a ruled line. Numbers huge, labels tiny. One idea per frame. Paper grain, soft vignette, no glow.
- **Signature device:** **an ECG/BP line.** It draws the cold-open BP reading, becomes the chapter wipe, and **dips sharply when he says "when they stand up"**, the visual for "lower, too low".
- **Signature shot:** a full-frame off-white card. A huge `150/80` builds digit by digit, then splits into two columns: *protect the heart* | *avoid falls*.
- **Sound:** paper ticks, soft clicks, a low heartbeat pulse, a warm piano bed under the talking head only.
- **Why it fits:**
  - Older viewers read high-contrast dark-on-light best.
  - It feels like a trusted clinic leaflet, not an ad.
  - It is the fastest to make look hand-crafted.

## 2. Midnight Clinic (from your inspiration images)
- **Palette:** deep navy `#0B1430` → cobalt `#1E4FD8` gradient, ice white text, accent red `#E5484D`, a subtle glass blue `#9DB7FF`.
- **Type:** Manrope bold plus an italic serif accent, white on dark.
- **Motion grammar:**
  - frosted glass cards that slide in with blur→focus;
  - rim-light sweeps;
  - light 3D (Three.js): a translucent glass heart and a 3D BP chart with one glowing bar.
- **Signature device:** a **glowing BP gauge needle** that swings and drops into a red zone when he stands up.
- **Signature shot:** a 3D bar chart of the targets (HYVET 150/80 vs newer 140/90), with one bar lit and the camera slowly orbiting.
- **Sound:** soft sci-fi hits on card reveals, airy whooshes, a low synth pad (under the talking head only).
- **Why it fits / risk:**
  - Very modern, and great for Shorts thumbnails.
  - Risk: it can look "tech/crypto" and less trustworthy to 65+ viewers.
  - Dark slides next to his bright grey office create big brightness jumps that need grading.

## 3. Hand-drawn Notebook
- **Palette:** cream paper `#FAF6EE`, pencil graphite `#3A3A3A`, a red-pen accent, a highlighter yellow `#F5D547` for key words.
- **Type:** a clean handwriting face for notes (e.g. Caveat) plus Inter for numbers.
- **Motion grammar:**
  - stroke-by-stroke SVG drawing, like a doctor sketching on a pad;
  - stop-motion 12 fps jitter on the drawings;
  - highlighter swipes under key words.
- **Signature device:** **a red pen circling** the key number or word.
- **Signature shot:**
  - a stick-figure-free side profile outline standing up;
  - a blood "level" in a drawn tube drops;
  - a spiral of dizziness is doodled.
- **Sound:** pencil scratch, paper turn, marker squeak, a ukulele/pizzicato bed.
- **Why it fits / risk:**
  - Very friendly and very clear for a 10-year-old.
  - Risk: it can feel less "specialist", and long drawings slow the pace.

---

**Recommendation: 1 (Clinical Calm), with one idea borrowed from 2:** a single light-3D moment (the target comparison chart) for the "premium" feel. It matches his footage, is easiest for older eyes, and stays trustworthy.
