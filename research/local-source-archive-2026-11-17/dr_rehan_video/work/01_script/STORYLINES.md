# Storylines: "Blood Pressure After 65" (picture story over the recorded narration)

The narration and Dr. Rehan's recording stay exactly as they are. Only the pictures change: they now tell one story instead of a set of unrelated stock shots.

**Audience:** adults aged 50+ in the USA, UK, New Zealand and Australia. There are two kinds of viewer:
- people in their late 60s–80s on blood pressure medicine;
- their sons and daughters in their 50s who look after them.

Every storyline gives both of them someone to recognise.

**The one thing to remember:** "My blood pressure should be controlled *safely*, not just pushed lower, and I should tell my doctor if I feel dizzy when I stand."

---

## A. "Two Mornings" (recommended: one person the viewer becomes, and a mirror ending that pays off the hook)

**Device:** a moment in someone's day. **Form:** a mirror, with match cuts between photo and notebook sketch.

**Who:**
- **Margaret, 72.** She takes her own blood pressure at home every morning.
- **Claire, 50.** Her daughter, who drives her to the check-up.
- **Lily, 7.** Her granddaughter, who is the reason it all matters.

**The film:**
- The video opens on a morning everyone over 65 knows. Margaret sits on the edge of her bed at dawn, the home monitor cuff on her arm, and the number on the screen is finally lower. She stands up, and the room tilts.
- The film follows her through the questions the narrator asks: the check-up, her own kitchen table (where the "notebook" explainers are drawn) and the dark hallway at night (where a fall could happen).
- **It ends on the same bedroom, the same camera angle, a different morning:** warm sun, and she stands up steady. Then she walks in the garden holding Lily's hand.

**First two seconds:**
- Blue dawn. A close-up of a home BP monitor on a bedside table, a woman's arm in the cuff, a gold wedding ring and a small silver watch.
- The blank screen gets code-drawn digits that tick DOWN.
- Narrator: "You're 72."

**Beats:**
1. Hook: the dizzy morning.
2. Intro: off to the check-up with Claire, then Dr. Rehan.
3. Why treat: her fridge full of grandchildren's drawings.
4. Explainer: her notebook, with Margaret redrawn as a sketch.
5. Tension: the hallway at night, the curled rug, the banister.
6. Answer: the consultation, and the pill organiser that fits *her*.
7. Payoff: the mirror morning and the garden.
8. End card.

**Look:** Clinical Calm daylight in her home. Midnight Clinic blue only for the dawn and the night hallway. Notebook sketches drawn in the same notebook that sits on her kitchen table.

**Sound:** the dawn is room tone and a clock tick. The night hallway gets low cobalt drones. The mirror morning gets birdsong and the violin bed coming back.

**Why it works for this audience:** every object is one the viewer owns: the home monitor, the weekly pill organiser, reading glasses on a chain, the handrail. A 72-year-old sees themselves. A 50-year-old sees their mum.

## B. "The Phone Call"

**Device:** one object across the film. **Form:** split screen collapsing to one.

**Who:** Claire, 52, at work in her city, and her dad, 74, at home in his. The film runs as a split screen of two houses.

**The film:**
- His home BP monitor readings travel to her phone as photos.
- When he says "the room spun this morning", the two halves collapse into one: she is now at his door.

**First two seconds:**
- A split frame: on the left an old hand presses the monitor button; on the right a phone buzzes on an office desk.

**Beats:**
1. Hook: the call.
2. Intro: the check-up together.
3. Explainers: on his notebook.
4. Tension: his stairs.
5. Answer: the consultation.
6. Payoff: the two of them on a walk.
7. End card.

**Look:** two colour worlds, cool for the office and warm for his home, merging at the payoff.

**Why it might not be first:** it speaks to the 50-year-old more than to the 70-year-old, and split screens get harder to read on phones.

---

## C. "The House That Tips" (the bold one)

**Device:** the object is the explanation. **Form:** a flight through one world.

**The idea:** her whole house is a balance scale. Each room is a stop on the route: the bedroom (dizzy), the kitchen (the medicine), the hallway (falls), the garden (protection). Objects in each room slide or tip as the pressure goes up or down, and the camera travels from room to room through doorways.

**First two seconds:**
- A dollhouse-style cutaway of a small home at dawn. The whole house tilts 3° as a light comes on in the bedroom.

**Why it might not be first:** it is clever, but more abstract. There is no single person to care about, and it needs many layered plates for the parallax.

---

**Recommendation: A.** One character with a mirror ending is the clearest, most human story for a 50+ audience, and it fits the recorded "You're 72…" narration word for word.

Switching to B or C later only changes the CAST and the shot list in `02_assets/asset_prompts.md`. The world lock and style locks stay the same.
