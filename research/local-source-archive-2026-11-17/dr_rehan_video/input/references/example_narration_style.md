## 2. Narrator bridge — The treatment dilemma
Insert after original 1:28

Here's the challenge.
Leaving high blood pressure untreated can put your heart and brain at risk.
But lowering it without considering dizziness, falls, and other medical conditions can also cause problems.
So how do doctors protect your heart without putting you at unnecessary risk?
That is where individualized treatment becomes important.

## 3. Medical clarification — Blood pressure on standing
Insert immediately after the original discussion around 1:20, or move before Bridge 2

When blood pressure falls after you stand up, doctors call it orthostatic hypotension.
In simple words, your blood pressure drops when you get up.
This can cause dizziness or fainting in some people.
That's why a doctor may check your blood pressure both before and after standing, especially if you've experienced dizziness or falls.

## 4. Medical evidence update — Treatment targets and medication
Insert immediately after the original medication discussion around 2:49

One important point needs clarification.
Diuretics, sometimes called water tablets, are not automatically unsuitable for older adults.
In fact, a major clinical trial called HYVET used a diuretic to treat high blood pressure in people aged 80 and older.
That trial targeted 150 over 80.
But this is not one universal target for everyone today.
Current recommendations differ, and doctors also consider dizziness, frailty, kidney function, and other medical conditions.
Even medicines used for prostate symptoms can sometimes make blood pressure drop on standing.

## 5. Final narrator takeaway
Place after the doctor's closing

So, what should your blood pressure be after 65?
There isn't one number that's right for every person.
The important question is whether your blood pressure is being controlled safely.
If you experience dizziness when standing, repeated falls, or symptoms after taking your medicine, speak to your doctor.
The goal is not simply a lower number. It's better protection for your health.
And never change your blood pressure medication on your own.

Clinical basis (user's note): HYVET used indapamide and targeted 150/80 in people aged 80+, while modern BP guidance emphasizes tolerability and individual assessment.
