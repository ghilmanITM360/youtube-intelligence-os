Q. How dangerous is it, really,
to be in a pool during a thunderstorm?

A. PRETTY DANGEROUS. WATER IS conductive, but that’s not the biggest
problem—the biggest problem is that if you’re swimming, your head is poking
up from a large flat surface. But lightning striking the water near you would still
be bad. The 20,000 amps spread outward — mostly over the surface—but how
much of a jolt it will give you at what distance is hard to calculate.

My guess is that youd be in significant danger anywhere within a minimum
of a dozen meters—and farther in fresh water, because the current will be hap-
pier to take a shortcut through you.

What would happen if you were taking a shower when you were struck by
lightning? Or standing under a waterfall?

You're not in danger from the spray—it’s just a bunch of droplets of water in
the air. It’s the tub under your feet, and the puddle of water in contact with the
plumbing, that’s the real threat.
