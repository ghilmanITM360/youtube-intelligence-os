The burning sulfur would spill into the bromine. Bromine is liquid at room
temperature, a property it shares with only one other element—mercury. It’s also
pretty nasty stuff. The range of toxic compounds that would be produced by this
blaze is, at this point, incalculably large. However, if you did this experiment from
a safe distance, you might survive.

The fifth row contains something interesting: technetium-gq, our first radio-
active brick.

Technetium is the lowest-numbered element that has no stable isotopes. The
dose from a r-liter cube of the metal wouldn't be enough to be lethal in our
experiment, but it’s still substantial. If you spent all day wearing it as a hat—or
breathed it in as dust —it could definitely kill you.
