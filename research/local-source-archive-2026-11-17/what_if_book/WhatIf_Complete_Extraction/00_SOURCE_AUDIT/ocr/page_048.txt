But forget the Earth —what would happen to the Moon?

The laser itself would exert enough radiation pressure to accelerate the Moon

However, the actual lunar rock wouldn't evaporate that fast— for a reason that
turns out to be very important.

When a chunk of rock is vaporized, it doesn’t just disappear. The surface layer
of the Moon becomes a plasma, but that plasma would still block the path of the
beam.

Our laser would keep pouring more and more energy into the plasma, and
the plasma would keep getting hotter and hotter. The particles would bounce off
each other, slam into the surface of the Moon, and eventually blast into space at
a terrific speed.
