But wait
There’s a fact that now becomes becomes important:

You float on blood.

As the blood sphere grows, the force on your shoulder weakens . . . because
the parts of your fingertips below the surface of the blood are buoyant! Blood is
denser than flesh, and half the weight on your arm was coming from the last two
knuckles of your fingers. When the blood is a few centimeters deep, the load gets
considerably lighter.

If you could wait for the sphere of blood to get 20 centimeters deep—and if
your shoulder were intact—you might even be able to pull your arm away.

Problem: That would take five times as much blood as you have in your body.

It looks like you’re not going to make it.

Let’s rewind.
