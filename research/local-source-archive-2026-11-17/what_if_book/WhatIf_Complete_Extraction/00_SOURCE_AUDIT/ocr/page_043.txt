WHAT IF?

The typical red laser pointer is about 5 milliwatts, and
a good one would have a tight enough beam to hit the
Moon—though it’d be spread out over a large fraction
of the surface when it got there. The atmosphere would
distort the beam a bit, and absorb some of it, but most of
the light would make it.

Let’s assume everyone has steady enough aim to hit the Moon, but no more

than that, and the light spreads evenly across the surface.

Well, that’s disappointing.

It makes sense, though. Sunlight bathes the Moon in a bit over a kilowatt
of energy per square meter. Since the Moon’s cross-sectional area is around 10%
square meters, it’s bathed in about 10% watts of sunlight—1o petawatts, or 2
megawatts per person—far outshining our 5-milliwatt laser pointers. There are
varying efficiencies in each part of this system, but none of it changes that basic
equation.
