FEDEX BANDWIDTH

Q. When-if ever—will the
bandwidth of the Internet
surpass that of FedEx?

— Johan Obrink

Never underestimate the bandwidth of a station wagon full of tapes hurtling down
the highway.

—Andrew Tanenbaum, 1981

A. IF YOU WANT TO transfer a few hundred gigabytes of data, it’s generally
faster to FedEx a hard drive than to send the files over the Internet. This isn’t a
new idea— it’s often dubbed “SneakerNet”—and it’s even how Google transfers
large amounts of data internally.

But will it always be faster?

Cisco estimates that total Internet traffic currently averages 167 terabits per
second. FedEx has a fleet of 654 aircraft with a lift capacity of 26.5 million pounds
daily. A solid-state laptop drive weighs about 78 grams and can hold up to a
terabyte.

That means FedEx is capable of transferring 150 exabytes of data per day, or
14 petabits per second—almost a hundred times the current throughput of the
Internet.

If you don’t care about cost, this 1o-kilogram shoebox can hold a lot of Internet.
