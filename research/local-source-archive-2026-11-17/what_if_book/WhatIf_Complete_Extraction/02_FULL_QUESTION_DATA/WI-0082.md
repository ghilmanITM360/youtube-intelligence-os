# WI-0082: All the Lightning

**Question:** If all the lightning strikes happening in the world on any given day all happened in the same place at once, what would happen to that place?

**Submitted by:** Trevor Jones

**PDF pages:** 281–284

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

A roughly six-meter bundle of a million lightning bolts delivers about two Hiroshima bombs of energy and devastates the vicinity.

## Scientific explanation

['Ordinary lightning is energetic but too sporadic and hard to capture to be practical power. Even frequent-strike regions receive vastly more solar energy. The thought experiment forces all daily strikes into adjacent parallel channels.', 'About a million channels each1–2cm wide make a bundle about6m across. Deposited energy is comparable to two atomic bombs, creates high-energy plasma, ignites surfaces miles away and launches a building-destroying shock wave; crater size is compared to a basketball court.', 'A meter-diameter copper conductor might survive the short pulse, but ground resistance at its end creates a molten-rock explosion. A lightning rod cannot protect against this scenario, even before considering thermal radiation.', 'Alternative: collect one night’s Catatumbo lightning near Lake Maracaibo. Almost-nightly storms can flash every2seconds; hypothetical storage might power a console and plasma TV for roughly a century.']
