# WI-0066: Drain the Oceans

**Question:** How quickly would the oceans drain if a circular portal 10 meters in radius leading into space were created at the bottom of Challenger Deep, the deepest spot in the ocean? How would the Earth change as the water was being drained?

## Answer summary

One portal drains extremely slowly; with accelerated drainage, basins disconnect and retain water while continents expand and ecosystems collapse.

## Scientific explanation

The destination matters: a nearby portal would return water through Earth's atmosphere, heating it and raining it back. The author places the outlet far away, jokingly over Curiosity on Mars. Despite pressure-driven high flow, a 10 m radius opening is tiny compared with ocean volume: initial sea-level decrease is under 1 cm/day and drainage takes hundreds of thousands of years. Surface whirlpool effects are negligible because the drain is so small and deep. To examine geography, more drains are assumed. At −50 m many islands connect to nearby land; at −100 m the Grand Banks emerges and some basins cease draining once isolated. At −200 to−500 m new islands and bridges appear; at−1 km the Arctic disconnects and New Zealand grows greatly. At−2 km Caribbean/Gulf connections close; at−3 km mid-ocean-ridge peaks emerge. By−5 km most major basins have disconnected. The final map retains substantial shallow seas and several deep trenches; it is a rough bathymetric illustration, not an exact hydrological forecast. Removing about half the oceans would radically disrupt climate and cause mass extinctions; the expanding-Netherlands narrative is comic framing.
