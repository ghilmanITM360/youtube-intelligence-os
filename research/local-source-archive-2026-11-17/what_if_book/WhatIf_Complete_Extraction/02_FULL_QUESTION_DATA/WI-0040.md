# WI-0040: No More DNA

**Question:** This may be a bit gruesome, but . . . if someone’s DNA suddenly vanished, how long would that person last?

**Submitted by:** Nina Charest

**PDF pages:** 146–151

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

The author infers an initially deceptively normal interval followed by severe symptoms, immune collapse, and death within hours or days.

## Scientific explanation

Removing DNA removes only about one-third pound of mass, so mechanical consequences are slight: perhaps a small twitch as unchanged muscle force accelerates slightly lighter limbs. The biological problem is losing instructions needed for continued cell function and protein synthesis. Since no one has lost all DNA, the answer reasons by analogy. Amatoxin poisoning interrupts an enzyme involved in reading DNA and can produce apparent recovery while irreversible injury accumulates. Chemotherapy and radiation damage particularly rapidly dividing cells—bone marrow, gut lining and hair follicles. Bone-marrow failure compromises white-blood-cell production and infection defense. The proposed outcome is abdominal pain, nausea, dizziness, immune collapse, and fatal systemic infection or organ failure. The exact sequence is expressly unknown.
