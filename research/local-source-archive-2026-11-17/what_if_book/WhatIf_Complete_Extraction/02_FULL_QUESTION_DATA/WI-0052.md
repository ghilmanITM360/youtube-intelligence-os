# WI-0052: High Throw

**Question:** How high can a human throw something?

**Submitted by:** Irish Dave on the Isle of Man

**PDF pages:** 183–188

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

The author estimates around 70 m for an elite baseball throw and perhaps 80 m for a golf ball, assuming speed can be directed vertically.

## Scientific explanation

Humans are unusually accurate throwers; release timing must be tighter than nerve travel times, showing the importance of coordinated predictive movement. Because people throw forward more effectively than upward, the author allows a passive device that redirects velocity vertically without adding energy. Aerodynamic calculations for baseballs give heights in 5 m giraffe units: ordinary throw three, good arm five, 80 mph pitcher ten, and Aroldis Chapman's 105 mph pitch fourteen. A golf ball might be better: lighter mass may reduce elbow-torque limits and allow higher speed, while its flight properties support an estimated sixteen giraffes. Roald Bradstock's varied-object throwing provides empirical motivation. Tools that actively add speed are excluded; a buoyant balloon is the ending loophole.
