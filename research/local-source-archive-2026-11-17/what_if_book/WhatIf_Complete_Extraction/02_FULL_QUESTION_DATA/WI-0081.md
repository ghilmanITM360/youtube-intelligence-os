# WI-0081: Stirring Tea

**Question:** I was absentmindedly stirring a cup of hot tea, when I got to thinking, “Aren’t I actually adding kinetic energy into this cup?” I know that stirring does help to cool down the tea, but what if I were to stir it faster? Would I be able to boil a cup of water by stirring?

## Answer summary

Ordinary stirring contributes negligible heat; open-cup power and fluid limits prevent boiling by stirring, though enclosed blenders can warm contents.

## Scientific explanation

['Stirring adds kinetic energy that dissipates into heat, but water’s volumetric heat capacity is large. Heating one cup from20°C to near100°C in2minutes requires about700W, comparable to microwave input.', 'Falling water illustrates the scale: gravitational energy at Niagara yields only0.12°C of heating. Vigorous tea stirring is estimated from industrial mixing reports to add about one ten-millionth watt.', 'Stirring may bring warm deep water to the surface, disturb overlying air and warm the mug. Cited measurements found stirring, dipping and leaving tea alone cool at similar rates, with spoon dipping slightly faster.', 'Heating faster requires roughly a horsepower. Slowing the heating increases losses. At tens of thousands of stirs/second, cavitation undermines coupling and splashing raises surface area, rapidly cooling the tea. A footnote acknowledges enclosed blenders can heat by mechanical dissipation.']
