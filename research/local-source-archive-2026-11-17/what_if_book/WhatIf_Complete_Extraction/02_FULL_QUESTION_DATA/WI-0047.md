# WI-0047: Everybody Out

**Question:** Is there enough energy to move the entire current human population off-planet?

**Submitted by:** Adam

**PDF pages:** 168–171

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

The ideal energy is manageable compared with annual world energy, but propellant and transport systems make mass evacuation implausible.

## Scientific explanation

The source starts from the minimum gravitational escape energy of a 65 kg person, about 4 GJ. Across seven billion people this is described as roughly 8 PWh or 5% of annual world energy. Real rockets carry fuel to lift fuel, producing exponential mass ratios via the Tsiolkovsky equation. For escape delta-v over 13 km/s and chemical exhaust at most about 4.5 km/s, ideal mass ratios already approach 20, and practical ships need 20–50 tonnes fuel per tonne. Food, water, vehicles, launch logistics and production raise the burden. Alternative concepts include a space elevator, whose tether needs unavailable strength, and nuclear pulse propulsion (Project Orion), which could in principle loft enormous masses but introduces immense engineering and environmental risks. No destination or sustainable off-world habitat is solved.
