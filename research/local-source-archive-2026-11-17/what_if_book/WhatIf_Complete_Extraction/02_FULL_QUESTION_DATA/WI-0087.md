# WI-0087: SAT Guessing

**Question:** What if everyone who took the SAT guessed on every multiple-choice question? How many perfect scores would there be?

**Submitted by:** Rob Balder

**PDF pages:** 293–294

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

Effectively none: all158 independent five-choice answers correct has probability about1/(2.7×10^110).

## Scientific explanation

['The2014 SAT has44math,67critical-reading and47writing multiple-choice questions. Non-multiple-choice items and essays are assumed correct to isolate guessing. Each independent uniform guess succeeds with probability1/5.', 'Multiplying the probabilities yields5^-158≈1/(2.7×10^110). Four million17-year-olds would almost certainly produce no perfect result even on any one section.', 'Even if each person took a million tests daily for5billion years, a perfect math section would remain about0.0001% likely. Lightning comparisons make the improbability intuitive, without altering the mathematical result.']
