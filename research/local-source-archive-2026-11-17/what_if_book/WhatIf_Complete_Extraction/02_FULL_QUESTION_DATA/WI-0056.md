# WI-0056: Speed Bump

**Question:** How fast can you hit a speed bump while driving and live?

**Submitted by:** Myrlin Barber

**PDF pages:** 194–197

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

A small bump's direct jolt can be survivable at surprisingly high speed, but vehicle damage, loss of control and aerodynamic lift become decisive.

## Scientific explanation

The answer distinguishes an isolated bump impulse from the resulting crash. Tires and suspension absorb many small-bump shocks; even if components fail, the occupant need not suffer a fatal vertical jolt. Typical bumps of 3–4 inches are comparable to tire cushioning, so the rim may not strike. Documented injuries disproportionately involve hard bus seats and bad roads. Larger humps or speed tables differ. At artificially increased car speeds, drag rises with speed squared and lift can overcome weight. A typical sedan might lift off and tumble around 150–300 mph before reaching the bump. Holding it down would expose body panels to destructive winds; still faster motion leads to heating and disassembly. The final 0.9c speeding-ticket calculation is a joke, not a survivable scenario.
