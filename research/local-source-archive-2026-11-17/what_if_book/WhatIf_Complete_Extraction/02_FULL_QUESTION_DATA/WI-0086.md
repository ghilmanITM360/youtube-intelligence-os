# WI-0086: Raindrop

**Question:** What if a rainstorm dropped all of its water in a single giant drop?

**Submitted by:** Michael McNeill

**PDF pages:** 289–292

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

A kilometer-scale drop from a large storm crashes down and spreads as a destructive supersonic wall of water.

## Scientific explanation

['Total precipitable water is the equivalent depth from condensing the atmospheric column. For a100km×100km storm with6cm TPW, water volume is0.6km³ and mass600million tons; condensing it produces a sphere over1km across.', 'The sphere forms a couple kilometers up. Around10seconds it emerges beneath the cloud, moving90m/s; air strips spray and foams the leading surface. Given enough fall distance it would disperse, but the ground is too near.', 'About20seconds after formation the edge strikes at over200m/s. Compressed air briefly heats intensely; then impact creates pressures above Mariana Trench conditions and forces water sideways against bedrock as a supersonic radial jet.', 'Everything within several kilometers is scoured to bedrock. Structures are destroyed20–30km out; terrain begins shielding regions and redirecting water into valleys. Downstream flash floods extend hundreds of kilometers over the following hours. A swimmer in the drop feels little until pressure builds and impact becomes fatal.']
