# WI-0046: Falling with Helium

**Question:** What if I jumped out of an airplane with a couple of tanks of helium and one huge, uninflated balloon? Then, while falling, I release the helium and fill the balloon. How long of a fall would I need in order for the balloon to slow me enough that I could land safely?

## Answer summary

Plausible in principle with enough rapid inflation; two portable tanks are inadequate, and higher release altitude does not remove the timing problem.

## Scientific explanation

A large balloon provides drag even with air, acting as a parachute, but an air-filled version would need 10–20 m diameter and impractical tank volume. Helium adds buoyancy so a smaller balloon can help, still huge by party standards. The largest consumer rental tanks described hold about 250 cubic feet; at least ten are needed to supply enough helium to support a person. Dense, smooth cylinders raise terminal speed and leave only minutes to inflate. Empty cylinders can be dropped. Starting higher does not proportionally extend useful time because thin upper air lets the assembly accelerate until reaching denser lower atmosphere. Connecting many cylinders simultaneously might inflate fast enough. Overinflation can produce unintended ascent rather than landing.
