# WI-0070: Longest Sunset

**Question:** What is the longest possible sunset you can experience while driving, assuming we are obeying the speed limit and driving on paved roads?

**Submitted by:** Michael Berg

**PDF pages:** 243–247

## Answer summary

About 95 minutes by following the slowly turning Arctic day–night boundary on suitable northern roads.

## Scientific explanation

['Sunset is defined from first contact with the idealized horizon until the Sun fully disappears, disallowing a touch-and-rise path or obstruction behind hills. Average atmospheric refraction is included.', 'Latitude and seasonal geometry alter the crossing angle. Equatorial equinox sunsets last a little over two minutes, London about 200–300 seconds, and the South Pole’s annual sunset about 38–40 hours; paved-road requirements rule out the poles.', 'Longyearbyen offers a stationary sunset just under an hour, but its small road network adds little. The northern mainland road limit is near E69 in Norway; trying to chase the Sun westward still requires about half the speed of sound and the road runs north–south.', 'Instead choose a date when the terminator barely reaches the observer. Drive north as it approaches to remain slightly ahead, then turn south rapidly enough to cross into darkness before it retreats. PyEphem and Norwegian highway GPS searches produced roughly95 minutes across many Arctic routes and speeds.']
