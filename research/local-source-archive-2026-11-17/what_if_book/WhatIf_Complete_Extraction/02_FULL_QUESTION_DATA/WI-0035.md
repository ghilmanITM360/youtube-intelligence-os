# WI-0035: Common Cold

**Question:** If everyone on the planet stayed away from each other for a couple of weeks, wouldn’t the common cold be wiped out?

**Submitted by:** Sarah Ewart

**PDF pages:** 129–133

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

Isolation could clear rhinoviruses in healthy people, but long infections in immunocompromised hosts would preserve reservoirs.

## Scientific explanation

Rhinoviruses reproduce in nose and throat cells; immune response produces many symptoms and later strain-specific immunity. Restricting hosts can extinguish local circulation, illustrated by St. Kilda's recurrent stranger's cough after ships arrived, followed by local disappearance. Healthy people usually clear infection in about ten days, and the consulted virologist reports no animal reservoir or persistent infection after normal clearance. However, severe immune suppression can allow weeks, months, or conceivably years of infection. Only a few remaining infections could restart global transmission. The logistics themselves are severe: economic interruption, food allocation and placing people on inhospitable land. Possible benefits and harms of childhood rhinovirus exposure are acknowledged rather than resolved.
