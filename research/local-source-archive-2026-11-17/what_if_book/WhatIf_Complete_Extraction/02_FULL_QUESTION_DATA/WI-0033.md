# WI-0033: Steak Drop

**Question:** From what height would you need to drop a steak for it to be cooked when it hit the ground?

**Submitted by:** Alex Lahey

**PDF pages:** 122–126

## Answer summary

Reentry can sear or ablate the surface, but the interior stays raw and the long cold final descent can freeze the steak.

## Scientific explanation

The author simulates an 8-ounce steak using hockey-puck drag and missile-reentry heating research, then compares heating duration with cookbook guidance. Compressive heating becomes important above roughly Mach 2. A steak released from rest accelerates until it enters thicker atmosphere at roughly 30–50 km, then slows to terminal speed. Regardless of initial height, the final 25 km takes six or seven minutes in mostly subfreezing air. A 39 km release is subsonic; about 50 km is needed to break the sound barrier. At 70 km the steak briefly encounters air at about 350°F, insufficient to cook it. At 100 km it spends around 90 seconds above Mach 2, but heating is soon replaced by cold airflow. At 250 km it can reach Mach 6 and sear outside, while remaining raw inside. Higher starts cause charring and ablation; sustained exposure removes material rather than producing a cooked intact steak. A Pittsburgh-rare result is the closest outcome.
