# WI-0083: Loneliest Human

**Question:** What is the farthest one human being has ever been from every other living person? Were they lonely?

**Submitted by:** Bryan J McCarter

**PDF pages:** 285–287

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

Six Apollo command-module pilots have the strongest documented claim at about3,585km from other humans; Collins and Worden described solitude without loneliness.

## Scientific explanation

['While two astronauts stood on the Moon, the command-module pilot could orbit the far side at about3,585km from them. Candidates are Mike Collins, Dick Gordon, Stu Roosa, Al Worden, Ken Mattingly and Ron Evans.', 'Ancient Polynesian voyagers or unknown shipwreck survivors might theoretically exceed that isolation, but evidence is lacking. Antarctic explorers and Pierre François Péron on Île Amsterdam came close yet were within range of other inhabited places.', 'No definitive all-history record can be guaranteed. Collins’s account distinguishes isolation from abandonment, and Worden explicitly enjoyed being alone and free of radio conversation. Physical separation does not imply subjective loneliness.']
