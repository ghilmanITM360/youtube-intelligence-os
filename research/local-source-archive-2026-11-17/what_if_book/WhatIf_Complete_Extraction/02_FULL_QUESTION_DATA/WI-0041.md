# WI-0041: Interplanetary Cessna

**Question:** What would happen if you tried to fly a normal Earth airplane above different solar system bodies?

**Submitted by:** Glen Chiacchieri

**PDF pages:** 152–156

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

Most worlds lack useful air; Titan is aerodynamically favorable but demands heating and life support.

## Scientific explanation

A Cessna 172 is converted to electric drive because most worlds lack oxygen for combustion. The 32-body chart mostly shows ballistic crashes. Only Earth, Mars, Venus, four gas giants, Titan and the Sun have atmospheres substantial enough to discuss. The Sun vaporizes the craft. Mars's thin air requires near-sonic flight; a 1 km drop cannot build enough speed to recover, while a 4–5 km drop might allow an unsurvivably fast glide. Venus's dense lower atmosphere supplies lift at jogging speed but melts and degrades the aircraft; at 55 km temperature and pressure are more suitable, but acid corrosion and hurricane winds remain. Jupiter's gravity demands more power and leads to a crushing descent; Saturn is somewhat less demanding, Uranus briefly more favorable, and Neptune offers similar cold/turbulence problems. Titan combines dense air with low gravity, allowing pedal-powered aircraft or human-powered wings, but its 72 K environment rapidly removes heat. With insulation and heat sources the author considers flight feasible.
