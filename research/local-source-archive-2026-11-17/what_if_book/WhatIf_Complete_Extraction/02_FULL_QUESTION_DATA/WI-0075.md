# WI-0075: Expanding Earth

**Question:** How long would it take for people to notice their weight gain if the mean radius of the world expanded by 1cm every second? (Assuming the average composition of rock were maintained.)

**Submitted by:** Dennis O’Donnell

**PDF pages:** 252–258

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

Weight change becomes noticeable over months to a year, while infrastructure and satellite systems fail earlier; decades of growth make Earth uninhabitable.

## Scientific explanation

['All Earth material expands uniformly while retaining composition/density; oceans also expand, human structures do not. Constant1cm/s surface motion produces only an initial acceleration jolt. Mass scales as radius cubed and surface gravity as mass/radius squared, giving gravity proportional to radius in the first approximation.', 'One day adds864m radius with tiny weight change. Circumference grows by2π times the radius increment, so daily strain initially resembles thermal expansion. Satellite timing is disrupted within hours, while pendulum clocks run fast as gravity increases.', 'After a month: radius+26km, mass+1.2%, gravity+0.4%; long concrete structures crack and some bridges fail. ISS faces rising atmosphere plus orbital shrinkage and needs evacuation. At one year gravity is about5% stronger and broad infrastructure failure is clear.', 'Atmospheric assumptions diverge: fixed atmospheric inventory spreads over more area and pressure falls despite heavier air; expanding atmosphere raises pressure. At5 years gravity+25%;10 years+50%;40 years about3g causes difficulty walking/breathing, crop/tree collapse and landslides.', 'Growing radioactive inventory relative to surface area increases internal heat flux and tectonic activity. At100 years over6g defeats circulation and mobility; water support might help mechanically but breathing and oxygen toxicity become fatal without pressure control.', 'Constant-density growth eventually becomes inconsistent with self-compression; adding mass could cause collapse toward compact objects rather than expansion. After several centuries, shrinking lunar orbit and expanding Earth bring the Moon within the Roche limit, creating temporary rings.']
