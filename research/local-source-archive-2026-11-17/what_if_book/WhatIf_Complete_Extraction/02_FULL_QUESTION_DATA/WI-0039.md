# WI-0039: Alien Astronomers

**Question:** Let’s assume there’s life on the nearest habitable exoplanet and that they have technology comparable to ours. If they looked at our star right now, what would they see?

**Submitted by:** Chuck H

**PDF pages:** 141–145

## Answer summary

Probably a point of light; biosignatures from Earth's atmosphere are more promising than accidental human radio leakage.

## Scientific explanation

Broadcast radio attenuates over interstellar distances and is economically wasteful when radiated away from audiences. Cable, fiber and focused cellular networks reduce leakage. Cold War early-warning radar was stronger and could be detected by a lucky listener in a beam; the source says these systems too became quieter. Arecibo's radar is described as the strongest terrestrial radio beam, but it is narrow and intermittent, so a chance interception gives a brief pulse rather than a continuing program. Visible reflected Earthshine and atmospheric transmission could reveal water-cycle properties and oxygen, indicating life without identifying humans. Algae have changed atmospheric composition for billions of years. Deliberate probes could carry a clearer message but would take tens of millennia and require durable guidance; failing to brake supplies the final impact joke.
