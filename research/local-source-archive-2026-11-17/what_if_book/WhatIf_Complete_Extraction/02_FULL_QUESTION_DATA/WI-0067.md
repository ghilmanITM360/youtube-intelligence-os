# WI-0067: Drain the Oceans: Part II

**Question:** Supposing you did drain the oceans, and dumped the water on top of the Curiosity rover, how would Mars change as the water accumulated?

**Submitted by:** Iain

**PDF pages:** 225–231

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

Earth’s transferred oceans flood nearly all Mars, leaving volcanic islands; the water eventually freezes.

## Scientific explanation

['Continuation of the Mariana Trench portal scenario. The outlet is above Curiosity in Gale Crater. Mars normally cannot retain liquid surface water because it is cold and its atmosphere is thin; a large, rapid flow a few degrees above 0°C is assumed to outrun freezing, boiling and sublimation.', 'Gale Crater fills around Mount Sharp. Curiosity is submerged, Mount Sharp becomes an island, and water spills over the northern rim before the peak disappears. Outflow crosses the surface and pools in the North Polar Basin.', 'Filling the northern lowlands initially leaves much equatorial terrain dry. Because Mars is smaller than Earth, Earth’s ocean volume produces a deeper global sea. Water enters Valles Marineris, covers Spirit and Opportunity, then reaches the Hellas impact basin. High terrain fragments into islands.', 'At the end, Olympus Mons and several other volcanoes remain well above sea level. Noctis Labyrinthus produces intricate islands. Possible transient greenhouse warming cannot prevent eventual freezing, dust burial and migration into polar permafrost.']
