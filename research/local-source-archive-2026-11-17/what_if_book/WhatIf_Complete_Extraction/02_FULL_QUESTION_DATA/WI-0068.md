# WI-0068: Twitter

**Question:** How many unique English tweets are possible? How long would it take for the population of the world to read them all out loud?

**Submitted by:** Eric H, Hopatcong, NJ

**PDF pages:** 232–236

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

About 2×10^46 meaningfully different 140-character English tweets; reading them is vastly beyond planetary lifetimes.

## Scientific explanation

['Counting raw strings gives 27^140 for letters plus spaces, or potentially vastly more Unicode strings, but most are meaningless. Restricting to grammar alone does not settle meaningful distinctness: unfamiliar names illustrate the ambiguity.', 'A language with only two allowed sentences carries one bit per message regardless of length. Information measures receiver uncertainty. Shannon’s experiments asked people to predict the next letter of truncated English passages; predictability yields about 1.0–1.2 bits per letter.', 'Applying 1.1 bits per character to 140 characters gives 2^(140×1.1), about 2×10^46 distinguishable messages. Message-length and unicity-distance issues make this an order-of-magnitude argument.', 'Reading the messages would take one person nearly 10^47 seconds. Even a billion readers cannot meaningfully reduce the task within Earth’s lifetime. A fanciful bird removes a few dust particles from a hundred-mile mountain once per millennium; one mountain’s erosion is an eternal day of 10^32 years. Reading 16 hours daily takes roughly ten thousand eternal years.']
