# WI-0076: Weightless Arrow

**Question:** Assuming a zero-gravity environment with an atmosphere identical to Earth’s, how long would it take the friction of air to stop an arrow fired from a bow? Would it eventually come to a standstill and hover in midair?

**Submitted by:** Mark Estano

**PDF pages:** 259–262

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

It slows quickly at first, then drifts for hours and several kilometers; a real ISS shot meets a wall first.

## Scientific explanation

['With gravity removed, quadratic air drag dominates initial motion. An85m/s arrow slows to25m/s in10 seconds after400m; after5 minutes it travels roughly a mile and slows to walking pace.', 'At ordinary speeds drag mainly reflects momentum transferred to displaced air. As speed falls far enough, viscous effects become dominant and the arrow gradually approaches rest. The precise low-speed airflow depends strongly on arrow shape.', 'In ideal relatively still air, total range is at least several kilometers and perhaps5–10km. Actual sustained weightless breathable space is confined: ISS Kibo is about10m long, so an actual experiment ends at a boundary.']
