# WI-0058: Orbital Speed

**Question:** What if a spacecraft slowed down on reentry to just a few miles per hour using rocket boosters like the Mars sky crane? Would it negate the need for a heat shield?

**Submitted by:** Brian

**PDF pages:** 202–206

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

Slow entry would reduce heating, but carrying enough fuel to cancel orbital speed is the central obstacle.

## Scientific explanation

Space is only about 100 km away, but reaching orbit means moving sideways around 8 km/s. Gravity in low orbit remains about 90% of surface gravity. Most launch energy goes into speed, not altitude. Rocket propellant grows exponentially with required velocity change; carrying fuel to cancel orbital speed greatly increases launch mass. Atmospheric braking with a heat shield is therefore practical. Curiosity's sky crane does not contradict this: atmospheric braking removed most of its arrival speed before rockets hovered near the surface. The chapter illustrates 8 km/s with ISS orbital period, rifle-bullet comparisons and a song-duration distance coincidence.
