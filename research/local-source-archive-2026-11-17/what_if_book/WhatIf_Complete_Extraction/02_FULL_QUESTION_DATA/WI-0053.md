# WI-0053: Lethal Neutrinos

**Question:** How close would you have to be to a supernova to get a lethal dose of neutrino radiation?

**Submitted by:** Dr. Donald Spector

**PDF pages:** 189–192

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

About a few astronomical units in the source model—probably inside the progenitor's outer layers.

## Scientific explanation

Neutrinos interact so weakly that enormous solar flux passes through the body and Earth with almost no interaction. Core-collapse supernovae produce enough of them that the rare interactions become significant close by. Citing Andrew Karam, the source uses a neutrino dose of 0.5 nSv at 1 parsec. Inverse-square scaling to a several-sievert fatal dose gives roughly 2.3 AU. That is just beyond the Sun–Mars distance and may lie inside a giant progenitor star. Other supernova effects would already be fatal; the scenario isolates neutrino damage conceptually. The text also uses a vivid brightness comparison: a supernova at solar distance outshines a hydrogen bomb against the eyeball by nine orders of magnitude.
