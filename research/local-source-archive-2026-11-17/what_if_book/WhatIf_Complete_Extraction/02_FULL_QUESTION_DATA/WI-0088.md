# WI-0088: Neutron Bullet

**Question:** If a bullet with the density of a neutron star were fired from a handgun (ignoring the how) at the Earth’s surface, would the Earth be destroyed?

**Submitted by:** Charlotte Ainsworth

**PDF pages:** 295–303

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

No: a magically stable dense bullet sinks through Earth to its core; near-surface gravity is locally dangerous but does not destroy the planet.

## Scientific explanation

['A bullet of neutron-star density has roughly the mass of the Empire State Building. Actual neutron-star material cannot remain stable outside its star; it would expand explosively. The chapter therefore uses magically stable material of equivalent density.', 'Dropped or fired, it pulls nearby dirt upward, punches through crust and vaporizes mantle along its route, leaving a plasma trail. It eventually lodges at Earth’s core; large human-scale energy has little planetary consequence. Its gravitational effect remains local, and the passage seals behind it.', 'A separate hypothetical supports it on an infinitely strong pedestal and city-block-size load-spreading foundation. It does not vacuum up the atmosphere; compression and warming are modest outside the immediate neighborhood. Approaching feels like walking down an increasingly steep slope as gravity becomes strongly nonuniform.', 'Unprotected contact produces enormous differential forces and pressure; a restrained person would still suffer catastrophic injury and blood loss. Accumulated liquid eventually provides buoyancy, but the necessary volume exceeds a person’s blood supply.', 'Surrounding the bullet with1–2m of water changes the situation: water and tissue experience similar acceleration, and buoyancy counteracts weight. Extremely close approach depends on matching densities, notionally using salt to increase water density or alcohol to reduce it. Decompression effects remain a caveat; this is an impossible-material thought experiment, not a real procedure.']
