# WI-0080: Sunset on the British Empire

**Question:** When (if ever) did the Sun finally set on the British Empire?

**Submitted by:** Kurt Amundson

**PDF pages:** 274–276

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

As of source publication it had not: the Pitcairn Islands fill the final daylight gap among British territories.

## Scientific explanation

['Continuous imperial daylight likely began with Australian acquisitions in the late1700s or early1800s, but assigning an exact date is difficult because colonial claims were arbitrary.', 'Fourteen overseas territories are counted separately from independent Commonwealth states sharing a monarch. Around midnight GMT, the Cayman Islands lose daylight before British Indian Ocean Territory sees sunrise after1am. Pitcairn alone covers that interval.', 'If those borders persist, near-term eclipses do not end continuous daylight. The April2432 total eclipse on Pitcairn occurs while Cayman and even London are sunlit. No eclipse over the next millennium reaches Pitcairn at the necessary time; an appropriately timed eclipse eventually could.']
