# WI-0078: Updating a Printed Wikipedia

**Question:** If you had a printed version of the whole of (say, the English) Wikipedia, how many printers would you need in order to keep up with the changes made to the live version?

**Submitted by:** Marein Könings

**PDF pages:** 267–269

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

About six constantly running inkjet printers at source-era edit rates, with enormous ink expense and paper accumulation.

## Scientific explanation

['Rather than estimating words changed, assume each edit entails replacing one page; multipage changes and reverts roughly balance. At90–100 edits/minute and15 pages/minute per printer, about six printers keep pace.', 'Using Rob Matthews’s featured-article book and average lengths, the author estimates the full English Wikipedia printout at300m³. Keeping up with edits generates this same paper volume every month.', 'Electricity, printers and staffing are smaller costs than consumables. Paper costs roughly$1,000/day; ink at5–30cents/page yields four or five figures/day, approaching half a million dollars in a month or two. Laser printing is preferable; the chapter ultimately recommends digital.']
