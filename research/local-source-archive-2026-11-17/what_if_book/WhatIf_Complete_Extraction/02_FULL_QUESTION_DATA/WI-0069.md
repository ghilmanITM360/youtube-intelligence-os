# WI-0069: Lego Bridge

**Question:** How many Lego bricks would it take to build a bridge capable of carrying traffic from London to New York? Have that many Lego bricks been manufactured?

**Submitted by:** Jerry Petersen

**PDF pages:** 237–242

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

Enough bricks exist for a thin connection, but a traffic-bearing ocean bridge overwhelms available supply and practical engineering.

## Scientific explanation

['A two-layer narrow connection needs 350 million bricks across 700 million studs. Existing production exceeds 400 billion pieces, but perhaps only 5–10 billion are standard 2×4 bricks. That is sufficient for a toy-width line, not a roadway.', 'A floating design avoids three-mile-deep supports. ABS is denser than water and assembled bricks leak; exterior sealant traps air so the structure becomes buoyant. A meter-thick, five-meter-wide bridge could support cars in still water.', 'Overlapping thin plates provide strong joints, with tensile strength compared to concrete. Ocean currents, waves and wind deflect the middle of an enormous span and generate intolerable tension. Seafloor tethers could limit drift, but open-ocean wave clearance requires a taller, wider structure with added buoyancy and anchors.', 'Floats increase drag, cable loads push the bridge downward, and more floats are needed: the design trends toward seabed support. Deep pressure collapses air pockets, requiring a massive causeway. Such a barrier blocks North Atlantic circulation and crosses the spreading Mid-Atlantic Ridge, requiring expansion joints.', 'Even the simpler steel-tethered design costs over $5 trillion in material. The chapter contrasts this with buying London real estate and shipping it to New York as a humorous cheaper connection.']
