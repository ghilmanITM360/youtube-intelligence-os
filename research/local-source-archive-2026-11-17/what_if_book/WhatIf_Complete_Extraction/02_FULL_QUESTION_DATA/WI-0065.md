# WI-0065: Sparta

**Question:** In the movie 300 they shoot arrows up into the sky and they seemingly blot out the sun. Is this possible, and how many arrows would it take?

**Submitted by:** Anna Newell

**PDF pages:** 215–218

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

Ordinary archery cannot darken an overhead Sun; implausible firing rates or low-Sun geometry are required.

## Scientific explanation

At eight to ten arrows per minute and about three seconds over the battlefield, only half the archers have an arrow aloft. Each arrow shadows roughly 40 cm², so average area per archer is only 20 cm². Dense ranks still block about 0.1% of overhead sunlight. Packing archers more closely, doubling depth and granting a cinematic seven arrows in eight seconds reaches only 1.56%. Arrow-firing Gatling guns at 70/s yield enough summed area to exceed ground area, but overlapping shadows leave about a third of light; visual logarithmic response further reduces perceived dimming. About 300 arrows/s per weapon is needed for 99% blockage in the scenario. Alternatively a near-horizon Sun sends light along the entire arrow cloud, multiplying optical depth; dawn geometry is easier, though the arrows then need not be aimed at the enemy.
