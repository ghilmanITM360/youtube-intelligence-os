# WI-0061: FedEx Bandwidth

**Question:** When—if ever—will the bandwidth of the Internet surpass that of FedEx?

**Submitted by:** Johan Öbrink

**PDF pages:** 207–209

**Status:** VISUALLY_REVIEWED; SOURCE_OCR_NEEDS_PROOFREAD

## Answer summary

Bulk transport of dense storage can outperform network throughput, but its latency is terrible; the comparison depends on changing storage and network technology.

## Scientific explanation

The author compares daily aircraft payload capacity with data density. FedEx's fleet filled with terabyte drives could move about 150 EB/day or 14 Pb/s, almost 100 times the source-era Internet traffic estimate. MicroSD cards improve density to roughly 160 TB/kg, taking the hypothetical fleet to 177 Pb/s or 2 ZB/day. Holding storage density fixed and growing Internet traffic 29% annually would reach that level around 2040, but storage also improves. Fiber clusters could already technically beat the fleet if sufficiently multiplied, and including all US freight raises physical data throughput further. Thus the claim is about hypothetical aggregate bulk capacity, not useful instantaneous connectivity; day-scale delivery produces huge ping times.
