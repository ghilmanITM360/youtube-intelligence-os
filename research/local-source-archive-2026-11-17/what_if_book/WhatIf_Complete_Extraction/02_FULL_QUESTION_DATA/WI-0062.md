# WI-0062: Free Fall

**Question:** What place on Earth would allow you to free-fall the longest by jumping off it? What about using a squirrel suit?

## Answer summary

Mount Thor supplies the largest purely vertical cliff drop; gliding and wingsuits substantially extend airtime and open different terrain.

## Scientific explanation

The source identifies Canada's Mount Thor as the largest purely vertical drop. With terminal speed around 55 m/s and an initial acceleration distance of a few hundred meters, falling takes a little over 26 seconds. For comparison, Ireland's 210 m Cliffs of Moher gives about eight seconds. A skilled skydiver can glide nearly 45 degrees, moving away from the cliff and gaining additional descent distance; clothes and terrain complicate records. Wingsuit tracking shows descent as slow as 18 m/s, extending a Mount Thor-scale fall beyond a minute even without horizontal gains. Mountains such as Nanga Parbat offer more than 3 km of sloping descent; thin air increases glide speed and requires oxygen. The source records Dean Potter's Eiger wingsuit BASE flight at 3 min 20 s, then adds a competitive-eating timing joke.
