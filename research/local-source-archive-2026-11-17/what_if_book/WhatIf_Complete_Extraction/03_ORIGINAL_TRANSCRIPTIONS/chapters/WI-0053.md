The reason you don’ notice the neutrino flood is that neutrinos mostly ignore
ordinary matter. On average, out of that massive flood, only one neutrino will
“hit” an atom in your body every few years.

In fact, neutrinos are so shadowy that the entire Earth is transparent to them;
nearly all of the Sun’s neutrino steam goes straight through it unaffected. To
detect neutrinos, people build giant tanks filled with hundreds of tons of target
material in the hopes that they'll register the impact of a single solar neutrino.

This means that when a particle accelerator (which produces neutrinos) wants
to send a neutrino beam to a detector somewhere else in the world, all it has to
do is point the beam at the detector—even if it’s on the other side of the Earth!
