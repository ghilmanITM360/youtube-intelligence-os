## Script

What would happen if the Moon were replaced with an equivalently-massed black hole? And what would a lunar (or "holar"?) eclipse look like?

A black hole the mass of the Moon would have an event horizon about the size of a sand grain.

Since the Moon's mass and position wouldn't change, the Earth’s motion and tides wouldn't change, either.

Our black hole Moon would be pretty low-profile. If it were much smaller, it would evaporate through Hawking radiation, but a black hole the mass of the Moon actually absorbs more energy from the cosmic background radiation than it emits through the Hawking mechanism. Our black hole would really be black.

At least, if it didn't eat anything. If the black hole devoured any objects, it would let off a tremendous blast of radiation. Black hole accretion disks are one of the most efficient ways to convert mass into energy in the universe.

Fortunately, there's not a lot out there for the black hole moon to eat, so it wouldn't glow very brightly for now.

It would spend most of its time drastically altering the orbits of nearby dust particles –one sand grain pushing other sand grains around.

All in all, the conversion of the Moon to a black hole might not be that big of a deal.

Unless, of course, it happened on a certain day in 1969, in which case the famous quote might read a little differently.

Ps – you might now be wondering what would happen if the moon were replaced with a black hole of the same size, not the same mass. Well, a moon-sized black hole would have around 600 times the mass of the sun: so we, and the Sun, would definitely fall into it – a total, final, lunar eclipse.
