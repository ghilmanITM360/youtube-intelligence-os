This question comes from Doug, who asks, "If you were in a boat directly over the Mariana Trench and dropped a 7-kg bowling ball over the side, how long would it take to hit bottom?" It's a good thing you mentioned the mass because of a very surprising fact. Most bowling balls float.

Bowling balls all have about the same volume, so they all displace the same amount of seawater, 12.13 lb or about 5 and 1/2 kg. But, the weights of the balls themselves vary substantially from as little as 6 lb to a maximum of 16.

Only the balls that weigh more than 12.13 lb will sink. "Hang on a moment," the serious bowlers are saying. "Most bowling balls are at the heavy end of that range. The average ball surely sinks." This is definitely true for serious bowlers, but for the overall population, it might be better to define the average ball using the distribution of ball weights you might find at the rack at the local bowling alley. So, I sifted through a bunch of online photos taken at bowling alleys and tallied up the weights visible on the balls. The average was 10 and 1/2 lb. For most people, the average bowling ball they picked up in their lives probably floats. If nothing else, this makes the popular simile, "He sank like a bowling ball," seem a little poorly chosen. But, Doug's bowling ball is 7 kg or 15 and 1/2 lb, making it plenty heavy enough to sink in the ocean. How fast will it fall? The speed of a bowling ball falling through water is driven by both the drag equation and the ball's apparent weight. For Doug's bowling ball, the terminal velocity will be roughly 1.3 m/s. The Mariana Trench is 11 km deep, so the ball will take about 2 hours and 20 minutes to reach the bottom. A 13-lb bowling ball, which is much closer to neutrally buoyant in seawater, would take over 4 and 1/2 hours to reach the bottom. On the other hand, a bowling ball made of solid iron would reach the bottom in 1/2 an hour. A bowling ball made of lead would reach the bottom in 24 minutes, and one made of solid gold would make it in 18.

However, a bowling ball made of solid gold would weigh more than the average bowler. It would also be worth over $10 million, so I'm not sure why you're throwing it in the Mariana Trench. A bowling ball made of solid osmium, the heaviest naturally occurring element, would weigh over 250 lb and could sink to the bottom of the Mariana Trench in just 16 minutes. But, what if the bowling ball you were using was the average 10.5 lb bowling ball, then the ball would never reach the bottom.
