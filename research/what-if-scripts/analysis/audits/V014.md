## G. Emotion

Curiosity and wonder; **delight** through the map-aesthetics subplot (the warmest personal beat in batch 2); playful dark humour about the rovers; and an open, contemplative ending (Fn., Medium).

## H. Pacing

Moderate, with a slow, technical opening (¶6–10) and a faster middle cascade. At 3.8 w/s it is among the densest narrations so far. The late long-term section (¶32–35) slows again.

## I. Payoffs

Micro: it won't freeze solid (¶10). Mini: Curiosity is buried, and rockbergs (¶13–14). Major: the Hellas breakthrough (¶25). Aesthetic payoff: the A+ map (¶28). Mega: Olympus Mons above the sea (¶31). Ultimate: the oceans freeze, *or* they don't (¶34–36). The promise is delivered. The final payoff is deliberately incomplete.

## J. Conclusion and CTA

No CTA in the subscribe sense. The ending, "only one way to find out", is an **implied action prompt** with no direction attached (Obs., High). It works as a curiosity close rather than a request.

## K. Strengths and weaknesses

- **Strengths.** (1) A serial callback that builds a franchise. (2) The narrator's taste as a comic subplot. (3) An honest, uncertain ending.
- **Opportunities.** (1) Front-loaded physics (¶6–9). (2) Visual dependence (¶8, ¶11). (3) The open ending may feel unresolved to some viewers (Speculative).
- **Distinctive.** The map-aesthetics running commentary.
- **Risk.** The technical opening (Speculative).
- **Lesson.** The narrator's personal opinion can be a subplot with its own arc.

## L. Formula

1. Question. 2. Callback to a previous episode. 3. Raise the physical objection and defeat it. 4. Flood basin by basin with one weird-physics beat each. 5. A narrator-opinion subplot, from boring to beautiful. 6. Long-term fate. 7. Overturn it with genuine uncertainty and leave it open.
