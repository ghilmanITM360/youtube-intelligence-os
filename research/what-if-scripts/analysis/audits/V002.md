## B. Opening hook forensics

- **Obs. (High).** ¶1–2 give the attributed question (Alex) as three stacked magnitudes, 15, then 0, then 25. ¶3 immediately answers the most extreme case with a shock: magnitude 25 would destroy the Sun. ¶4 is a self-interrupt ("getting ahead of ourselves") that pulls back to the basics.
- **Hook types.** Question Hook, plus Shock / Unexpected-Fact flash-forward, plus Self-interrupt.
- **Construction.** The script **flash-forwards to the most extreme payoff, then rewinds**. The viewer now knows the destination, which is cosmic destruction, and waits to see how the scale gets there (Fn., Medium).
- **Withheld.** What magnitude 15 actually does (it arrives at ¶11), and whether a magnitude 0 exists (¶20–22).
- **Weakness.** The flash-forward spends the biggest number, 25, early. The script recovers by reusing it as the ceiling at ¶18 (Obs.).

## D. Narrative spine

Hook (¶1–3) → Rewind and setup: the scale is unbounded (¶5–7) → Practical ceiling: a magnitude 9 changes the day length, and 10–11 need planet-sized faults (¶8–10) → Ascent: 15 evaporates the oceans (¶11) → 18 is Earth's binding energy, a Death Star joke (¶12–14) → 24 is the Sun's maximum, compared to Jupiter full of H-bombs (¶15–17) → 25 explodes the Sun, closing the flash-forward (¶18) → **Pivot**: "Hold on…" (¶19) → Descent: 0 exists (¶20–23) → Comic catalogue from 1 down to −15 (¶25–33) → Tonal button (¶34).

The structure is a **two-way scale ladder**, up and then down. Each rung is one comparison. The pivot at ¶19 restarts momentum with a second, inverted ladder (Obs., High).

## G. Emotion

The first half runs on awe and dread through escalating destruction. The second half runs on playful relief (cats, pennies, feathers). The emotional arc **inverts at ¶19**, from cosmic catastrophe to cosy domestic scenes. The strongest beat is the relief contrast, which ¶34 makes explicit (Obs. + Fn., Medium).

## H. Pacing

Fast and rhythmic. Each ¶ is one rung with one comparison. Sentences are short in the list section (¶27–33). The only dense stretch is ¶5–10, the scale explanation, and it is the slowest part. Setup-to-payoff distances are short for the A and B rungs and long for C (¶2→¶22). Pacing is variable by design: it accelerates in both ladders.

## I. Payoffs

Micro: each rung (¶9–17, ¶25–33). Major: 25 explodes the Sun (¶18), which closes the teaser. Reversal: the scale goes *below* zero (¶23). Ultimate: magnitude −15 is a dust mote (¶33), and then the tonal close. Is the central promise (magnitude 15 in NYC) delivered? **Partially.** ¶11 gives the global effect, but NYC specifically is answered only by implication in ¶3 (Obs., High).

## J. Conclusion and CTA

There is no CTA. The ending is a one-line tonal reflection (¶34) that comments on the series' tendency to destroy things. It feels earned, because the descending list built the contrast.

## K. Strengths and weaknesses

- **Strengths.** (1) The flash-forward and rewind opening. (2) The mid-point direction reversal that revives a dormant loop. (3) The domestic comparisons are vivid and quotable.
- **Opportunities.** (1) The NYC framing from the question is dropped after ¶3; a callback could close it. (2) ¶5–10 front-loads definitions. (3) The upward ladder skips 12–14, which is fine for pace but leaves the "15 in NYC" moment generic.
- **Distinctive.** The bidirectional scale ladder.
- **Risk.** The definitional block ¶5–7 (Speculative).
- **Lesson.** A pivot that inverts direction can restart a script's momentum without a new topic.

## L. Formula

1. Stack several versions of the question. 2. Flash-forward to the most extreme answer. 3. Rewind and correct the viewer's mental scale. 4. Climb rungs, each with one vivid yardstick. 5. Close the flash-forward at the top. 6. Pivot ("hold on"), then descend with comic comparisons. 7. Close with a self-aware tonal line.
