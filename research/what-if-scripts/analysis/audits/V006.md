## B. Opening hook forensics

- **Obs. (High).** In the attributed question (Zach, ¶1), the asker calls umbrellas "boring". ¶2 is a **stammering self-interruption**: the narrator starts two sentences ("sounds totally reasonable, but… Well… ok", and "it—") and abandons both. Then comes a flat restart, "The idea… is a thing we are currently talking about." ¶3 delivers the verdict: not practical.
- **Hook types.** Question Hook, plus a **Comic Self-Interrupt** (performed reluctance), plus Answer-First (impractical).
- **Construction.** The narrator *performs* failing to take the premise seriously, which tells the viewer the answer is no and makes "how impractical?" the game (Fn., Medium).
- **Withheld.** *How* impractical, and in what ways.
- **Weakness.** The answer-first "not practical" lowers suspense about the verdict. The script relies on *degree* and comedy instead (Obs.).

## D. Narrative spine

Hook (¶1–3) → Obstacle 1, energy: 2.6 MJ/L × rain rate = 9 kW/m², about 10× sunlight (¶4–5) → Consequence: you'd be living in a human-sized autoclave (¶6) → Escalation, "But it gets worse!": lasers splatter droplets instead of vaporising them (¶7–8) → Obstacle 3, targeting: solvable in principle (Hawk-Eye-style tracking, adaptive optics, 500 pulses/s) (¶9–10) → An alternative, firing randomly; the beam range is the same as rain visibility, hundreds of metres (¶11) → Reversal button: firing lasers randomly to protect a neighbourhood definitely won't help (¶12).

This is a **feasibility audit**: three obstacles (energy, then physics, then control) followed by a "lazy alternative" that fails comically (Obs., High).

## K. Strengths and weaknesses

- **Strengths.** (1) The performed-reluctance opening sets the comic contract. (2) Concrete comparisons for abstract power. (3) The mirrored-sentence button.
- **Opportunities.** (1) Verdict-first removes suspense; a "surprisingly, one part *is* solvable" tease could add a loop. (2) Loops are sequential, not nested. (3) The opening spends about three sentences before content.
- **Distinctive.** The scripted stammer.
- **Risk.** A slow opening for an impatient viewer (Speculative).
- **Lesson.** When the answer is obviously no, make "how badly no" and the narrator's personality the engine.

## L. Formula
