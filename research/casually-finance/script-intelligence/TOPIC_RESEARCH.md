### 1. The Fed's own rate-hike bill (SELECTED)

- **Editorial link:** applies the exact mechanism of the channel's #3 Short (rSa2aziEsAI, 11.85×: a central bank paying interest on reserves while holding low-yield bonds) to the Fed. Sits inside the live rate-hike arc (xtqbRt94TTw; 9eBM5IF_bco) and the debt arc (the Fed's halted payments to the Treasury).
- **Relevance:** the Sep 16 hike; three post-hike weekly H.4.1 prints through Oct 7; FOMC Oct 28.
- **Surprising mechanism:** the Fed's main liability (~$3T of reserves) reprices instantly, while much of its asset income was locked in at 2020–21 yields.
- **Core question:** why does raising rates cost the Fed money, and who bears it?
- **Data:** H.4.1, FOMC implementation notes, the Fed's May 2026 report and 2021 remittance release, FRED.
- **Original angle:** the 2026 reversal. The hole was finally shrinking and stalled; since the hike it has grown three straight weeks.
- **30–59 s explainability:** one mechanism, two numbers, one dated turn. The title formula is Why + subject + state. No existing upload covers it.
- **Why the next slot:** it rides the Fed-hike news cycle the channel is currently covering, ahead of the Oct 28 meeting, without duplicating the Oct 13 long-form's angles (global hikes, real rates, junk bonds, inelastic demand).

## 4. Why #1 beats the alternatives

It is the only idea that scores top marks on all three of surprise, evidence and explainability:

- **Surprise:** a central bank that loses money by doing its job.
- **Evidence:** weekly primary data dated within the last week.
- **Explainability:** one mechanism.

It also inherits the strongest proven editorial template (the Japan Short) while staying **new to the channel**. #2 is better on financial significance but repeats a saturated debt-cost run. #3 collides with the Oct 13 long-form. #4 and #5 lack verified primary numbers today.

Risk to manage: three weekly prints are a short window and weekly values are noisy (the hole also rose on Aug 26). The script therefore states the sequence as fact and the mechanism separately, with no claim that the hike alone caused each weekly change, and it explicitly says the losses do not impair the Fed.
