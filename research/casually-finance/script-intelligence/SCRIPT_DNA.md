- `OBSERVED_PATTERN`: **one central mechanism per Short.** No Short introduces a second unrelated mechanism.

### Long-form grammar (2026, 7 transcripts)

Hook fact → contradiction / central question → eliminate easy answers → promise of a structural explanation (often numbered: "three things", "four separate things") → chapters of named-actor evidence → **sponsor at 22–55% of runtime** (5umJ63d3rzQ 55%, -X6YzlY_8tM 52–61%, 0ll9rEtoRrs 22–28%, DgbHW0IqHrI 41%, xtqbRt94TTw 45%, Uumnx2Fo6cE 47%, roe3SgezmmU 25%) → explicit return line ("Now back to…", 8 uses) → escalation → pre-emptive counterargument ("I know what some of you are thinking") → payoff/takeaway → CTA, which often comes **before** the final takeaway (85–97% of runtime). `OBSERVED_PATTERN`.

The 2025 long-forms have no sponsor beat and use enumerated comic archetypes (kwMHrTFCWUE) or a fictional-character arc (o3FQcd91MIs).

- `OBSERVED_PATTERN`: **pace roughly 190 wpm, rising over time** (2025 long-forms 152–171, 2026 long-forms 185–199). Shorts are dense: long compound sentences that chain clauses with "so", "and", "which means", punctuated by **short blunt lines** ("It is an invoice." / "It can't do both." / "Well the answer is you."). 5 of 6 Shorts contain a sentence of ≤7 words (3 of 6 have one of ≤5 words); YGC3ao005as is the exception (shortest sentence: 9 words).
- `OBSERVED_PATTERN`: **conversational-explanatory register**. Technical terms (basis point, yield, primary deficit, buyback, seasoning) appear but are glossed in plain words in the same or next sentence.
- `OBSERVED_PATTERN`: **number anchoring**. Concrete dated figures carry the argument (5.3% auction, 120% by 2036, 2031, $22 billion).
- Common devices: contradiction/paradox, named authority (CBO, CRFB, Fed chair, Druckenmiller, Lennar CEO), dated events, short-line punchlines, parallel negation, metaphor ("financial monster", "suck the oxygen out of the room").
- Rare devices (≤2 of 16): profanity (SpaceX), pop-culture gags (Eminem lyric), invented dialogue (2025 only), "armchair economists" objection (Japan only). Humour markers fell from 1.10 to 0.19 per 1k words between 2025 and 2026 long-forms. Comedy moved largely to **visual gags** (beat `devices` column: chat-bubble gags, meme insertions) rather than narration (`OBSERVED_PATTERN` in beats; visual delivery itself was not re-verified).
- Absent from Shorts narration (6/6): subscribe CTAs, chapter promises, sponsor mentions. Only the SpaceX Short's *description* carries a subscribe line.

The typical o3FQcd91MIs delays its question to ~64 s. Delay alone is not proven harmful: the long setup in o3FQcd91MIs still scored typical.

Editorial process that could produce similar scripts (`PLAUSIBLE_HYPOTHESIS`; the creator's private workflow is unknown):

1. A fresh primary release or headline (auction result, FOMC decision, Redfin report, viral chart).
2. Find the paradox inside that data.
3. Collect 3–6 named institutional sources.
4. Write a 13–17-minute long-form with numbered causes.
5. Cut 4–10 Shorts from the strongest self-contained passages, promoting the punchiest line to the front.

## G. Five strongest observed writing patterns (summary)

1. **Contradiction-first hook with the topic named in sentence one** (6/6 Shorts; 6/7 2026 long-forms open on a dated fact). `OBSERVED_PATTERN`
2. **One institutional mechanism per Short, explained by chained cause-and-effect clauses at ~190 wpm** (6/6). `OBSERVED_PATTERN`
3. **Dated, named-source numbers as the argument's spine** (CBO, CRFB, Fed, Treasury, Redfin); sources are spoken, never linked. `OBSERVED_PATTERN`
4. **Long explanatory sentences broken by short punchlines** (≤7 words in 5/6 Shorts; Short sentences ≥25 words: 24%). `OBSERVED_PATTERN`
5. **Shorts are re-sequenced long-form passages released in topic runs after the long-form** (5/6 transcript-confirmed, plus 8 description-confirmed). `OBSERVED_PATTERN`

Our Short must be **original**, so pattern 5 informs topic timing (the next editorial slot), not copying.
