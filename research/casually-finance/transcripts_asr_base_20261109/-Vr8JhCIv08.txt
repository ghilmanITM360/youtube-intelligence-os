America became the first country in history to cross $40 trillion in national debt. More than $117,000 for every person in this country. It's a number that's doubled in the last 10 years, and quadrupled in the last 20, which is how we hit this $40 trillion debt number ahead of schedule. The Congressional Budget Office projected we'd cross it in 2028. We got there two years early, which makes it about the only deadline Washington has ever beaten. But the $40 trillion number is also the least interesting thing about what's currently happening. Because a big debt at a low enough price is survivable, like a mortgage. What makes debt dangerous isn't how much you owe. It's what it costs to carry. And right now, the United States spends roughly $3 billion a day on interest. Over the next decade, the Congressional Budget Office has America spending more than $16 trillion on interest alone. That's money that buys nothing. No roads, no schools, no research. It's the rental fee on decisions we already made. Interest now eats 3.2% of the entire American economy, a level only seen once before in history.
