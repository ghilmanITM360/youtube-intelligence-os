30 years ago in 1992, Japan's asset bubble burst, real estate collapsed, equities collapsed, and Japan entered what became known as the Lost Decades. It was a three decade long period of being financially f**ked. There was no real growth, no inflation, and no wage expansion. It was like the economy hit pause, and nobody could find the remote. So the Bank of Japan did what central banks do when they're out of options. They cut rates to zero, and then they cut rates below zero. And then, when that wasn't enough, they introduced something called yield curve control, which sounds like a Marvel villain, but actually just means we're gonna cap long-term bond yields, whether the market likes it or not. Basically, the Japanese government superglue interest rates to the floor. So for decades, Japanese government bonds yielded pretty much nothing, and that created massive problems for Japanese investors.
