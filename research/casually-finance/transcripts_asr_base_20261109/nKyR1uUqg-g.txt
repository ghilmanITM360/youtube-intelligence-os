Right now, everyone's blaming inflation on the Middle East. That's the narrative we're being told, and everyone's accepted it. And it makes sense why. It's easy to follow. There's a clean cause and a clean effect. It's so simple you can draw it on a napkin. Except there's one problem with this story. The Federal Reserve keeps its own inflation scoreboard. They don't use the number you see in the headlines. They target 2% inflation measured by personal consumption expenditures, the PCE. That's the scoreboard that counts. That's also where the mainstream story falls apart. The conflict in the Middle East started in February, just 5 months ago. But the Federal Reserve's measure has now missed the inflation target for 63 months in a row, more than 5 years straight. The PCE currently sits at a little over 4%, more than double the Fed's target, and is continuing to climb. So everyone's using a 5-month-old story to diagnose a 5-year-old problem.
