When the Fed raises rates now, it presses down in two places at once. The first is the brakes, higher rates, price your loans, less borrowing, and a slower economy. Simple. The second is hidden underneath and quietly emerges by accident. Higher rates also mean the government pays more interest on what it already owes, and that bill is not small. Net interest is running around a trillion dollars a year, more than the entire military budget. In 2021, interest date about 9% of every dollar the federal government collected. This year, it's 19%. So every time the Fed raises rates to cool the economy down, it also raises the size of the check that gets written and paid out, which then gets spent, lend it out, or rolled into the next bond at the new higher rate. Which basically means the same move that's supposed to pull money out of the economy starts quietly sending it back in. So one foot presses the brake, the other one quietly presses the gas.
