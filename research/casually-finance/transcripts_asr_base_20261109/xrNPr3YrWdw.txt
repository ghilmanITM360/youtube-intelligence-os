In 1985, the Bureau of Labor Statistics found that 80% of full-time workers at Medium and Large American Companies were in a defined benefit plan or a pension. By 2000, that number was 36. Today, that number sits around 14%. Which may just sound like a small paperwork change, but it isn't. Under a pension, the company owes a fixed amount on a fixed schedule, so it has to go by something to match that shape. But under a 401k, the company owes nothing past payday. According to the numbers from EBRI and the Investment Company Institute, more than 70% of all 401k assets sit in stocks, while standalone bond funds get about 5%. The organization for economic cooperation and development is an international organization of 38 developed countries. You can think of it as the Avengers, but for economics. Across its member countries, pension funds domestic bond holdings have been cut in half from 8% in 2007 down to 4% last year. Which means the biggest change in structural demand in the bond market in 50 years was caused by someone in HR named Susan.
