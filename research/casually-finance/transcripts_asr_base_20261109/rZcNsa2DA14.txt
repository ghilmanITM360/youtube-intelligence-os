Let's rewind to the late 70s. Inflation wasn't drifting anymore. It was sprinting. It peaked at over 13.5%. At that pace, prices double in about nine years. The dollar in your pocket was rotting while you held it. And every single person could feel it happening. So who do you call when inflation is out of control? No, not Ghostbusters. You call six foot seven, cigar smoking, Paul Volker, the kind of man who looks like he eats recessions for breakfast. Volker takes over his fed chair in 1979 and does the one thing nobody else had the stomach for. He drives interest rates to 20%. He deliberately triggers not one recession, but two, back to back, strangling the money supply until inflation has nothing left to feed on. It was so aggressive, it earned a name of its own, the Volker shock, and it worked. Inflation went from over 13.5% down to bottoming out under 2% in the late 80s.
