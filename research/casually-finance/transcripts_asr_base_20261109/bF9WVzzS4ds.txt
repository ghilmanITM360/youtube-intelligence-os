There's a tool at the Federal Reserve where a foreign government can hand over its American bonds, take dollars in exchange, and buy the bonds back later at a slightly higher repurchase price. It's a pawn shop for governments. It's what you build for a creditor you cannot afford to lose. When the US Department of the Treasury and Japan's Ministry of Finance executed a joint market operation to prop up the rapidly depreciating Japanese yen. 48 hours after the operation, Treasury Secretary Scott Besant took to Twitter to say that, the FIMA repo facility is an important backstop and that he'd encourage the Fed to expand it in the coming months. But here's the detail that makes the request strange. Nobody was using it. The facility has a ceiling of $60 billion per country and the balance was sitting at zero. Days after he asked, Bloomberg reported it had gone unused for an eighth straight week. So he wasn't asking for more room because he'd run out. Nobody was anywhere near the ceiling. He was asking the central bank in public 48 hours after a currency operation to build capacity for something that hadn't happened yet. You don't publicly ask the central bank to make an empty facility bigger unless you know something is coming.
