Governments and companies put together are on track to borrow about $29 trillion in 2026. Four trillion more than they borrowed just two years ago. Every year, the OECD publishes a report on exactly this. It's 200 pages of reading. That's so data heavy, it makes you question all the life choices that brought you to reading it on a Saturday morning. In the report, there are some big takeaways. Nearly 80% of everything its member governments borrow this year doesn't pay for anything. It goes to refinancing the debt they already have, not paying for roads or schools, just moving the old pile of debt forward. Last year, the borrowing cost to refinance existing debt was about $13.5 trillion. This year, it's expected to grow to closer to $14.5, and it doesn't arrive evenly. A third of all the fixed rate debt those governments owe comes due by 2027, which matters because of when it was borrowed. Similar to the United States situation we just covered, 60% of the fixed rate debt in the OECD that matured by 2027 was issued in 2021 or earlier, back when money was nearly free. Since 2023, the rate on new government debt has run about 2% points higher than the maturing debt it's replacing, so a third of the developed world's debt is about to get swapped out. One maturity at a time. For the same debt at a much higher price, and we're already seeing it happen. One fifth of the fixed rate bonds outstanding in 2025 have been issued at a yield of more than 4%. The highest rate since 2015.
