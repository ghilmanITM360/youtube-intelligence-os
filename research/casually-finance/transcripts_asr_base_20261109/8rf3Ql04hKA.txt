These methodologies don't create an accurate picture of the economy. They create blind spots where bad data can hide. And underneath all of this is something called Good Heart's Law. The idea is simple. The moment a metric becomes a target, it stops being a good metric. So once policymakers, institutions, and markets start optimizing for a specific target number, they stop optimizing for reality. And that's how your lived experience can say one thing while the official number says another. Because we're no longer measuring the economy itself, we're just managing the metrics we use to measure the economy. And over time, this is why the numbers drift further and further away from what people are actually experiencing.
