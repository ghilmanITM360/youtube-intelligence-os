# Reference visual fingerprint — authority for chair-test V2

## Authority and scope

**Actual source MP4 frames outrank forensic descriptions, reconstruction prompts, aggregate DNA, and the rejected prototype.** The coffee/heart test is not evidence here. This fingerprint is based on 112 newly extracted overview frames from all 14 source files, 59 curated frames, targeted multi-state sequences in every source, and 42 existing forensic frame/contact-sheet selections. It is not an exhaustive annotation of every frame of every video.

Source titles, paths, timestamps and images are in [REFERENCE_FRAME_STUDY.md](REFERENCE_FRAME_STUDY.md). IDs R02–R15 identify the 14 sources; R01 is unused. The numerical gap has no analytical meaning. Eight sources are portrait: R02, R04, R05, R06, R09, R11, R13, R15. Their spatial organization has priority over landscape layouts for this 9:16 project.

Every conclusion uses one of these categories:

- **MEASURED / OBSERVED ACROSS CORPUS:** coverage of all 14 source files or an explicitly defined aggregate dataset. A sampled observation is not an every-frame claim.
- **OBSERVED IN MULTIPLE EXAMPLES:** named supporting images, with no assertion that every source follows it.
- **OBSERVED IN ONE REFERENCE:** a useful exception or device limited to its cited source.
- **NEW CREATIVE DECISION:** V2's authored choices. These are not presented as recovered house rules.

## Human figures

**OBSERVED IN MULTIPLE EXAMPLES — fundamental construction.** R02 at 2.016, 56.443 and 96.760 s; R09 at 0.949 and 6.644 s; R13 at 1.762 and 36.995 s; R04 at 2.183 and 15.283 s show an unfilled circular or slightly oval head, a single narrow torso stroke, single-stroke arms and legs, and elbows/knees made by bends rather than outlined anatomy. Most selected heads have no eyes, nose or mouth. Hands often terminate as a short stroke. Feet can be a bent terminal line; detailed shoes are unnecessary. The figure can be expressive without facial features.

**OBSERVED IN MULTIPLE EXAMPLES — poses explain relations.** A head tilted toward a hand conveys frustration at the keyboard (R02 56.443); extended arms register a reaction to the changed display (R04 15.283); hands at the head register alarm while the diagram itself changes (R13 31–37); a mug is discarded after an imagined consequence (R09 3–7). These poses have a subject and consequence. A static person with a floating warning icon does not reproduce that relationship.

**OBSERVED IN MULTIPLE EXAMPLES — identity is economical.** Ponytail/headphones in R02 70.554, hair/beard/glasses in R08 68.127, and ponytail in R04 104.800 distinguish figures without turning their torsos into filled clothing silhouettes. Props and hair are optional identity cues, not mandatory anatomical detail.

**OBSERVED IN MULTIPLE EXAMPLES — color is not skin/clothing by default.** Black or white line figures predominate in these selected human examples. R07 2–8 uses red disintegration; R04 61.133/91.700 uses white figures against black. These are state/background-dependent treatments, not a clothing palette.

**NEW CREATIVE DECISION — V2 character.** Unfilled head, black single-stroke body and limbs, no face, no shirt/trouser/skin fills, no detailed shoes. The principal participant is bald; a second participant has one small hair contour, not a separate illustration family. A generic explainer is not a portrait of Dr. Rehan. Pose continuity—not costume—identifies the person. No figure is a copy of a particular reference composition.

## Objects and furniture

**OBSERVED IN MULTIPLE EXAMPLES.** R02 2.016 and 56.443 has chairs, a table, a keyboard fixture, books and laptop. R09 0.949 has an office chair, desk, monitor and mug. R08 22.709 and 90.837 has a refrigerator, fan, toaster, outlet and cable. Objects are often more detailed than the people: layered book edges, chair supports, appliance coils and plugs remain when they explain identity or mechanism. Therefore “all objects should be icons” is unsupported.

**OBSERVED IN MULTIPLE EXAMPLES.** Linework has mild contour irregularity and rounded human heads, but straight physical edges remain legible. Perspective is selective: furniture and appliances have side/three-quarter cues while diagrams flatten space. An absolute ban on perspective would contradict R02's desks and R08's appliance views.

**NEW CREATIVE DECISION.** Use a coherent side-on chair/figure setup with a small floor line, enough chair structure for contact and stability, and no wheels or decorative detailing. Generate the chair and pose together in registered opaque scene states; this avoids independently drifting anatomy, disappearing chairs and mismatched line weight. Later paper folds, table edges and stamp underside communicate physical handling, not app panels.

## Background and color

**MEASURED / OBSERVED ACROSS CORPUS — sampled coverage.** All 14 overview sets contain explanatory drawn imagery. The sets do not share one background or palette. No exact corpus-wide hex color, stroke width, paper tone, fill percentage or font can be inferred from these samples.

**OBSERVED IN MULTIPLE EXAMPLES.** Near-white/light-neutral fields support black drawing in R02, R05, R09, R13 and the opening of R15. Dark backgrounds support contrasting diagrams/figures in R04, R07, R11, R13. R06 has yellow suns, blue dusk and a saturated sunset. R15 uses extensive green, pale yellow, cyan and gray map regions with magenta location dots. R11 uses luminous red-white physical effects. Full-frame red promotional endings appear in multiple landscape examples.

**OBSERVED IN MULTIPLE EXAMPLES — semantic color is local.** R10 41–45 uses red to correct a weight annotation; R09 uses red inside a thermometer; R08 uses red heat and energy annotations; R11 uses red plasma/radiation; R15 uses geographic color. “Red always means stop,” “only one accent color,” and “no gradients” are not corpus-wide rules.

**NEW CREATIVE DECISION.** V2 uses a flat white canvas, black drawing, at most a restrained red rejection stroke in inference/safety beats. White is a production choice grounded in the light-field subset, not a measured universal. Avoid a simulated paper texture. Use no full-body fills. No exact hexadecimal match is claimed. The clinical subject does not need the cosmic references' glow or destruction effects.

## Text

**OBSERVED IN MULTIPLE EXAMPLES.** Hand-lettered-looking uppercase labels, units and dialogue are integrated with drawing: R02 28.222 has an energy heading, droplet temperature and squirrel displacement; R05 uses a vertical magnitude scale; R13 uses equations and scale comparisons; R09's thought experiment has selective reactions. The strokes of many letters vary in width, height and baseline; exact original fonts remain unknown.

**OBSERVED IN MULTIPLE EXAMPLES — exceptions matter.** Gray question banners with ordinary typeset text appear in R09 and R15 and landscape openings. R02 42.333 is deliberately text-heavy: rewriting and strike-through are the explanation itself. R03's spoof-phone frame resembles an advertisement on purpose. R09 has a warning triangle in a reactor drawing. R12 has diagram boxes and arrows. The lesson is not “no cards, no text, no triangles, no diagrams.” It is “choose a device because it performs the explanation.”

**OBSERVED IN MULTIPLE EXAMPLES — captions.** The selected portrait frames do not show a persistent lower transcript strip. Labels and speech can occur high, low or beside the relevant object. Occasional ending copy and question cards do not establish full-time captioning. Their sampled absence is not an exhaustive no-subtitles census.

**MEASURED / OBSERVED ACROSS CORPUS — file limitation.** These are flattened video frames. Whether original text was baked into a PNG or separately composited cannot be determined from the final pixels. The old `TEXT BAKED: NO` instructions describe a reconstruction method, not observed source authoring.

**NEW CREATIVE DECISION.** Generate no exact text. Make the few required words, tally and symbolic count natively in CapCut for control. Select an installed hand-lettered-looking face after comparing the actual glyphs; do not assert an exact font now. Accessibility subtitles stay separate by default. This is an implementation choice, not a claim that the corpus was built that way.

## Composition, density and empty space

**OBSERVED IN MULTIPLE EXAMPLES.** Portrait frames often use an upper explanatory region and a lower reacting/reading figure: R02 2.016, R04 2.183, R06 2.598, R13 1.762, R15 1.667. The regions form a relationship; they are not a grid of disconnected icons. R09 couples a desk scene below to a thought experiment above.

**OBSERVED IN MULTIPLE EXAMPLES.** Sparse examples are legitimate: R05 18.468 and 27.702 holds a ruler and headline; R02 23 s holds a droplet and quantity. Dense examples are also legitimate: R15 maps, R11 expanding plasma, R02 a tall book pile, R08 a cutaway refrigerator. Density changes with explanatory need. No numerical occupied-area target or universal “fill the frame” rule has been measured.

**NEW CREATIVE DECISION.** Main V2 test scene occupies approximately x18–80%, y25–79% of a 2160×3840 design canvas; count/timer sit next to the action. This is a layout target, not corpus statistics. Comparison uses two large interacting adults and their records, not three small repeated icons. Later stamp and foldout use the upper region, with the participant anchoring the lower region. Keep space where an action is about to occur.

## Scene progression and motion

**OBSERVED IN MULTIPLE EXAMPLES — additive explanation.** R02 0.5/2/3.5 adds typing marks inside an otherwise stable scene; 23/25/27 adds a new physical analogy beneath the energy amount. R12 93/97/101/105 develops a row of water/air comparisons and then adds consequences. R15 74/77/80/82 adds a localized comic inset to a stable map.

**OBSERVED IN MULTIPLE EXAMPLES — reaction and reversal.** R09 3/5/6.5/7 changes imagined teeth, then discards a real mug. R04 9/11/13/15 changes the pictured world while the presenter changes pose. R10 41/43/45 preserves the person and scale while correcting the number. R13 31/33/35/37 develops an unstable physical diagram with a reacting observer. No single stock bounce can substitute for these cause/effect states.

**OBSERVED IN MULTIPLE EXAMPLES — sustained changes exist.** R11 grows a destructive physical field; R13 scales a black hole; R06 develops a sunset gag. Consequently “only hard cuts, no continuous motion anywhere” is false. Our sampled sequences establish changed states, not exact easing curves or one-frame durations for every event.

**MEASURED / OBSERVED ACROSS CORPUS — inherited table facts, not newly remeasured motion.** Master tables contain 581 states, 438 motion events and 1,421 sync events. Visual DNA classifies 522/581 states as locked/static. Motion DNA includes 144 events shorter than 0.5 s but also 81 longer than 5 s. These table summaries support state-based construction. Because source checks reveal local timestamp/description errors, they must not be promoted to frame-accurate scheduling authority.

**NEW CREATIVE DECISION.** V2 uses registered pose replacements, short physical translations of stamp/paper and a two-state unfolding drawing. No image-to-video generation is required. No continuous sway, generic entrance presets, elastic bounces or routine zooms. Brief state changes must alter meaning, not merely increase motion density.

## Humor and payoff

**OBSERVED IN MULTIPLE EXAMPLES.** An impossible thought changes a mundane action (R09 mug); a small physical effect is compared to a squirrel movement (R02); an escalating diagram intrudes on its own presenter (R13); sunset extension becomes a human-scale joke (R06); a scientific explanation concludes with an unexpected local scene (R15). Humor is in the inference or consequence, not in dressing a generic character brightly.

**NEW CREATIVE DECISION.** V2's oversized stamp embodies the temptation to apply a result indiscriminately and label someone immediately. It is prevented from touching the paper. The final unfolded sheet shows why an isolated count needs explanation. These metaphors have not been found verbatim in the references; they borrow transformation, reaction, object continuity and changing scale. Safety beats themselves stay restrained.

1. R02 at 70.554 s visibly shows the headset gamer; the master time lookup returns F10.1's calendar composition. At 56.443 s the visible book pile is missing from part of the legacy description. Raw timestamps are used instead of pretending these rows match.
2. R03 at 29.190 s visibly shows a standing explorer/shovel scene, while the lookup describes a triptych. At 116.760 s the visible phone gag does not agree with a literal reading of the associated narration sentence as a picture description.
3. R07 at 42.531/127.593/212.654/255.185 s visibly shows reactor/turbines/source comparison/reactor-vial imagery; several corresponding master rows point to different later scenes, including credits. Those state IDs are not adopted as proof.
4. R10 at 220.938 and 265.125 s visibly shows the 1G/6G body comparison and board diagram respectively, not the retrieved toxicity chart/space description.
5. R13's image prompt specifies dot eyes and mouth. The inspected presenter frames show blank heads. The pixels win.
6. A zero gap between timeline clips does not establish absence of an opacity fade inside those clips. Source-specific rebuild files describe local fades, and sampled R07 states visibly dissolve. Do not repeat the global “zero fades exist” claim.
7. The old typing manifest says no audio, while the actual repository MP4 probes with audio and later semantic transcripts exist. Historical manifests are not current media truth.

These conflicts do not prevent a visual study. They do prevent using legacy IDs as unquestioned evidence. Every selected frame is addressable by actual source filename plus extraction timestamp; uncertain legacy mappings are marked separately.

## V2 acceptance criteria

- At thumbnail size, participant reads as a blank-headed line figure, not recolored corporate anatomy.
- Seated/leaning/standing/paused/stopped scenes share chair, ground line, proportions and drawing treatment.
- Every reveal adds a fact, demonstrates a consequence or denies a false inference.
- The count remains one person's count; no threshold, diagnosis, age rule or sex rule is invented.
- Warning ends action. No collapse, symptom loop or continued unsafe repetition.
- Two comparison figures are large, distinct actors handling records, not duplicated pictograms.
- Final image resolves the number-only problem visually; it does not dissolve into a text slide.
- Approval depends on generated images alongside the cited frames. Prompt compliance alone is not proof of visual fidelity.
