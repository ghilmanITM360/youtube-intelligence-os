## What the playbook teaches, and where it breaks

The ten-question decision prompt, claim register, layer template and evidence workflow are more useful than an element catalog. The oil scene demonstrates a visible statistic plus a proof layer. The Bitcoin opener alternates a host reaction with identity, comments and object metaphor. V1 Rent explains the CPI composition with a part/whole bar and later explains panel rotation with original procedural diagrams. Broad principles transfer to new topics.

The playbook should explicitly resolve claim conflicts before composition, and maintain visual identity across neighboring clauses. Four situations need conditional rules with examples:

| Problem | Rule and original application |
|---|---|
| Actual mechanism or worked hypothetical | Use annotated stages with conserved units. For two bonds paying the same cash amount, retain the numerators as prices change and switch to a payment timeline when maturity matters. Keep “hypothetical” visible. |
| Two authoritative quantities or complex comparisons | Match definition, population, period and basis before using a common axis. For advertised rent versus lease prices, introduce each metric separately, retain both dates/denominators and compare only the shared attribute. |
| Real people, organizations and history | Use a verified identity visual for identification, a source passage for attribution and a time series for magnitude. For a regulatory change, show its dated effective provision before graphing what happened later. A chart alone cannot establish causation. |
| Abstract claim, humor or attention reset | Pick a metaphor with a one-to-one relationship the viewer can follow, then return to the referent. Keep evidence or a complex comparison on screen while it can be read. Use a static, character-free shot where a character competes for attention. Reveal on meaning changes and allow white, color or space to serve the editorial purpose. |
