# Scientific visual design: Slot 07

Written 2026-11-26. The claims ledger says whether each sentence is *true*. This document says how each concept becomes *understood*. Numbers come from the ledger (C01-C13); this file adds no new facts. Everything is educational and non-operational (no instrument settings, no pointing procedure).

**Test for every concept:** with the sound off and no text longer than a symbol or a number, can a viewer say what happened? Each block ends with its muted-viewer test. Nothing here is tested yet; the animatic is where the tests run and are recorded in `SCIENTIFIC_VISUAL_QA.md`.

**Model declarations** (what the picture claims to be):

| Model class | Used for | How it is declared |
|---|---|---|
| Actual object (illustrated from photographs) | astronaut, reflector panel, dome and telescope silhouettes, Earth and Moon appearance | none needed; labelled generic where it is not a real site |
| Simplified scientific illustration | corner-cube cross-section, atmosphere layer, histogram | clean geometric style, different from the "real object" style |
| Educational symbol | the light pulse, photon markers, rays | colour grammar: outbound warm, return cool; never drawn as a visible beam in space |
| Magnified inset | beam footprint at B20, array marker inside it | round frame with a magnifier tab; no numbers |
| Non-scale schematic | Earth-Moon spacing, moon orbit ellipse | one small "not to scale" mark the first time it appears (B05), then the same frame language |
| Time-stretched (slow motion) | the pulse crossing the gap at B05-B11 and B32 | the stopwatch is always visible and slowed with the pulse, so the two stay in step; its final value is the true one |

Light really crosses in 1.28 s and the narration gives each leg about 1.2-1.7 s, so the slow-motion factor is small and varies by beat. State this in the Roman Urdu report if asked.

## V1. A laser is a question, not a floodlight (B01-B04)
- **Narration:** "Imagine pointing a powerful laser at the moon. Not to light it up. To ask it a question. You fire a pulse."
- **Viewer must understand:** the goal is a *measurement*, and the laser is a very short signal sent out, not a continuous beam.
- **Object looks like:** a generic dome observatory with its slit open, the Moon high above; inside, a telescope tipping up. Real lunar-ranging beams are almost invisible in the dark, so the film never draws a glowing beam to the Moon.
- **Relationship shown:** B02 a dashed floodlight cone toward the Moon gets a crossed ring over it (the rejected idea); B03 a dot-built question mark rises; B04 a small compact marker (the pulse) leaves the aperture and exits the top of the frame, alone, with empty sky behind it.
- **Misconception:** "the laser shines on the Moon so we can see it" / "a continuous red beam".
- **Animation that removes it:** the rejected cone vanishes and what leaves is a single bead of warm light with a clean gap behind it. One departure event, on the word "pulse".
- **Disclose:** the pulse is a symbol; the real pulse is about 90 ps long (C08) and invisible to the eye in flight.
- **Muted test:** viewer sees "cone rejected, question, one bead sent".

## V2. Timing the round trip (B05-B07)
- **Narration:** "at a reflector sitting on the lunar surface. Now, how long before that light comes home? About two and a half seconds."
- **Understand:** we start a clock at emission and stop it at return; the answer is a duration of about 2.6 s.
- **Looks like:** Earth limb at the bottom, Moon at the top, a ring on the Moon (no hardware yet, so the reflector reveal at B17 still pays off), a large stopwatch.
- **Relationship:** pulse leaves Earth as the stopwatch starts, touches the ring, comes back; the stopwatch stops on 2.56 (C01: mean round trip 2.564 s).
- **Misconception:** the pulse is instantaneous; or the Moon is a few thousand km away.
- **Animation:** the narration gives this trip about 4.7 s (11.4 s to 16.1 s) for a 2.56 s event, so the pulse and the stopwatch are **slowed together by the same factor (about 1.8x)**. The stopwatch always ends on the true value (2.56) and the pulse always arrives when it does, so the clock and the pulse never disagree. The same rule holds at B10/B11 and B32 with their own factors; the factor is not shown on screen.
- **Disclose:** schematic, not to scale (once, at B05).
- **Muted test:** a clock starts, a bead goes up and returns, the clock stops at about two and a half.

## V3. There and back (B08-B11)
- **Narration:** "Wait, that's there and back. Not two and a half seconds each way. A little over a second to reach the moon and a little over a second to return."
- **Understand:** 2.56 s is the whole trip; each leg is about half, 1.28 s (C02).
- **Looks like:** the same vertical path with a two-way arrow: warm up, cool down.
- **Relationship:** a bracket over the whole stopwatch interval, a small x on the idea of "2.5 s each way", then the interval splits at the Moon into two equal brackets; the leg timer reads 1.28 on arrival, the stopwatch reads 2.56 at the end.
- **Misconception:** 2.5 s one way (the narration anticipates it); that the two legs differ.
- **Animation:** the split happens at the physical turn-around point (the Moon ring), so "half the time" and "half the path" are the same event.
- **Disclose:** "a little over a second" is 1.28 s, the mean. The exact value varies (V4).
- **Muted test:** the trip splits into two equal halves at the Moon.

## V4. The distance keeps changing (B12-B13)
- **Narration:** "And because the moon's distance keeps changing, the timing changes too."
- **Understand:** the Moon's orbit is not a circle, so the distance, and therefore the light time, drifts by a few percent over a month.
- **Looks like:** a top-down orbit with an exaggerated ellipse, a ruler between Earth and Moon with a live reading, the stopwatch beside it.
- **Relationship:** as the Moon moves along the ellipse the ruler lengthens and shortens and the stopwatch reading follows it between 2.38 s (near) and 2.71 s (far) (C03/C04).
- **Misconception:** the Moon is at a fixed distance; or the orbit visibly looks like that eccentric (it does not: e is about 0.055).
- **Animation:** one slow, continuous motion; the *numbers follow the geometry* with no gap, so cause and effect are visible.
- **Disclose:** orbit eccentricity exaggerated (magnified/non-scale); ruler endpoints "about 357,000 and 407,000 km" if shown (C03).
- **Muted test:** Moon farther means longer time.

## V5. Why would light come back? (B14-B16)
- **Narration:** "But hold on. Why would the light come back at all? Did someone leave a mirror on the moon?"
- **Understand:** the Moon's surface is rough dark dust, so ordinary scatter sends light everywhere; a return needs something special.
- **Looks like:** the Moon growing to fill the frame, then a close grey regolith surface under a low horizon.
- **Relationship:** the arriving pulse lands and a fan of scatter arrows leaves in *all* directions (none aimed back); then a dashed ghost mirror lies on the surface with a question ring.
- **Misconception:** the Moon reflects the light back like a bounce off a wall.
- **Animation:** the contrast between the many arrows and the empty return direction is the point; hold one beat of silence at B14 ("But hold on") with everything stopped.
- **Disclose:** the arrows are an illustration; real diffuse scatter is far weaker and wider than any arrow shows.
- **Muted test:** the light spreads everywhere, little would come home; someone proposes a mirror.

## V6. The reveal: a real reflector, placed by people (B17-B18)
- **Narration:** "Actually, yes. Apollo astronauts left special reflectors on the lunar surface."
- **Understand:** the answer to the question is a real piece of hardware, deployed by Apollo crews (C05, C12).
- **Looks like:** the ghost mirror dissolves into the real array in the same spot; then a low wide view with the array, a kneeling astronaut in an A7L suit, black sky, a one-direction sun.
- **Relationship:** same position, same scale: the dissolve is a match cut, so the viewer knows the ghost and the real object are the same idea.
- **Misconception:** the Moon contains a natural reflecting feature; or that the reflector is a big mirror.
- **Animation:** the reflector is small and flat, a grid of round faces in a dark frame; the astronaut shows its size (about 46 cm panel against a human). Dust follows 1/6 g ballistic arcs, no billowing, no wind cloth.
- **Disclose:** no astronaut is named on screen (source status in C05); the film shows one array, Apollo 11, not three.
- **Muted test:** the question gets answered by an actual object on the surface, set there by astronauts.

## V7. The corner trick (B19-B21)
- **Narration:** "Their corners have a clever trick. They redirect a tiny fraction of incoming light back toward where it came from."
- **Understand:** each little cube sends any ray back *parallel to the way it arrived*, whatever the angle; a flat mirror would send it off elsewhere.
- **Looks like:** the array fills the frame, a macro push to one cube, then a clean cross-section on a neutral field, with a small inset of the third face (a 2-D cutaway shows only two of the three reflections).
- **Relationship:** three rays at three different angles enter, bounce on two faces, leave exactly opposite; B21 puts a ghosted flat mirror beside it (A/B): the mirror deflects the ray away, the cube returns it.
- **Misconception (the two to prevent here):** (a) that corner cubes bend light *toward Earth specifically* (they send it back where it came from, which is Earth because that is where the pulse came from); (b) that the reflector returns only a small share of what *hits it* (C06).
- **Animation:** rays enter staggered, leave staggered, entry and exit paths shown parallel with a faint pair of guide ticks. On "tiny fraction" (B20) a magnified inset shows the km-wide footprint and the array as a tiny marker inside it, with *no numbers*; the main frame keeps the ray returning in full.
- **Disclose:** the cutaway is simplified (two of three reflections drawn, third face inset); the inset is magnified; colours follow the warm/cool grammar.
- **Muted test:** every ray comes straight back; a flat mirror would not; the beam is wide and the array is tiny.
- **Risk:** B20 is only 2.2 s; if it overloads at G2, move the footprint inset to B21 (recorded in the plan).

## V8. Almost none of the light makes it back (B22-B25)
- **Narration:** "Back on Earth, a telescope looks for the return. And here's the really strange part. Almost none of the light makes it back to the detector."
- **Understand:** the beam widens on the way out, the array catches a sliver, the returning beam widens again, and the telescope catches a sliver of *that*.
- **Looks like:** ground view of the dome, the telescope cutaway with a highlighted path to the detector, then a wide vertical schematic with two widening cones and two tiny discs.
- **Relationship:** the outbound cone widens from Earth; the array disc sits inside it at the Moon; the return cone widens again to a tiny telescope disc. The ratio is too small to draw to scale, so size relations are *qualitative* (C07).
- **Misconception:** a lunar return is a bright flash; or that the light is absorbed by the Moon.
- **Animation:** the screen dims as the cones widen; the return is a faint cool shimmer, never a beam.
- **Disclose:** not to scale; no ratios on screen; if one number is wanted, "one in 25 million photons even finds the reflector" (C06/C07).
- **Muted test:** it spreads out twice, so only a speck comes back.

## V9. Counting photons across pulses (B26-B28)
- **Narration:** "Scientists have to collect just a few returning photons from many laser pulses. A few photons."
- **Understand:** one pulse yields at most a handful of detections, so the answer is built from many pulses.
- **Looks like:** a dark field with a thin time axis, dots landing on it, a pulse counter, a histogram that grows.
- **Relationship:** each pulse adds zero, one or a few dots; dots from many pulses stack into a peak at the right time, over a flat background of stray counts.
- **Misconception:** each pulse gives a bright answer; or that one detection is a measurement.
- **Animation:** at B28 three dots centred, each with a soft ring, everything else dark ("A few photons") so the sparseness is emphasised.
- **Disclose:** histogram counts are illustrative (C08); a real best-station average is about 1 photon per shot, and most stations see far fewer.
- **Muted test:** a few dots per pulse; many pulses make a peak.

## V10. The atmosphere adds a delay (B29)
- **Narration:** "They even account for the effects of Earth's atmosphere."
- **Understand:** the air slows and bends the light a little; the delay is known and removed.
- **Looks like:** a side view with a wavy band of air above the limb; the beam passes through and the footprint wobbles.
- **Relationship:** a small bump in the path length, then a correction tick that removes it.
- **Misconception:** the atmosphere blocks the beam entirely; or that the effect is negligible.
- **Animation:** the wobble settles when the correction tick lands.
- **Disclose:** illustration only; no number unless "about 1.5 m of zenith delay at a high-altitude site, attributed" is wanted (C09).
- **Muted test:** air disturbs the path, scientists correct for it.

## V11. Precision from statistics (B29b-B30)
- **Narration:** "And somehow those incredibly faint returns let them measure the distance with remarkable precision."
- **Understand:** many faint detections average into a tight peak, so the time is known far better than one photon allows.
- **Looks like:** the histogram sharpens; an error bar shrinks around the peak.
- **Relationship:** more counts means a narrower uncertainty; the width of the peak is the spread of single detections, the error bar is how well the centre is known.
- **Misconception:** the measurement is as fuzzy as one photon; or that "precision" means a single exact reading.
- **Animation:** error bar shrinks in time with the peak growing, then one on-screen figure at most: "about 1 millimetre = 7 trillionths of a second" (C10, 6.7 ps). Say "millimetres", not "one millimetre".
- **Disclose:** statistical precision, not accuracy against truth.
- **Muted test:** more detections, narrower uncertainty.

## V12. The distance formula (B31-B36)
- **Narration:** "So how does it work? A cosmic stopwatch. Take the round trip time, multiply it by the speed of light, then divide by two. That's your distance."
- **Understand:** time times speed gives the *round-trip* path; half of it is the distance to the Moon.
- **Looks like:** the Earth-Moon line, a stopwatch, a bar as long as the whole path, the same bar folded in half.
- **Relationship:** a bracket over the whole path; "t" times "c" extends it into a long bar; the bar folds at the Moon so out-and-back becomes one leg; the ruler between Earth and Moon fills and the readout appears (C11).
- **Misconception:** forgetting the divide-by-two, which would make the Moon twice as far.
- **Animation:** not a formula on screen but a *transformation*: the long bar physically folds. The formula is shown only as the same bar's label (d = c x t / 2).
- **Disclose:** simple model (ignores atmosphere and array geometry, C11).
- **Muted test:** the long path folds in half to give the Moon's distance.

## V13. The Moon is a moving target (B37-B39)
- **Narration:** "The moon isn't just hanging in the sky. With each faint return, we can measure where it is."
- **Understand:** each faint return is one position measurement of a moving body, repeated over years.
- **Looks like:** a long pull-back from Earth with night lights, the Moon drifting; one small dim pulse arrives; the ruler settles.
- **Relationship:** the faint pulse arrival is the very event that sets the ruler.
- **Misconception:** the Moon is fixed; or that the measurement is a one-off.
- **Animation:** restrained; ends on stillness, room tone fading out, not a cheerful flourish.
- **Disclose:** non-scale schematic remains in force. The optional recession fact (3.8 cm/yr, C13) is **not** in the narration and is not shown unless the owner asks.
- **Muted test:** a moving Moon whose position a returning flash fixes.

## Cross-cutting rules
1. **Warm outbound, cool return, whole film.** If the viewer sees warm, the light is going away; cool, coming back.
2. **Earth bottom, Moon top** in every schematic frame, so up is out and down is home.
3. **One sun direction** film-wide (Earth, Moon, astronaut, reflector shadow).
4. **No decorative motion.** Every moving element answers "what is the viewer supposed to understand now?".
5. **One reveal per beat**, on the anchor word (see `narration/NARRATION_BEAT_MAP.csv`), not before.
6. **Word budget:** zero sentences on screen. Allowed: numbers from the ledger, the symbols t, c, d, one "not to scale" mark, and a small "magnified" tab on insets.
7. **Reflect-toward-source vs tiny-fraction-detected** is the film's hardest idea: V7 shows return-in-full per ray; V8 shows loss from spreading. The two must never appear in the same shot at the same visual weight.
