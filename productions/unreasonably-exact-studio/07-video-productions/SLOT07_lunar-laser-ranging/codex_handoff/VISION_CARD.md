# VISION_CARD: Slot 07 "Lunar Laser Ranging" (HC V4 section 2A)

Written 2026-11-26. Decisions, not adjectives. Assumptions are marked (A). Nothing here is approved; approval is the owner's.

| Field | Decision |
|---|---|
| **Audience effect** | A phone viewer with the sound on should leave knowing one thing they did not know: the Moon's distance is read from a stopwatch, and the whole trick is "time x speed of light / 2". They should feel mild astonishment that a few returning photons are enough, and a quiet sense of scale at the end. They should be able to repeat: "they time a laser bounce, then halve it." |
| **Creative thesis** | A real, photographed, human-scale world (a night dome, the Apollo surface) is the *question*; a spare, exact, code-drawn diagram layer is the *answer*; every cut moves from one to the other by a movement or a match, never by a flash. |
| **Visual identity** | **Medium:** layered 2.5D scenes built from reference-based real-object layers (final art: painterly-clean Codex illustrations of the same layers; animatic: colour-matched public-domain NASA photographs and textures) plus flat vector scientific graphics. **Shape language:** real objects keep their true silhouettes; graphics are straight lines, circles and rounded caps only. **Detail density:** high in the real-object layer, minimal in the graphics layer (one idea per frame). **Palette roles:** night blue (#070B1A to #16223F) is the page; warm #FFAC40 = light going out, cool #60C8FF = light coming back (reserved for code-drawn light, never large painted areas); white/grey #E6E9F0 / #82889C for instruments, rulers and numbers; red only for a rejected idea. **Light:** one Sun, upper left, slightly toward the viewer: every sphere lit on the left with a soft terminator, every cast shadow to the right; the dome never receives sunlight. **Type:** Avenir Next Demi Bold for captions and labels, Menlo Bold for every number (tabular, so counting readouts do not jitter). No outline strokes on real objects. |
| **Scope** | 9:16, 540x960 animatic (1080x1920 design space), 24 fps, 94.0 s (92.53 s narration + 1.47 s tail, 0.8 s fade), captions burned in for the animatic, English narration from the unmodified master WAV. (A) No platform UI overlay is known; caption band y 1480-1730 and key-subject box y 150-1440 are OUR provisional mask. |
| **Reference grammar** | Learned, not copied: (1) NASA surface photography: low sun, black sky, hard shadows to one side, subject small in a large empty ground; (2) laser-ranging night photographs: a plain dome with a slit, almost no visible beam (so the film never draws a beam through space; the pulse is a small bead); (3) corner-reflector hardware photographs: a flat dark face of many round cups in a white frame; (4) NASA SVS Earth and Moon maps for the spheres. Prohibited: reproducing any real site, signage, named person, or a specific frame of a documentary; mission patches; reflecting visors showing anyone identifiable. |
| **Anti-generic rules** | 1. No whoosh/flash/glitch transitions: every cut is a match (position, direction of travel) or a movement handoff. 2. No full-frame text: words appear only as the narrator's captions plus a handful of one-word disclosures (NOT TO SCALE, magnified, illustrative, mean distance). 3. One new idea per beat: at most one moving diagram element plus one number. 4. Light is never drawn as a beam in space: a bead, a trace after the fact, or a cone only where the cone *is* the lesson (B25). 5. Real objects never get a graphics-style stroke and diagrams never get a photographic texture. 6. No universal ease: light moves at constant speed, objects that have mass decelerate, readouts pop with a short overshoot. 7. Earth is at the bottom and the Moon at the top in every schematic; light that returns travels down. 8. A subject that is not the point sits still (restraint). |
| **Hero moments** | **H1 B17 "Actually, yes."** the dashed ghost mirror becomes the real reflector at the same spot and scale. **H2 B19-B21** the array face opens (iris) into the corner-cube cross-section; rays one at a time; the flat mirror fails, the cube returns. **H3 B34-B36** the traced round-trip path peels off the Earth-Moon line, forms a bar, folds in half, and returns to the line as the distance. **H4 B04-B05** the pulse leaves the dome and the camera pulls out of Earth with it, the Moon landing on its schematic spot. **H5 B37-B39** the slow pull-back and the last faint return. |
| **Restraint moments** | B02 locked frame after the rejection; B14 "But hold on." everything stops for 0.4 s; B23 quiet hold after the detector lights (50.5-53.1 s); B24 dimmed hush; B28 three dots in the dark; every schematic from B05 to B13 is a locked camera so only the pulse and the clock move; the 1.47 s tail. |
| **Approval evidence** | What a reviewer must see, watching the MOVING film at phone size with sound: (a) the dome-to-schematic handoff at 8.0-8.9 s reads as one move and the Moon does not jump; (b) the stopwatch and the bead agree at B07 (2.56 s, bead home); (c) ghost to reflector at 37.6-38.1 s has no pop; (d) at B20 only one ray is active, the inset arrives on "tiny fraction", nothing else competes; (e) the bar fold is readable as "the same path, folded"; (f) captions never touch a key subject; (g) final frame resolves the opening (Moon above Earth, ruler set) and fades to black; (h) the astronaut's walk and the reflector settle are acceptable *as a placeholder* or are flagged for a rig; (i) separate EDITORIAL and ANIMATION passes are written down. None of this has been performed by a human yet. |
