# V7 skydiver model sheet

![Four body projections and close-up, at rest and in an alternate pose](rigs/turnaround.jpg)

The face is an adult skydiver concentrating on balance. Keep the mouth small and let the eyes lead a reaction. The raised chin, soft bent elbows and open hands describe air pressure even when the character holds still.

## Drawing decisions

- Give the helmet a slightly lopsided crown, a broad goggle band and one broken highlight. Profile shows the nose beyond the lens. Three-quarter compresses the far lens and pushes the bridge towards the nose. The rear dome has a continuous goggle strap.
- Trace the action from the heels through the chest to the raised chin. The waist is narrower than the chest. Let the two knees bend to different heights. A cuff, thumb and bent toe finish each extremity.
- Draw the outer shape in charcoal `#272822`. Outer contours are generally 2.6–3 design units, structural seams 1.55–2.2 and fine folds around 1.35. The contours are smooth, mildly irregular Béziers with rounded joins. Leave broad paper shapes quiet.
- Upper fabric is warm paper `#f2efe5`, near trousers are grey-green `#d6d7cb`, and far trousers recede into `#acaea3`. Use `#535852` for straps, cuffs and boots. Skin is `#f4e5cc`. The closed parachute pack carries the one muted equipment accent, `#bc9a77`, with a paler stitched flap.
- Keep harness webbing attached to the suit. The pack rides high and close to the back. The rear view shows its trapezoid flap, closure and pocket seam. A slim side profile explains the same object without making it read as a deployed canopy.

## Staging by camera

| View | What the viewer should read |
|---|---|
| Side | Nose faces right. Long prone silhouette, knees below hips, heels raised and hands out in front. The far hand rests higher. |
| Three-quarter | Near elbow projects toward the bottom of the picture and far wrist rises behind the face. The short torso, angled flap and unequal goggle lenses turn the character toward camera. |
| Front | Face and chest overlap the central leg roots. The feet peek above and inside the open arm silhouette. The shoulder and heel lines tilt in opposite directions. |
| Back | Camera sits above the tail of the body. The pack covers the chest, the helmet peeks over the top and the folded knees widen toward the foreground. |
| Close-up | Scale the same helmet, goggles and face together. The upper chest can run past the crop. Use an eye shift, brow change or brief blink for attention. |

The turnaround's second column demonstrates a pose test. Shot timing belongs to the production scene. Front and back are discrete drawings for camera cuts. The three-quarter drawing bridges them if the edit needs an intermediate angle.
