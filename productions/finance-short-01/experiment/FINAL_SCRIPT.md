# The interest that waits

English voiceover, original draft for owner approval. U.S. consumer-credit context. The sofa purchase is an illustrative scenario, not a reported customer case. Read normally; no competitor voice imitation.

## Complete narration

No interest? Then why the interest bill?

Imagine buying a sofa on a store card: “No interest if paid in full within twelve months.”

That “if” is the switch.

With deferred interest, interest builds from purchase day. It just hasn't been charged yet.

Meet the terms and clear the promotional balance before the deadline, and that interest is waived.

Leave some unpaid, and the accumulated interest can land on your bill.

Not just interest on what's left: interest calculated across the balances you carried since buying the sofa.

Even making every minimum payment might not clear it in time.

A true zero-percent introductory APR is different. Leaving a balance doesn't trigger interest for that zero-percent period.

So check the promotion's deadline, not just your monthly payment date.

The sofa came home on day one.

The interest was waiting there too.
