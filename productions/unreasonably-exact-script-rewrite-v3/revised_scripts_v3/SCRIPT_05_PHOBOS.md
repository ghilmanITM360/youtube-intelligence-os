## Voiceover

You're standing on Phobos, a small moon racing around Mars. You jump. On Earth, you know which way the story ends: up briefly, then back down. But could a jump here send you away for good?

Phobos has much less mass than Earth, so its gravity is much weaker. A jump could last much longer. But weak gravity doesn't mean every jump escapes.

For a rough comparison, replace Phobos with a smooth, isolated sphere of the same mass and average radius. In that simplified model, the escape speed is about eleven metres per second, roughly forty kilometres an hour. That's a speed, not a promise about what a person in a spacesuit could do.

The real moon isn't a smooth, isolated ball. It's irregular, it rotates, and Mars pulls on you too. Your starting point and the direction of your jump matter. A detailed trajectory would need all of those conditions.

And leaving Phobos would not automatically mean leaving Mars. You would already be moving around the planet along with the moon. The next chapter of your journey belongs to Mars as well as your jump.
