## Voiceover

You send a message to Voyager 1. It leaves Earth at the speed of light. By tomorrow, it can finally reach the spacecraft.

Is something slowing the signal down? No. The problem is how much space it has to cross.

Radio waves and visible light are both electromagnetic waves. In the near vacuum between Earth and Voyager, they travel at essentially the same speed. A radio signal could reach the Moon in a little over a second. Voyager is much, much farther away.

In November 2026, NASA confirmed that Voyager 1 had reached one light-day from Earth. That's the distance light travels in an entire day: nearly twenty-six billion kilometres.

So a command sent around that milestone needed about a day to arrive. If Voyager replied immediately, the return signal would need roughly another day. Any time spent preparing the reply adds to the wait.

Earth and Voyager keep moving, so the exact delay changes. But the message isn't crawling. It has an almost unimaginably long journey at the fastest speed a signal can travel.
