## Voiceover

You're falling from 10 kilometers above the ground. Your parachute is still closed, gravity is still pulling you down, and somehow you're slowing down. Wait, what? Did gravity change its mind? Did the air suddenly grow brakes? No, but the air is doing something surprisingly powerful.

Imagine the fall, starting from rest. Up here, the air is thin. At first, gravity wins.

You keep going faster. But as you fall, you're entering denser air. And here's where things get strange.

The air pushes against you, and the faster you fall, the harder it pushes back. Eventually, in a simple model where your body position stays fixed, that upward push can actually become stronger than your weight. Think about that.

You're still moving down, but the net force is now pointing up, so your downward speed starts to decrease. The speedometer drops, the ground gets closer, and your parachute is still closed. That's the strange part.

Slowing down doesn't mean you've stopped falling. It means the air is pushing back harder than gravity is pulling you down. But remember, this is a model, not a prediction for a real person.

Change the posture or the conditions, and the result can change too.
