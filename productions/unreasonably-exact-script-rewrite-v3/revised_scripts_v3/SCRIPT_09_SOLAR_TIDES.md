## Voiceover

Take the Moon away, and the tide comes in anyway. How can that happen if the Moon is famous for making tides?

Because the Sun makes them too. Its gravity pulls on the whole Earth, but it pulls a little more strongly on the side closer to it than on the side farther away. That difference across the planet helps move ocean water and produces a solar tide.

The Sun pulls on Earth much more strongly overall than the Moon does. But the Moon is far closer, so its pull changes more across Earth's width. That difference is why the Moon's tide-generating effect is stronger.

Today, solar and lunar effects sometimes reinforce one another and sometimes partly oppose one another. Without the Moon, the solar influence would remain, but that familiar lunar-solar cycle would disappear.

This doesn't give every beach the same new tide. Ocean depth, coastlines and Earth's rotation shape how water responds. So the answer is yes: tides would remain. Predicting your particular shoreline is another calculation.
