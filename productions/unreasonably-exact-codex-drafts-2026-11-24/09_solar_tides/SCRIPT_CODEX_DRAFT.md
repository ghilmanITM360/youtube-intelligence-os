## Voiceover

Take the Moon out of the picture. Does that make the ocean tideless?

No. The Sun makes tides too.

The key is not simply how hard the Sun pulls Earth. It's how that pull differs across Earth. The side facing the Sun is a little closer, so the Sun pulls on it a little more strongly than it pulls on Earth's centre. On the far side, the pull is a little weaker. Those differences create a tide-generating effect.

We can see the Sun's contribution today. When the Sun and Moon's effects reinforce one another, the tidal range tends to grow; when they partly oppose one another, it tends to shrink.

Without the Moon, solar forcing would remain. But that doesn't tell us a universal percentage of today's tide height. Each ocean basin responds according to its own shape and depth. The Sun supplies part of the push; the ocean decides how that push shows up at a particular coast.
