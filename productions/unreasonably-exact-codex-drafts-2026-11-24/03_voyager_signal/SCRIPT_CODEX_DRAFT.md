## Voiceover

Send a message to Voyager 1, and the signal sets off at the speed of light. It still needs about a day to get there.

That's not because the radio signal is moving slowly. Light can cross the distance from Earth to the Moon in a little over a second. But Voyager has been travelling outward since 1977, and the distance has become enormous.

On November eighteenth, 2026, Voyager reached one light-day from Earth, NASA reported: about twenty-six billion kilometres away. A light-day is simply how far light travels in a day. Voyager's radio signal travels at that same speed through space.

So a command sent near that milestone needed about a day for the outward trip. A reply needed roughly another day to return. That is travel time alone; the spacecraft would also need time to respond.

The exact delay changes as Earth and Voyager move. But the reason for the wait is straightforward: even light has a long way to go.
