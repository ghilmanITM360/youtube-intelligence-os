## Voiceover

Gravity is pulling you down. There's no parachute slowing you. So why is your speed dropping?

Imagine dropping from ten kilometres up, starting from rest. High above the ground, the air is thin. As you fall, gravity wins and your speed climbs.

But you are also entering denser air. Air pushes upward against your motion, and the faster you move, the harder it pushes. Eventually, in a simple model with a fixed body position, that upward push becomes stronger than your weight.

You are still moving down. But now the net force points up, so your downward speed starts to fall. The speedometer goes down while the ground keeps coming closer.

This is a model, not a prediction for a real person: posture and conditions change the result.
