## Voiceover

Phobos is one of Mars's moons, and it is small enough to make a strange question worth asking. Could you jump off it and never come back?

First, replace the real moon with a smooth, stationary ball, alone in space, using roughly Phobos's mass and average radius. In that simplified model, escape from the surface takes about eleven metres per second. That's roughly forty kilometres an hour.

Suppose you could push off at three metres per second. That is an assumption about the jump, not a measurement on Phobos. In our isolated-ball model it is well below escape speed, so you would come back.

The real moon is lumpy and rotating, and Mars is close enough to matter. Location and launch direction change the calculation. The ball model cannot settle every real trajectory; that needs a model of the actual environment.

And leaving Phobos would not automatically mean leaving Mars. You would begin the trip already travelling around the planet with the moon.
