## Methodology detail

1. Pulled all 73 rows from `channels`, joined video counts from `videos` and real-transcript counts from `transcripts`, and dataset linkage from `dataset_channels`/`datasets`.
2. For the 44 channels with `video_count > 0`, ran a keyword sweep (case-insensitive substring match) across every video title, using a keyword list covering the fixed niche taxonomy (senior/elderly/aging/dementia/arthritis/osteoporosis/blood pressure/cardiovascular/diabetes/longevity/mobility/doctor/geriatric/nutrition/chronic/brain/cognitive/stroke/fitness/wellness/nursing home, etc., **including a deliberately broad "hip" token to catch hip-pain/hip-replacement content**).
3. Manually reviewed every keyword match (including reading the actual title) to separate true positives from false positives -- the "hip" token in particular caught many false hits from "cruise ship", "relationship", "hippo" that had to be individually discarded.
4. For the 6 channels with genuine senior-health signal, pulled all video titles (10-122 each, largest first) and a transcript sample, and read them directly to determine sub-niche emphasis and check for a "doctor" credential claim vs. generic AI-narrated framing.
5. For the 29 identity-only channels (no videos ingested), classified on channel name + current handle + canonical URL alone, per task instructions: unambiguous topic-domain names (e.g. "...Invests", "...Finance", "Parenting Hacks", an explicit "Animation" handle) were called `NOT RELEVANT`; anything generic, punny, or health-adjacent-but-unconfirmed was called `IDENTITY UNRESOLVED`.

## Duplication finding (Core Rule 9 -- detect duplication before treating datasets as independent)

A title-level duplication check across the 6 senior-health channels found **3 verbatim-identical video titles shared between `Dr. Ares Senior` and `Dr. Elias Senior Health`** (e.g. "How Often Should Seniors Take a Bath After 60? 7 Shocking Facts You Need to Know!"), plus a much larger set of near-identical templated scripts recurring across *all six* channels (e.g. "Taking Amlodipine, Losartan, or Lisinopril for Blood Pressure? ... seniors...", "Why You Always Have Mucus in Your Throat...", "What Time Should The Elderly Go to Bed After 70..."). This is strong evidence that at least some of these 6 channels are drawing from a shared script/content-mill source rather than being fully independent evidence of "what senior-health creators do" -- any strategy analysis that treats all 6 as independent data points should account for this. This finding was **not** in the task instructions to look for; it surfaced from the evidence itself and is flagged per repository Core Rule 9.

## Doctor-credential caveat (per task instruction 4)

All 6 senior-health channels use physician-style branding ("Dr.", "Cardiologist Warns", "Doctor Reveals", "Neurologist Explains", "Surgeon Reveals"). **No verification of any actual medical license was performed or is possible from this evidence.** A sampled transcript (Dr. Ares Senior, "Cardiologist Warns: These 4 Morning Habits...") reads as generic third-person scripted narration ("cardiologists have tracked...") rather than a named individual asserting personal credentials on camera, which is consistent with -- but does not prove -- a content-mill / ghostwritten format rather than a verified practicing clinician. Treat "doctor-led" as an **unverified branding claim** for all 6, not a fact.
