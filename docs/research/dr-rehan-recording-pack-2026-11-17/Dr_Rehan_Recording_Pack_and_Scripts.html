A. Complete reference script (all spoken words)

[Interviewer] Why did my doctor want to compare my ankle and arm blood pressure?

0:00 Why compare ankle and arm blood pressure? Because the ankle pressure can show whether blood is flowing freely to the legs. The test is called the ankle-brachial index, or ABI. We measure the top blood pressure number in the arm and at the ankle, and divide the ankle number by the arm number. In a healthy leg, the ankle pressure is about the same as the arm, or a little higher. If the arteries in the leg are narrowed, the ankle pressure is lower. The ABI helps us diagnose peripheral artery disease, or PAD, when leg arteries are narrowed by plaque. (101 words, Answer first)

[Interviewer] When would a doctor order an ABI?

0:48 A doctor orders an ABI when the symptoms or the examination suggest PAD. The classic symptom is calf, thigh or buttock pain when you walk, that goes away when you rest. Other clues are weak or absent foot pulses, cool or pale feet, hair loss on the legs, and sores on the feet or toes that heal slowly. An ABI is recommended in this situation to establish the diagnosis. It is a different question from screening someone who has no symptoms, which I will come to. (86 words, Why a doctor orders it)

[Interviewer] How is the test done?

1:29 The ABI is usually done lying down, resting, with blood pressure cuffs on both arms and the ankles. The nurse or technician uses a small handheld ultrasound device over the foot pulse to detect the pressure. It is generally quick, and the cuff may feel tight for a moment. You can usually go home right after. There is no radiation and no needle. Your own clinic will tell you if there is any preparation, such as avoiding smoking or caffeine beforehand. (81 words, How it is done)

[Interviewer] What do the numbers mean?

2:08 The current guideline groups them like this. A value of zero point nine or lower is abnormal and suggests PAD. Zero point nine one to zero point nine nine is borderline. One point zero to one point four is normal. And above one point four is called noncompressible. That means the artery walls are too stiff for the cuff to squeeze them properly, so the number is falsely high. (69 words, Reading the numbers)

[Interviewer] What if the number is high, or the pain does not match the result?

2:42 A high number is not necessarily reassuring. Stiff, calcified arteries are more common in people with diabetes and chronic kidney disease, and then the ABI can look normal or high while the arteries are actually narrowed. In that case doctors may measure the pressure in the toe, which is called a toe-brachial index. And if you have leg pain with walking but a normal ABI at rest, the test can be repeated after exercise, or other tests, such as an ultrasound scan of the arteries, may be done. So the result is read together with your symptoms. (97 words, Stiff arteries and other tests)

[Interviewer] Should everyone over 65 have an ABI?

3:28 No, not everyone over sixty-five needs an ABI automatically. When there are symptoms or signs, the ABI is a standard test. For people without symptoms, the advice differs. The American guidelines say screening with an ABI can be reasonable in people at increased risk, and your doctor will know if you fall in those groups. The US Preventive Services Task Force looks at all adults without symptoms and says the evidence is not yet enough to say whether screening helps or harms. These statements describe different groups, so they are not in conflict. (93 words, Screening vs testing)

[Interviewer] Does the ABI check the heart arteries? Can I do it at home?

4:12 The ABI is not a direct test of the arteries of the heart. But PAD is a sign that the arteries elsewhere in the body may also have plaque, so your doctor will look at your heart risk too. And please do not try to do this at home with an ordinary home blood pressure monitor. The measurement needs the right technique and a Doppler probe, and a wrong number could falsely reassure you or frighten you. If your legs hurt when you walk, see your doctor. (87 words, What ABI is not)

[Interviewer] What happens if the test shows PAD, and what is an emergency?

4:54 Treatment is more than one test. It includes a supervised walking program, which the guideline strongly supports, stopping smoking, controlling blood pressure, cholesterol and diabetes, and medicines such as a statin and an antiplatelet when appropriate. Some people need a procedure. And there are urgent signs. A leg that suddenly becomes painful, pale, cold, numb or weak is an emergency. Foot sores that do not heal, or pain in the foot at rest, especially at night, need urgent review. (79 words, Treatment and urgent signs)

[Interviewer] What should people remember?

5:32 To recap. The ABI compares ankle and arm pressure to help diagnose PAD. It is ordered when symptoms or examination suggest it. A value of zero point nine or below is abnormal, and a value above one point four may be falsely high. Further tests may be needed. It does not measure the heart arteries, and it is not for home. If your legs hurt when you walk, talk to your doctor. I'm Dr. Rehan Omer. Take care. (78 words, Recap)
