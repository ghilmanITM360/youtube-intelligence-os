### 1. The deferred-interest deadline — selected

Question: How can a no-interest offer charge you for the past? A shopper brings home a sofa; a second, initially uncharged interest ledger grows behind the offer. Paying the promotional balance under the terms clears that ledger; leaving a balance when the promotion expires can trigger the accrued charge. A closing comparison prevents confusion with true zero-percent introductory APR.

Primary evidence: [CFPB explanation, reviewed January 22, 2024](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/i-got-a-credit-card-promising-no-interest-for-a-purchase-if-i-pay-in-full-within-12-months-how-does-this-work-en-40/), [CFPB comparison, June 8, 2017](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/blog/how-understand-special-promotional-financing-offers-credit-cards/), and [Synchrony's current product explanation](https://www.synchrony.com/consumer-resources/deferred-interest), accessed October 13, 2026. The lender page has no clear publication date; access date is not publication date.

Current context: the Federal Reserve's [October 7, 2026 G.19 release](https://www.federalreserve.gov/Releases/g19/Current/) reports August consumer credit rising at a 1.9% seasonally adjusted annual rate, while revolving credit fell at a 4.2% annual rate. These aggregate measures neither isolate deferred-interest products nor establish rising household distress. They are research context only and are deliberately excluded from narration. Evergreen usefulness is an editorial hypothesis; no search-volume evidence was acquired.

Originality: the closest saved title is tXST56E5oJ4, “The Real Credit Card Crisis Isn't the Debt.” Its saved description discusses what Americans buy on credit, not deferred-interest mechanics. No explicit deferred-interest title/description match was found across 206 unique records. This is bounded evidence, not proof the topic was never mentioned. Avoid its crisis framing and aggregate-debt hook.

### 2. Why slower inflation does not rewind your grocery bill

Question: Why can inflation fall while prices stay high? Show a shopping basket climbing a staircase more slowly; the staircase does not reverse. Explain rate of change versus price level. Do not imply all individual prices always rise.

Primary mechanism: [St. Louis Fed, March 1, 2024](https://www.stlouisfed.org/publications/page-one-economics/2024/03/01/the-inflation-rate-is-falling-but-prices-are-not). Timely peg: [BLS schedule](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/2026/) lists September CPI for October 14, 2026, 08:30 U.S. Eastern, or 17:30 Pakistan time. At this research cutoff the release is still upcoming; no September result is claimed. The accessible [August release](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.htm) reports headline CPI rising 0.4% month over month, seasonally adjusted, and 3.4% over 12 months, unadjusted. It does not establish a new fall in headline inflation. The idea must remain conditional/educational.

News scan: AP's October 9 preview, [“2 Inflation reports, retail sales and big bank profits all arrive in the coming week”](https://apnews.com/article/75c52cd60818e6c8957edfe8f8f535a9), surfaced in live search; full-page retrieval failed. It was used only as a discovery signal, not financial proof. BLS supplies the verified timing.

Tradeoff: broad relevance and an imminent release, but substantial channel overlap: turJ6VMfUjI “Why You (Mathematically) Need Inflation,” PLIyDdVjh4A “How We Measure Inflation Is Broken,” and jXo9t3t4Wus “The Harder We Fight Inflation... ### 3. The smaller car payment that makes the car cost more

Question: How can a lower payment cost more overall? Two identical cars pull loan calendars of different lengths. Holding principal and interest rate constant, a longer term lowers the monthly payment while increasing total interest. A short schematic replaces a complex amortization lecture.

Primary evidence: [CFPB comparison guide, reviewed January 30, 2024](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-do-i-compare-auto-loan-offers-what-should-i-look-at-besides-the-monthly-payment-en-753/) and the October 7 G.19 release for current credit context. A production would require a clearly labeled hypothetical calculation with identical principal/APR and no fees, rather than comparing unmatched advertised offers. No explicit auto-loan-term title was located in the saved catalog. Evergreen relevance is plausible, but measured demand is unavailable. Strong alternative; the central contradiction is more familiar and the calendar comparison less distinctive than deferred interest.
