Most people think clogged arteries
happen suddenly, but in reality,
arterial problems usually develop slowly
over many years. Cholesterol and
inflammation build up inside the blood
vessels, making it harder for blood to
flow freely. The good news, some
everyday foods may help support healthy
arteries and circulation. Today, I'll
show you five foods that cardiologists
often recommend for supporting arterial
health. Hello, I'm Dr. Rehan Omar,
consulting cardiologist. Many patients
ask me a simple question, "Doctor, is
there anything I can eat that helps
protect my heart and arteries?" The
answer is yes. Well, no food can
magically remove plaque overnight.
Certain foods contain nutrients that
support healthy cholesterol levels,
reduce inflammation, and improve blood
vessel functions. Let's go through five
of the most important and helpful ones.
The first food is fatty fish such as
salmon, sardines, and mackerel. These
fish are rich in omega-3 fatty acids,
which are known to support heart health.
Omega-3 fats may help reduce
triglycerides, support healthy blood
pressure, and improve the flexibility of
blood vessels. That's why many heart
specialists recommend eating fatty fish
at least twice per week. Food number
two, berries, including blueberries,
strawberries, raspberries, blackberries,
etc., etc. Berries contain powerful
plant compounds called antioxidants,
especially flavonoids. These compounds
help reduce inflammation and oxidative
stress in the body. Why does that
matter? Because inflammation plays a
major role in the development of
atherosclerosis, a process that causes
arteries to narrow over process of time.
Adding a handful of berries to breakfast
or yogurt is a simple way to include
these protective nutrients in your diet.
Food number three, leafy green
vegetables, such as spinach, kale, and
arugula. These vegetables are rich in
dietary nitrates. Inside the body,
nitrates can be converted into molecules
that help relax blood vessels and
improve circulation. Leafy greens are
also high in fiber and essential
vitamins, making them one of the most
heart supportive foods you can ever eat.
Next on the list are nuts, especially
almonds and walnuts. Nuts contain a
combination of healthy fats, fibers, and
plant sterols. These nutrients may help
support healthy cholesterol levels.
Cholesterol balance is important because
excess LDL cholesterol balance
contributes to plaque buildup inside the
blood vessels. Just a small handful of
nuts each day can be beneficial.
However, moderation is important because
nuts are calorie dense, which can result
in excessive weight loss. Fifth food is
extra virgin olive oil. Olive oil is a
key part of Mediterranean diet, which
has been widely associated with
cardiovascular health. It contains
monounsaturated fats and polyphenols,
which may help support healthy
cholesterol levels and reduce
inflammation. Using olive oil instead of
highly processed cooking oils can be a
simple step toward a heart healthier
diet. Now, it is important to understand
something. No single food can completely
remove plaque that has already built up
in the arteries. Heart health depends on
a combination of habits, including
balanced nutrition, regular physical
activity, healthy weight, and good blood
pressure control, avoiding smoking. But,
putting these foods regularly can
support a healthier cardiovascular
environment. Here's a simple example of
how these foods might fit into a day.
You can have yogurt with berries and few
nuts at breakfast. For lunch, a salad
with leafy greens and olive oil
dressing. While at dinner, grill salmon
with vegetables. These small dietary
choices repeated daily can make a
meaningful difference over time. So, the
key takeaway of my message is that your
arteries play crucial role in delivering
oxygen and nutrients throughout your
body. Supporting them with the right
foods can help maintain healthy
circulation and heart function. In the
