Most seniors are trying to control their blood 
pressure but ignoring one of the simplest things
that could help. It's not a medication. It's not 
expensive. It's a fruit you probably already have
at home and cardiologist often recommend it for a 
very specific reason. Hello, I'm Dr. Rahan Omar,
a consultant interventional cardiologist. I treat 
patient with high blood pressure, coronary artery
disease, heart failure and arrhythmias. Blood 
pressure is one of the most important factors
in heart health after 60. Small daily habits, 
especially diet, can make a meaningful difference
over time. That magic fruit is banana. Bananas are 
rich in potassium, a mineral that plays a key role
in blood pressure regulation. Most people focus 
on only on reducing salt, but the real balance is
between sodium and potassium. When sodium levels 
are high, blood pressure can rise. Therefore,
potassium helps counter the effect by supporting 
how blood vessels relax and function. It also
helps regulate fluid balance, which affects 
pressure inside the blood vessel. Wait, this is
important. It's not just about eating banana. It's 
about what they replace in your diet. If bananas
replace processed snacks or high sodium food, 
the effect becomes much more meaning because now
you're reducing sodium, increasing potassium. But 
bananas are not the only fruit that supports heart
health. There are three others which cardiologists 
often recommend and one of them may surprise you.
Fruits that support cardiovascular health include 
berries which are rich in antioxidants, oranges,
which provide vitamin C and potassium, 
avocados contains potassium, healthy fats,
and good amount of proteins. Each works 
differently, however, but all supports the
vascular health. One of my patients who was 70 
years old asked me if diet changes could help
support his blood pressure. He stated he started 
adding potassium rich foods, walking daily,
reducing processed foods. Over time, these changes 
supported his overall cardiovascular health. But
here's where most people go wrong. They expect one 
food to fix everything. No single food replaces
medical care, medication, and lifestyle change. 
But small daily choices repeated consistently
can support long-term heart health. Next,
