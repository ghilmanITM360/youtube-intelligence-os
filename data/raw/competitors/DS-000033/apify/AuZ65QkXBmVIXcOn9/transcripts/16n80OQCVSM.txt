Now, what does it mean for a medication to be anti-cholinergic?
Well, an anticholinergic is a medication that has the chemical property
of blocking or otherwise diminishing the effects of a very important
neurotransmitter called acetylcholine.
A neurotransmitter is a compound that helps neurons,
so that means nerve cells,
communicate whether they are in your brain or elsewhere in the
body. And it turns out that acetylcholine is used quite a
lot by brain cells.
Your brain cells need to use acetylcholine to communicate with each

Acetylcholine is also widely used outside the brain.
In fact,
it's used throughout the body often in ways related to what
we call the parasympathetic nervous system.
So basically the body has something called the autonomic nervous system,
which is not under your voluntary control,
but regulates a lot of your bodily functions.
And one part of it is the sympathetic nervous system,
which generates what people often call the "fight or flight" response.
That system mostly does not use acetylcholine.
However, the counter to that system,
the parasympathetic nervous system,
which generates a lot of your "rest and digest" functions in
the body,
that one does heavily rely on acetylcholine.

the bowels,
the bladder,
