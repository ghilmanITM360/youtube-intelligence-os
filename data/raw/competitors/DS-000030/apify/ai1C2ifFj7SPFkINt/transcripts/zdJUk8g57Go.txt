seniors must be extra careful with fish.
Fish is a great source of high quality
protein, omega-3 fatty acids, and
essential vitamins like D and B12.
However, there are modern risks to
consider. Pollution leads to mercury
contamination, and farmed fish may
contain antibiotics and pesticides.
These risks are especially significant
for seniors as their bodies are less
efficient at filtering out toxins. The
buildup of mercury can harm the nervous
system and antibiotics can disrupt gut
health. Therefore, choosing the right
fish is about avoiding harmful
substances, not just getting nutrients.

Number two, farmed salmon, often labeled
Atlantic salmon.
The difference between wild and farmed
salmon is significant. Farmed salmon are
raised on processed feed pellets,
resulting in a nutritional profile that
is less favorable for seniors. It
contains higher levels of inflammatory
omega-6 fatty acids and lower levels of
beneficial omega-3s compared to wild
salmon. It can also contain contaminants
like PCBs.
Your non-negotiable rule, always look
for the words wild caught on the label.
It is a critical choice for your health.
Number three, tilapia. Tilapia is
popular due to its mild flavor and low
cost, but it offers little nutritional
value. It is often farmed in intensive
systems and has a problematic fat
composition. It provides almost no
anti-inflammatory omega-3s while being
loaded with pro-inflammatory omega-6
fatty acids. For seniors managing
conditions like arthritis or heart

any other fish on our best list such as

farm-raised catfish, particularly from
Asia. The primary concern with some

overcrowded ponds that are treated with
antibiotics and antifungal agents not
approved in the United States or Europe.
For a senior with a potentially
vulnerable immune system, consuming this

these chemical residues, placing an

safer approach. If you enjoy catfish,
seek out US farmed varieties which are
subject to stricter regulations or
choose a different mild white fish like
cod or hatock. Number five, cheap canned

Number one, wild caught salmon, sckeye,

Place a salmon fillet on a baking sheet.

Number two, high quality sardines in
olive oil or water. Don't underestimate
these small fish. Sardines are low on
the food chain, so they accumulate very
little mercury. They are packed with
calcium and phosphorus from their edible
bones, making them excellent for bone
health. They are also an excellent
source of vitamin B12, which is
essential for nerve health and energy.
Easy snack idea.
Mash a can of drained sardines with a
tablespoon of Greek yogurt, lemon juice,
and black pepper. Use it as a spread on
whole grain crackers. Number three,
trout. Rainbow trout. Trout is a
fantastic and often more affordable
alternative to wild salmon. US farmed

clean, flowing water, resulting in a
clean tasting fish with a mild flavor.
It offers a healthy dose of omega-3s and
high quality protein and is easy to
digest.
Simple preparation.
Lightly coat trout fillets in a mixture
of whole wheat flour and paprika. Pan
sear in a non-stick skillet with a small
amount of olive oil until the skin is
crispy and the flesh is cooked through.
Number four, herring. This traditional
fish is a nutritional powerhouse for
seniors. Herring is exceptionally high
in omega-3 fats and is one of the best

nutrient for bone health and immune
