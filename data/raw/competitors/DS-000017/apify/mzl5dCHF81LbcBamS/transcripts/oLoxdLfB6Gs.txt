You may have heard of Rayleigh scattering 
as the answer to “why is the sky blue.”
This is sort of true:
bluer light scatters more 
off of air than redder light,
so the sky is redder close to the 
sun and bluer farther away from it.
But honestly… a better answer to this 
question might be “because air is blue.”
Sure, air appears blue for 
a bunch of physics reasons,
but everything appears the color it 
is for a bunch of physics reasons.
When you ask, “Why is the 
Statue of liberty green?”
The answer is something like,
“The outside of the statue is copper, 
so it used to be copper-colored.
Over time, a layer of copper 
carbonate compounds formed
(through oxidation and corrosion), and 
copper carbonate compounds are green.”
You don't say
“The statue is green because of frequency-specific 
absorption and scattering by surface molecules.”
So we should probably just say
“the sky is blue because the sky 
is made of air, and air is blue.”
