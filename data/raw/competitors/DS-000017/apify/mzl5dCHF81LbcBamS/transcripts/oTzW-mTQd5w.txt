This question comes from Rebecca, who
asks, "If you suddenly began rising
steadily at 1 ft per second, how exactly
would you die? Would you freeze or
suffocate first, or something else?" My
question for Rebecca, did you bring a
coat?
Rising a foot per second isn't that
fast. It's substantially slower than a
typical elevator. It would take you 5 to
7 seconds to rise out of arms reach,
depending on how tall your friends are.
After 30 seconds, you'd be 30 ft off the
ground. This is getting close to your
last chance for a friend on the ground
to throw you a sandwich or a water
bottle or something. After a minute or
two, you would be above the trees. If
it's a breezy day, it would probably get
slightly chillier thanks to the steady
wind above the tree line. After 10
minutes, you would be above most office
towers, and after 25 minutes, you'd pass
the spire of the Empire State Building.
The air at these heights is about 5%
thinner than it is at the surface.
Fortunately, your body handles air
pressure changes like that all the time.
Your ears may pop, but you wouldn't
really notice anything else. A foot per
second is just over a kilometer per
hour. So, after an hour, you'd be about
a kilometer off the ground. At this
point, you'd definitely start to get
chilly. If you have a coat, you'll still
be okay, although you might notice the
wind starting to pick up. At about 2
hours and just over 2 km, the
temperature would drop below freezing.
The wind would also most likely be
picking up substantially. If you have
any exposed skin, frostbite starts to
become a concern. At this point, the air
pressure would drop below that of an
airliner cabin, and oxygen deprivation
effects would start to become more
significant. But, unless you had a warm
coat, the temperature drop over the next
2 hours would be a bigger problem. The
scholarly authorities on freezing to
death seem to be, unsurprisingly,
Canadians, who have developed the most
widely used model for human survival in
cold air. According to them, the main
determining factor in the cause of death
would be your clothes. If you were nude,
you'd probably succumb to hypothermia
somewhere around the 5-hour mark before
your oxygen ran out. If you were bundled
up, you might get frostbitten, but you
would probably survive long enough to
reach the death zone, where you would
die a different horrible death. Above
8,000 m, above the tops of all but the
highest mountains, the oxygen content in
the air is too low to support human
life. Near this zone, you you a range of
symptoms, possibly including confusion,
dizziness, clumsiness, impaired vision,
and nausea. As you continue to rise,
your blood oxygen content would fall and
your breathing rate would increase to
compensate until eventually it couldn't
keep up. The result would be a loss of
consciousness and death, which would
likely happen somewhere after the 8-hour
mark, which is around when you'd surpass
the elevation of Mount Everest. And 2
million years later, your frozen body,
still moving along steadily at a foot
per second, would pass through the
heliopause into interstellar space. A
foot per second is also pretty close to
a light-year every billion years. In 6
or 7 billion years, the sun will become
a red giant, but the much longer-lived
Proxima Centauri is only 4.3 light-years
away. So, with a lot of luck and some
careful aim, by the time your old planet
has been vaporized, you'll be safe and
sound on your new home.
So, there's that.
