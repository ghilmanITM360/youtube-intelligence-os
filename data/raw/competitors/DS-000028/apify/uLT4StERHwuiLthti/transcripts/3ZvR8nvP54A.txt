There are three things that happen to
your body the moment you lie flat in
bed. The first is circulatory. When you
stand or sit, gravity pulls your blood
toward your legs. Your calf muscles are
constantly squeezing the veins, pumping
blood back up to your heart. The
arteries in your legs are working at
full pressure to keep oxygen flowing to
your feet. The moment you lie down, that
pump shuts off. Blood flow to your feet
can drop by as much as 40% within the
first 20 minutes of lying still. Damaged
nerves need oxygen to function quietly.
starve them and they start screaming.
That is the burning sensation. That is
the tingling. That is the electric shock
feeling. It is not in your head. It is
your nerves running on too little fuel.
The second thing that happens is
mechanical. When you lie on your back,
the sciatic nerve, which runs from your
lower back down the back of each leg all
the way to your feet, gets compressed
against the mattress at three pressure
points. Your hip, your hamstring, and
your calf. Throughout the day, when you
move, walk, and shift position, that
nerve glides and slides freely through
its sheath. At night, it is pinched. A
pinched nerve is a loud nerve. The
signals it sends to your brain are
amplified. So, pain that was a 4 out of
10 at 6:00 in the evening becomes a 7
out of 10 at midnight. The third reason
is hormonal. During the day, your body
is flooded with cortisol, dopamine, and
adrenaline. These are the chemicals that
help your brain ignore background pain
signals so you can focus on living your
life. The moment you lie down to sleep,
those chemicals drop. Suddenly, your
brain has nothing else to pay attention
to. The nerve signals that were always
there get full volume. That is why pain
that was barely noticeable in the
afternoon becomes unbearable at night.
It is not getting worse. You are just
finally hearing it. The five exercises

nerves get oxygen. They mobilize the
sciatic and tibial nerves so they are
not pinched against the mattress. And

nervous system, the calming side of your
nervous system, which dampens pain
signals so your brain can quiet down
enough to sleep. You will do all five in

minutes before you plan to fall asleep.
Wear loose pajamas. Have a pillow

Let us begin. Exercise one is the ankle
pump. This is the calf muscle pump and

Lie flat on your back in bed. Legs

one rep. You will do 30 of these. What
is happening inside your leg while you
do this? Every time you flex your foot
up, your calf muscle, the gastrochemus
and the solius contracts. When it
contracts, it squeezes the deep veins
running through the calf. That squeeze
pushes a slug of blood up toward your
heart. When you point your foot down,
the calf relaxes and fresh blood rushes
back into your foot from above. Up.
Squeeze. Down. Refill 30 times. By the
time you finish, you will have moved
more blood through your feet than 10
minutes of lying still ever would. You
will feel a warm, almost tingly flush
move into your toes by repetition 20.
That is exactly what you want. That is
oxygenated blood reaching the small
nerves at the ends of your toes. For
some patients with severe neuropathy,

by half within a week of nightly use.
Now, the biggest mistake seniors make
with this exercise, they do it too fast.
20 seconds, 30 pumps, done. And they

seconds down. The calf needs that full
squeeze. If you rush, the calf only half
contracts, and the veins do not empty
properly. Slow is strong. Slow is the
answer. Exercise two is toe curls and
spreads. This one wakes up the tiny

reactivates the small sensory nerves

nerves that get hit first in diabetic

that respond first to gentle exercise.
Still lying on your back, leave your

every toe. Hold for 3 seconds. Scrunch.
Spread. Scrunch. Spread. 20 repetitions.

Exercise four is the bent knee air

happening here is critical. The sciatic

the buttock down the back of the thigh,
splits at the knee, and continues down
the lower leg as the tibial and common
paranal nerves. When the nerve is
shortened, it stops gliding. The signal
it sends becomes static. Static is pain.
The slow bicycle motion stretches the
nerve to its full length, then shortens
it, then stretches it again. With each
cycle, the nerve learns to glide freely
inside its sheath. The static drops, the
signal cleans up. pain volume falls. A
small note. If you feel a sharp shooting
